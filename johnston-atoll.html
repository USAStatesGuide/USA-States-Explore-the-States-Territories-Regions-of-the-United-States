About Johnston Atoll ▼ +

Johnston Atoll is a remote coral atoll in the central Pacific Ocean and an unincorporated, unorganized territory of the United States. It is located approximately 716 nautical miles southwest of Honolulu. The atoll consists of four islands: Johnston Island, Sand Island, North Island, and East Island.

The atoll began forming about 70 million years ago through a series of underwater volcanic eruptions. Johnston Atoll has a long history of conservation and military use and is now home to the Johnston Atoll National Wildlife Refuge, which protects important terrestrial and marine habitats.

Geography of Johnston Atoll ▼ +

Johnston Atoll is a low-lying coral atoll in the central Pacific Ocean. It consists of a shallow lagoon surrounded by coral reef and four islands: Johnston Island, Sand Island, North Island, and East Island. The atoll is one of the most isolated atolls in the world.

The natural landscape is dominated by coral, sand, low vegetation, beaches, shallow reef areas, and surrounding ocean waters. Human activities substantially modified the size and shape of several islands during the twentieth century, particularly Johnston Island and Sand Island.

Location and Surroundings ▼ +

Johnston Atoll is situated in the central Pacific Ocean, approximately 716 nautical miles southwest of Honolulu, Hawaii. Its remote location places it far from major continental landmasses and population centers.

The atoll is surrounded by extensive Pacific Ocean waters and coral reef ecosystems. Its marine environment forms part of the Pacific Islands Heritage Marine National Monument.

Highest Point ▼ +

Johnston Atoll is extremely low-lying and does not have a significant natural mountain or high point. Its islands are generally only a few meters above sea level.

The atoll's low elevation is characteristic of coral atolls, which develop on the remains of volcanic structures and are surrounded by shallow coral reefs and ocean waters.

Islands and Lagoon ▼ +

Johnston Atoll consists of four main islands: Johnston Island, Sand Island, North Island, and East Island.

Johnston Island is the largest of the islands. Sand Island, North Island, and East Island are smaller coral islands located around the atoll. The islands are surrounded by a shallow coral reef and lagoon environment that supports a diverse range of marine life.

History of Johnston Atoll ▼ +

Johnston Atoll was designated as a federal bird refuge in 1926. In 1934, the atoll was placed under the control and jurisdiction of the U.S. Navy. It later became an important military installation in the central Pacific.

During the twentieth century, the atoll was used for military activities, including aviation and other defense operations. Johnston Atoll was also associated with the United States nuclear testing program during the late 1950s and early 1960s.

Military infrastructure was gradually removed after the end of major operations. By 2005, most of the infrastructure on Johnston Island had been removed and personnel had left the atoll.

Administrative Status ▼ +

Johnston Atoll is an unincorporated, unorganized territory of the United States. It is one of the United States insular areas in the Pacific Ocean.

The emergent lands of Johnston Atoll remain under U.S. military administrative jurisdiction, while the Johnston Atoll National Wildlife Refuge is managed for wildlife conservation by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

Major Settlements ▼ +

Johnston Atoll has no permanent civilian cities or towns. The atoll's remote location and protected status mean that it does not have a conventional urban population.

Johnston Island historically contained military facilities, housing, an airfield, and other infrastructure associated with U.S. operations. Most of this infrastructure was removed after military activities ended.

Marine Environment ▼ +

The waters surrounding Johnston Atoll contain extensive coral reef habitats. The shallow reef covers approximately 32,000 acres and supports a diverse marine ecosystem.

More than 300 species of fish have been recorded in the reefs around Johnston Atoll. The marine environment also includes numerous species of coral and algae, as well as green sea turtles and other marine wildlife.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Johnston Atoll provides important habitat for seabirds and marine species. The low coral islands support vegetation adapted to the atoll's warm and relatively dry environment.

The refuge is home to numerous seabird species, including boobies, frigatebirds, terns, petrels, noddies, and shearwaters. Johnston Island supports one of the largest known colonies of red-tailed tropicbirds in the world.

The surrounding waters and coral reefs also provide habitat for fish, corals, sea turtles, and other marine organisms. Johnston Atoll is therefore an important conservation area in the central Pacific.

Conservation ▼ +

Johnston Atoll National Wildlife Refuge was established to protect native wildlife and habitats. In 2009, the surrounding waters became part of the Pacific Remote Islands Marine National Monument, now known as the Pacific Islands Heritage Marine National Monument.

Conservation work has included the successful eradication of invasive yellow crazy ants. After more than a decade of work, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service announced in 2021 that the invasive ants had been eradicated from Johnston Island.

Johnston Atoll is not open to the general public. Access to the national wildlife refuge is restricted and requires an appropriate special use permit.
