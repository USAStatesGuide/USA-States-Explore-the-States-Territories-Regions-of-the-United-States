Missouri, United States

About Missouri ▼ +

Missouri is a state in the Midwestern region of the United States. It is bordered by Iowa to the north, Illinois, Kentucky, and Tennessee to the east, Arkansas to the south, and Oklahoma, Kansas, and Nebraska to the west. The capital of Missouri is Jefferson City.

Missouri became the 24th state of the United States on August 10, 1821. The state is known for its rivers, forests, prairies, rolling hills, Ozark Mountains, agricultural lands, and central location within the country.

Geography of Missouri ▼ +

Missouri lies at an important geographic meeting point between the Great Plains, the Midwest, and the southern uplands. Northern Missouri is characterized by broad, gently rolling plains shaped by glacial deposits, while western and central areas include the Osage Plains and rolling prairie landscapes. Southern Missouri is dominated by the Ozark Plateau, where wooded hills, limestone and dolomite formations, springs, caves, and deeply cut valleys create a much more rugged landscape.

The Missouri River crosses the state from west to east and has shaped major valleys and agricultural areas, while the Mississippi River forms most of Missouri's eastern border. Southeastern Missouri contains the Bootheel, a low and unusually flat extension of the Mississippi Alluvial Plain with fertile farmland and wetlands. The state also contains distinctive waterways and reservoirs, including Lake of the Ozarks, while the confluence of the Missouri and Mississippi rivers near St. Louis gives Missouri a particularly important position within the central U.S. river system.

Missouri Borders ▼ +

Missouri shares land borders with eight states: Iowa to the north, Illinois and Kentucky to the east, Tennessee to the southeast, Arkansas to the south, Oklahoma and Kansas to the west, and Nebraska to the northwest.

Missouri has two major river boundaries. The Mississippi River forms much of its eastern boundary, while the Missouri River crosses the state from the west toward the east.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Missouri is Taum Sauk Mountain, which reaches approximately 1,772 feet (540 meters) above sea level.

Taum Sauk Mountain is located in the St. Francois Mountains of southeastern Missouri, within the Ozark region. It is the highest natural point in the state.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Missouri has an extensive network of rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Missouri River, Mississippi River, Osage River, Gasconade River, Meramec River, Platte River, and Grand River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, transportation, agriculture, water resources, recreation, fishing, and communities throughout the state.

Economy of Missouri ▼ +

Missouri has a diversified economy with important roles for manufacturing, transportation, agriculture, healthcare, financial services, and aerospace. Kansas City is a major center for transportation, distribution, animal health, food processing, and business services, while St. Louis has strong concentrations in healthcare, biotechnology, financial services, manufacturing, and logistics. The state's central location and access to the Missouri and Mississippi rivers have also made transportation and warehousing important economic activities.

Agriculture is especially important in northern and western Missouri and the southeastern Bootheel, producing soybeans, corn, cattle, hogs, poultry, rice, and other crops. Manufacturing includes automobiles and vehicle components, aircraft and aerospace equipment, machinery, chemicals, and food products. Missouri also has a significant beer and spirits industry, while tourism and outdoor recreation benefit from the Ozarks, Lake of the Ozarks, rivers, forests, and historic cities.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Missouri is divided into 114 counties. The City of St. Louis is an independent city and is not part of any county.

Counties are an important level of local government within the state. Missouri also contains cities, towns, villages, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Missouri include Kansas City, St. Louis, Springfield, Columbia, Independence, and St. Joseph.

Jefferson City is the state capital. Kansas City is the largest city by population and an important center for commerce, transportation, culture, and industry. St. Louis is a major metropolitan center on the Mississippi River, while Springfield and Columbia are important regional and educational centers.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Missouri's natural environment is especially distinctive in the Ozarks, where limestone and dolomite bedrock have created extensive cave systems, springs, sinkholes, and clear streams. Forests of oak, hickory, and other hardwoods cover much of the Ozark region, while northern and western Missouri contain remnants of tallgrass prairie mixed with woodlands and agricultural landscapes. The Current, Jacks Fork, and Meramec rivers are known for their clear water, wooded valleys, and spring-fed ecosystems.

The Missouri and Mississippi rivers create broad floodplains, wetlands, and forested river habitats that are important for fish, waterfowl, and migratory birds. Southeastern Missouri contains additional wetlands and lowland forests around the Bootheel. Across the state, habitats support white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, black bears, river otters, beavers, and many species of fish, amphibians, and birds. Protected areas such as the Ozark National Scenic Riverways preserve some of Missouri's most distinctive river, spring, cave, and forest environments.
