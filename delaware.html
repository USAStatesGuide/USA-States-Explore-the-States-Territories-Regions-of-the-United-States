About Delaware ▼ +

Delaware is a small state in the Mid-Atlantic region of the United States. It is bordered by Pennsylvania to the north, New Jersey across the Delaware River and Bay to the northeast, Maryland to the west and south, and the Atlantic Ocean to the east. The capital of Delaware is Dover.

Delaware became the first state of the United States on December 7, 1787. The state is known for its coastal areas, beaches, wetlands, forests, farmland, and important historical role in the founding of the United States. Its small size makes it one of the most compact states in the country.

Geography of Delaware ▼ +

Delaware is a small, predominantly low-lying state on the Delmarva Peninsula. Northern Delaware is the most elevated part of the state, where the Piedmont rises into rolling hills around Wilmington. South of this area, the land becomes flatter and transitions into the Atlantic Coastal Plain, with broad agricultural fields, forests, and wetlands.

The state's eastern side is closely connected to Delaware Bay, a large tidal estuary formed where the Delaware River reaches the Atlantic Ocean. Its geography includes tidal marshes, creeks, beaches, and coastal dunes, while the southern coast faces the Atlantic near places such as Rehoboth Beach and Bethany Beach. The Delaware River forms much of the state's northern and eastern boundary, making waterways a defining feature of Delaware's landscape.

Delaware Borders ▼ +

Delaware shares land borders with Pennsylvania to the north and Maryland to the west and south. Its northeastern boundary is separated from New Jersey by the Delaware River and Bay.

The eastern part of Delaware borders the Delaware Bay and the Atlantic Ocean. The state's coastal location has played an important role in its history, transportation, tourism, and economy.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Delaware is located near Ebright Azimuth, which reaches approximately 448 feet (137 meters) above sea level.

Ebright Azimuth is located in northern New Castle County, near the Pennsylvania state line. Delaware has the lowest maximum elevation of any U.S. state.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Delaware has several important rivers and waterways. Major waterways include the Delaware River, Delaware Bay, Christina River, Brandywine Creek, and Choptank River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, transportation, drinking water, agriculture, fishing, recreation, and trade. The Delaware River and Delaware Bay are especially significant because they connect the state to major waterways of the Mid-Atlantic region.

Economy of Delaware ▼ +

Delaware's economy is strongly associated with corporate services, finance, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, healthcare, manufacturing, and logistics. Wilmington is a major center for banking, credit-card services, and corporate activity. Delaware is also internationally known for its corporate law, under which a large number of U.S. companies choose to incorporate because of the state's specialized legal system and Court of Chancery.

Manufacturing has long been important in the Wilmington and New Castle County area, particularly in chemicals and pharmaceutical products. The Port of Wilmington connects the state to international trade and is known for handling imported and exported goods, including agricultural products and automobiles. In southern Delaware, poultry production is a major agricultural industry, alongside corn, soybeans, vegetables, and other crops, while coastal tourism contributes to the economy around Delaware's Atlantic beaches.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Delaware is divided into only 3 counties: New Castle County, Kent County, and Sussex County.

These counties extend from the more urbanized northern part of the state to the agricultural and coastal areas in the central and southern regions. Delaware also contains cities, towns, and other local communities.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Delaware include Wilmington, Dover, Newark, Middletown, Smyrna, and Milford.

Wilmington is the largest city by population and an important financial and business center. Dover is the state capital and an important governmental center. Newark is home to the University of Delaware, while coastal communities such as Rehoboth Beach and Lewes are important tourism destinations.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Delaware's natural environment is closely shaped by the low-lying Atlantic Coastal Plain and the waters of Delaware Bay. Broad salt marshes, tidal creeks, mudflats, and shallow estuaries dominate many coastal areas, while inland parts of the state contain pine and hardwood forests, freshwater wetlands, and agricultural landscapes. Coastal dunes and barrier beaches form another distinctive environment along the Atlantic shore.

Delaware Bay is an important stopover for migratory shorebirds, especially during spring migration when large numbers of birds feed on horseshoe crab eggs along the bay's shores. The bay and its tidal wetlands also provide nursery habitat for fish, shellfish, and other aquatic species. Inland forests and wetlands support white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, waterfowl, amphibians, and many other species, linking Delaware's coastal and freshwater ecosystems.
