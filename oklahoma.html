About Oklahoma ▼ +

Oklahoma is a state in the south-central region of the United States. It is bordered by Colorado and Kansas to the north, Missouri and Arkansas to the east, Texas to the south and west, and New Mexico at its northwestern corner. The capital and largest city of Oklahoma is Oklahoma City.

Oklahoma became the 46th state of the United States on November 16, 1907. The state is known for its Great Plains landscapes, prairies, forests, rolling hills, mesas, rivers, lakes, and diverse geographic regions extending from the High Plains to the wooded areas of eastern Oklahoma.

Geography of Oklahoma ▼ +

Oklahoma's geography changes from the dry High Plains of the west to the greener forests and mountains of the east. Western Oklahoma is dominated by open plains and grasslands, including the Oklahoma Panhandle and the western edge of the Great Plains. Central Oklahoma contains rolling prairie and the distinctive Cross Timbers, a belt of oak woodland that forms a transition between the open plains and the more heavily wooded eastern part of the state.

Southwestern Oklahoma contains the Wichita Mountains, an isolated range of rocky hills, granite formations, grasslands, and lakes. Eastern Oklahoma is more humid and includes wooded hills, valleys, and the state's southern extension of the Ozark and Ouachita regions. Major rivers include the Arkansas, Canadian, Cimarron, and Red rivers, while the Red River forms much of the southern boundary with Texas. Oklahoma's landscape therefore combines Great Plains grasslands, wooded transition zones, isolated mountains, and river valleys within a relatively compact area.

Oklahoma Borders ▼ +

Oklahoma shares borders with six states: Kansas and Colorado to the north, Missouri and Arkansas to the east, and Texas and New Mexico to the south and west.

Oklahoma has no ocean coastline, but it contains numerous lakes and rivers that form important freshwater systems throughout the state.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Oklahoma is Black Mesa, which reaches approximately 4,973 feet (1,516 meters) above sea level.

Black Mesa is located in the Oklahoma Panhandle near the borders with Colorado and New Mexico. It is part of the High Plains region and is the highest natural point in the state.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Oklahoma has an extensive network of rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Arkansas River, Canadian River, North Canadian River, Red River, Washita River, Cimarron River, and Neosho River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, water resources, agriculture, recreation, flood management, transportation, and communities throughout the state. Oklahoma also contains numerous reservoirs and lakes.

Economy of Oklahoma ▼ +

Oklahoma's economy has strong ties to energy, aerospace, agriculture, manufacturing, and transportation. Oil and natural gas remain major industries, supported by production fields across several regions of the state, while Oklahoma has also expanded wind energy across its open plains. Oklahoma City is an important center for energy companies, aerospace, aviation, healthcare, and business services, while Tulsa has major activities in aerospace, manufacturing, energy, and transportation.

Agriculture remains a major part of Oklahoma's rural economy, with cattle ranching and wheat production especially important across the Great Plains. The state also produces poultry, cotton, soybeans, hay, and other crops, while food processing and agricultural businesses support the sector. Oklahoma's central location provides connections through major highways, railways, and pipelines, supporting freight transportation and energy distribution. Manufacturing, construction, technology, healthcare, education, and tourism further diversify the state's economy, with outdoor recreation centered on its lakes, mountains, forests, and state parks.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Oklahoma is divided into 77 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Oklahoma include Oklahoma City, Tulsa, Norman, Broken Arrow, Edmond, and Lawton.

Oklahoma City is the state capital and largest city by population. Tulsa is a major economic and cultural center in northeastern Oklahoma and has strong connections to energy, aerospace, manufacturing, healthcare, and education. Norman is home to the University of Oklahoma, while Lawton is an important regional center in southwestern Oklahoma.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Oklahoma's natural environment reflects its position between the Great Plains and the forested regions of the eastern United States. The western part of the state contains shortgrass and mixed-grass prairies adapted to relatively dry conditions, while the central Cross Timbers contains distinctive oak woodlands mixed with grasslands. Eastern Oklahoma is wetter and more heavily forested, with wooded hills, streams, and habitats associated with the Ozark and Ouachita regions.

The Wichita Mountains in southwestern Oklahoma combine rocky formations, native grasslands, and open woodland and provide habitat for American bison, elk, white-tailed deer, and other wildlife. The state also contains unusual wetland and salt environments, including the Great Salt Plains, as well as numerous reservoirs and river systems. The Arkansas, Canadian, Cimarron, and Red rivers support riparian habitats and aquatic species, while Oklahoma's prairies, forests, wetlands, and lakes provide habitat for coyotes, bobcats, wild turkeys, waterfowl, reptiles, and many grassland birds.
