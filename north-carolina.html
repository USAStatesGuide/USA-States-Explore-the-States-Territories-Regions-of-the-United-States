About North Carolina ▼ +

North Carolina is a state in the southeastern region of the United States. It is bordered by Virginia to the north, Tennessee to the west, Georgia and South Carolina to the south, and the Atlantic Ocean to the east. The capital of North Carolina is Raleigh.

North Carolina became the 12th state of the United States on November 21, 1789. The state is known for its Appalachian Mountains, Piedmont region, Atlantic coastline, Outer Banks, forests, rivers, agricultural lands, and major cities such as Charlotte and Raleigh.

Geography of North Carolina ▼ +

North Carolina has a distinctive west-to-east landscape divided into the Appalachian Mountains, Piedmont, and Coastal Plain. The western mountains include the Blue Ridge and Great Smoky Mountains, with steep forested slopes, deep valleys, and high ridges. The Piedmont forms a broad zone of rolling hills between the mountains and coast, with the Fall Line marking an important transition where rivers descend toward the Coastal Plain.

Eastern North Carolina is much flatter and contains extensive farmland, pine forests, wetlands, and slow-moving rivers. Along the Atlantic coast, the Outer Banks form a long chain of barrier islands separated from the mainland by sounds and shallow waterways, including Albemarle and Pamlico sounds. Cape Hatteras and the surrounding coastline are particularly distinctive, with sandy beaches, dunes, tidal marshes, and shifting coastal landscapes. Major rivers such as the Roanoke, Neuse, Cape Fear, and Tar shape valleys and estuaries across the eastern part of the state.

North Carolina Borders ▼ +

North Carolina shares land borders with four states: Virginia to the north, Tennessee to the west, Georgia to the southwest, and South Carolina to the south.

The eastern part of North Carolina has an extensive coastline along the Atlantic Ocean. The coast includes beaches, barrier islands, sounds, estuaries, marshes, and other important coastal environments.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in North Carolina is Mount Mitchell, which reaches approximately 6,684 feet (2,037 meters) above sea level.

Mount Mitchell is located in the Black Mountains of western North Carolina. It is the highest mountain in the state and the highest point east of the Mississippi River in the contiguous United States.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

North Carolina has an extensive network of rivers, lakes, sounds, estuaries, and wetlands. Major rivers include the Cape Fear River, Neuse River, Roanoke River, Yadkin River, Catawba River, and French Broad River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, agriculture, water supplies, recreation, transportation, and communities. Major lakes and reservoirs include Lake Norman, Jordan Lake, Falls Lake, Kerr Lake, and Lake Mattamuskeet.

Economy of North Carolina ▼ +

North Carolina has a diversified economy with major strengths in finance, technology, biotechnology, healthcare, manufacturing, and agriculture. The Research Triangle around Raleigh, Durham, and Chapel Hill is a major center for universities, scientific research, biotechnology, pharmaceuticals, and technology companies. Charlotte is one of the largest banking and financial centers in the United States, while the Piedmont has long been associated with furniture, textiles, food processing, and other manufacturing industries.

Agriculture remains important across the Coastal Plain and other rural regions, with North Carolina producing tobacco, sweet potatoes, soybeans, corn, peanuts, poultry, and pork. The state's Atlantic coastline also supports commercial fishing, seafood processing, and port activity, particularly around the Port of Wilmington. Aerospace, automotive manufacturing, information technology, and renewable energy have expanded the industrial base, while tourism contributes significantly through the Blue Ridge Mountains, Outer Banks, beaches, historic towns, and coastal recreation.

Administrative Division ▼ +

North Carolina is divided into 100 counties. Counties are an important level of local government and administration within the state.

Some of the state's major counties include Wake County, which includes Raleigh, Mecklenburg County, which includes Charlotte, Guilford County, which includes Greensboro, Forsyth County, which includes Winston-Salem, and Buncombe County, which includes Asheville.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in North Carolina include Charlotte, Raleigh, Greensboro, Durham, Winston-Salem, Fayetteville, Wilmington, and Asheville.

Charlotte is the state's largest city and a major financial and business center. Raleigh is the state capital and an important center for government, technology, and education. Durham is known for research, healthcare, and higher education, while Wilmington is an important coastal city and tourism center.

Natural Environment ▼ +

North Carolina's natural environment changes significantly from the Appalachian Mountains to the Atlantic coast. The Blue Ridge and Great Smoky Mountains contain extensive deciduous forests, mountain streams, waterfalls, and high-elevation habitats, while the Coastal Plain is characterized by longleaf pine forests, pocosins, swamps, and broad river systems. The state also contains distinctive wetlands such as the Great Dismal Swamp and the pocosin habitats of eastern North Carolina.

Along the coast, the Outer Banks, Pamlico Sound, salt marshes, tidal creeks, dunes, and estuaries form an extensive network of marine and coastal habitats. These environments support sea turtles, dolphins, fish, shellfish, waterfowl, and large numbers of migratory birds. Inland forests provide habitat for black bears, white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, and many other species, while the state's rivers and wetlands support diverse freshwater ecosystems. Protected areas such as Great Smoky Mountains National Park and Cape Hatteras National Seashore preserve some of North Carolina's most distinctive natural landscapes.
