Pennsylvania, United States

About Pennsylvania ▼ +

Pennsylvania is a state in the northeastern and Mid-Atlantic regions of the United States. It is bordered by New York to the north, New Jersey to the east, Delaware and Maryland to the south, West Virginia and Ohio to the west, and Lake Erie to the northwest. The capital of Pennsylvania is Harrisburg.

Pennsylvania became the 2nd state of the United States on December 12, 1787. The state is known for its Appalachian Mountains, forests, rivers, fertile valleys, agricultural lands, major cities, and important historical sites.

Geography of Pennsylvania ▼ +

Pennsylvania's landscape is strongly shaped by the Appalachian Mountains, which cross the state from southwest to northeast. The central and eastern regions contain the Ridge-and-Valley Appalachians, where long forested ridges alternate with narrow valleys and fertile farmland. Farther west, the Allegheny Plateau rises into a broad area of dissected hills, deep valleys, and forested uplands. The Pocono Mountains in the northeast form another distinctive mountainous region with forests, lakes, and steep valleys.

Pennsylvania's rivers have played an important role in shaping its geography and settlement. The Susquehanna River crosses much of the eastern part of the state, while the Allegheny and Monongahela rivers meet at Pittsburgh to form the Ohio River. In the southeast, the Delaware River borders New Jersey and connects the Philadelphia region with the Atlantic coast. Northwestern Pennsylvania reaches Lake Erie, giving the state a Great Lakes shoreline with beaches, wetlands, and coastal lowlands. These mountains, river valleys, plateaus, and lake environments create a highly varied landscape within the state.

Pennsylvania Borders ▼ +

Pennsylvania shares land borders with six states: New York to the north, New Jersey to the east, Delaware and Maryland to the south, and West Virginia and Ohio to the west.

Pennsylvania also has a shoreline along Lake Erie in the northwestern part of the state.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Pennsylvania is Mount Davis, which reaches approximately 3,213 feet (979 meters) above sea level.

Mount Davis is located in Somerset County in southwestern Pennsylvania. It is part of the Allegheny Mountains and is the highest natural point in the state.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Pennsylvania has an extensive network of rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Susquehanna River, Delaware River, Ohio River, Allegheny River, Monongahela River, Lehigh River, and Youghiogheny River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, drinking water, agriculture, transportation, recreation, industry, fisheries, and communities throughout the state. The Susquehanna, Delaware, and Ohio river systems are especially significant.

Economy of Pennsylvania ▼ +

Pennsylvania has a highly diversified economy, with major strengths in healthcare, higher education, manufacturing, finance, energy, technology, and professional services. Philadelphia is an important center for healthcare, pharmaceuticals, biotechnology, finance, and business services, while Pittsburgh has a strong base in healthcare, technology, advanced manufacturing, and engineering. The state also retains an important industrial tradition, particularly in metals, machinery, chemicals, and food manufacturing.

Agriculture remains significant, especially in the fertile valleys and rural areas of southeastern and central Pennsylvania. The state is a major U.S. producer of mushrooms and dairy products and also produces poultry, corn, soybeans, apples, and other crops. Natural gas production has expanded in western and northern Pennsylvania as part of the Marcellus Shale region. The Port of Philadelphia, Pennsylvania's extensive highway and rail networks, and Pittsburgh's river transportation system support trade and distribution, while tourism contributes through the Pocono Mountains, historic sites, state parks, and major cities.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Pennsylvania is divided into 67 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, boroughs, townships, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Pennsylvania include Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Allentown, Erie, Reading, and Scranton.

Harrisburg is the state capital, while Philadelphia is the largest city by population and a major center for business, education, healthcare, culture, and history. Pittsburgh is an important economic and technological center in western Pennsylvania, while Allentown, Erie, Reading, and Scranton serve as important regional population and economic centers.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Pennsylvania is largely covered by temperate forests, especially across the Appalachian Mountains, Pocono Mountains, and Allegheny Plateau. These forests contain oak, maple, hickory, and other hardwoods, along with areas of hemlock and pine. The state's mountainous terrain also includes clear streams, waterfalls, wetlands, and forested valleys, with the Laurel Highlands and surrounding Appalachian landscapes providing important wildlife habitats.

Pennsylvania's rivers and wetlands form major freshwater ecosystems, particularly along the Susquehanna, Delaware, Allegheny, and Monongahela rivers. The Lake Erie shoreline in the northwest contains beaches, wetlands, marshes, and shallow freshwater habitats used by fish and migratory birds. Forests and rural landscapes provide habitat for white-tailed deer, black bears, wild turkeys, beavers, bobcats, and many woodland birds, while the state's streams and rivers support trout, bass, and other freshwater species.
