About Arizona ▼ +

Arizona is a state in the southwestern region of the United States. It is bordered by Utah to the north, Colorado and New Mexico to the northeast and east, Nevada and California to the west, and Mexico to the south. The capital and largest city of Arizona is Phoenix.

Arizona became the 48th state of the United States on February 14, 1912. The state is known for its deserts, mountains, canyons, plateaus, forests, and distinctive southwestern landscapes. The Grand Canyon is one of its most famous natural features.

Geography of Arizona ▼ +

Arizona's geography is shaped by strong changes in elevation and distinctive landforms. Southern Arizona is dominated by the Sonoran Desert, while northern and northeastern areas rise onto the Colorado Plateau, a region of high mesas, cliffs, and deeply eroded landscapes. The Mogollon Rim forms a prominent escarpment across central Arizona and marks a major transition between the lower desert and higher forested regions.

The Grand Canyon is Arizona's most famous geographic feature, extending across the northern part of the state along the Colorado River. The surrounding Colorado Plateau also includes the Painted Desert and numerous mesas, buttes, and canyons. In northern Arizona, the San Francisco Peaks rise above the surrounding plateau, while forests of ponderosa pine cover many of the higher elevations. Together, these contrasting landscapes give Arizona an unusually diverse geography for a southwestern state.

Arizona Borders ▼ +

Arizona shares land borders with four U.S. states: Utah to the north, New Mexico to the east, Nevada and California to the west.

Arizona also borders the Mexican states of Sonora and Baja California to the south. Colorado meets Arizona at the Four Corners, where four U.S. states share a common point.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Arizona is Humphreys Peak, which reaches approximately 12,633 feet (3,851 meters) above sea level.

Humphreys Peak is located north of Flagstaff in the San Francisco Peaks. It is the highest natural point in Arizona and is part of the Coconino National Forest.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Arizona has several important rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Colorado River, Gila River, Little Colorado River, Salt River, and Verde River.

The Colorado River is especially important because it forms part of Arizona's western boundary and flows through the Grand Canyon. Arizona's rivers and reservoirs provide water for communities, agriculture, ecosystems, recreation, and other activities.

Economy of Arizona ▼ +

Arizona has a diverse economy shaped by its growing metropolitan areas, desert environment, and strategic location in the Southwest. Phoenix is a major center for technology, business, manufacturing, healthcare, and financial services. The state has also become an important location for semiconductor manufacturing and advanced technology industries, while aerospace and defense activities are especially significant around Phoenix and Tucson.

Arizona also has long-established mining and agricultural industries. Copper mining is particularly important to the state's history and economy, while farms in irrigated desert valleys produce crops such as cotton, vegetables, citrus, and hay. Tourism is another major sector, supported by destinations including the Grand Canyon, Monument Valley, desert landscapes, national parks, and the state's many outdoor recreation areas.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Arizona is divided into 15 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Arizona include Phoenix, Tucson, Mesa, Chandler, Scottsdale, and Glendale.

Phoenix is the state capital and largest city by population. Tucson is a major city in southern Arizona and an important center for education, research, and culture. Mesa, Chandler, Scottsdale, and Glendale are major population centers in the Phoenix metropolitan area.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Arizona is known for its striking contrasts between low desert environments and high mountain forests. The Sonoran Desert covers much of the southern part of the state and is especially notable for its giant saguaro cacti, which have become a defining symbol of Arizona. Farther north and at higher elevations, the landscape changes to grasslands, pine forests, fir forests, and cooler mountain ecosystems.

Northern Arizona contains some of the state's most distinctive natural environments, including the Grand Canyon and the Colorado River. The Mogollon Rim forms a major transition between the lower desert regions and the higher Colorado Plateau. Arizona's varied elevations create habitats for species such as desert bighorn sheep, pronghorn, elk, mule deer, black bears, and many species of reptiles and birds.
