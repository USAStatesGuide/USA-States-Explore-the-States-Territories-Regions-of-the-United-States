About New Jersey ▼ +

New Jersey is a state in the Mid-Atlantic and Northeastern regions of the United States. It is bordered by New York to the north and northeast, Pennsylvania to the west, and Delaware to the southwest. The state also has coastlines along the Atlantic Ocean and Delaware Bay. The capital of New Jersey is Trenton.

New Jersey became the 3rd state of the United States on December 18, 1787. The state is known for its Atlantic coastline, beaches, forests, wetlands, farmland, and highly developed urban and suburban areas. It is also located within the major metropolitan region surrounding New York City and Philadelphia.

Geography of New Jersey ▼ +

New Jersey's landscape changes noticeably from north to south. The northern part contains the Appalachian Highlands, including the Kittatinny Mountains and forested ridges, valleys, and lakes. Central New Jersey includes the Piedmont and rolling hills, while the southern part is dominated by the flat Coastal Plain. The Pine Barrens are one of the state's most distinctive landscapes, covering a large area of sandy soils, pine forests, wetlands, and slow-moving streams.

New Jersey has an extensive Atlantic coastline known as the Jersey Shore, with barrier islands, sandy beaches, tidal bays, and coastal estuaries. The Delaware River forms most of the western boundary with Pennsylvania, while the Hudson River separates northeastern New Jersey from New York City. The Meadowlands near the New York metropolitan area form an unusual urban wetland landscape, combining tidal marshes and waterways with one of the most densely developed regions in the United States.

New Jersey Borders ▼ +

New Jersey shares land and river boundaries with three states: New York to the north and northeast, Pennsylvania to the west, and Delaware to the southwest.

New Jersey also has an extensive coastline along the Atlantic Ocean, including many beaches, barrier islands, bays, and coastal communities.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in New Jersey is High Point, which reaches approximately 1,803 feet (550 meters) above sea level.

High Point is located in the northern part of the state within High Point State Park and the Kittatinny Mountains. It is the highest natural point in New Jersey.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

New Jersey has an extensive network of rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Delaware River, Passaic River, Raritan River, Hackensack River, and Mullica River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, drinking water, transportation, agriculture, recreation, industry, and communities throughout the state. The Atlantic coastline also contains numerous bays, estuaries, marshes, and coastal wetlands.

Economy of New Jersey ▼ +

New Jersey has a highly diversified economy, with particular strengths in pharmaceuticals, biotechnology, finance, technology, healthcare, manufacturing, and professional services. The state has one of the largest concentrations of pharmaceutical and life-science companies in the United States, especially in northern and central New Jersey. Its position between New York City and Philadelphia also supports a large network of corporate offices, research facilities, and business services.

New Jersey is a major transportation and distribution center because of its location on the Northeast Corridor. The Port of New York and New Jersey handles large volumes of international cargo, while highways, railways, and airports connect the state with major U.S. markets. Agriculture remains important in southern and central areas, producing blueberries, cranberries, tomatoes, vegetables, and nursery products. Tourism also contributes to the economy through the Jersey Shore, Atlantic City, the Pine Barrens, and historic communities.

Administrative Division ▼ +

New Jersey is divided into 21 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, townships, boroughs, villages, and other municipalities. Local governments provide various public services to residents throughout New Jersey.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in New Jersey include Newark, Jersey City, Paterson, Elizabeth, Trenton, and Edison.

Trenton is the state capital. Newark is the largest city by population and an important transportation, commercial, and cultural center. Jersey City is a major business and residential center across the Hudson River from Manhattan, while Paterson and Elizabeth are important urban centers in northern New Jersey.

Natural Environment ▼ +

New Jersey contains several distinctive ecosystems despite its relatively small size. The Pine Barrens are characterized by sandy soils, pitch pine and oak forests, cedar swamps, wetlands, and clear tea-colored streams. Northern New Jersey has the forested Highlands, rocky ridges, lakes, and mountain streams, while the Meadowlands near New York Harbor contain tidal marshes and waterways surrounded by a heavily developed metropolitan landscape.

The Atlantic coast supports barrier beaches, dunes, salt marshes, tidal creeks, and estuaries, including important habitats around Barnegat Bay and Delaware Bay. These coastal environments provide feeding and nursery areas for fish, shellfish, shorebirds, and migratory birds. New Jersey's forests and wetlands also support white-tailed deer, black bears, beavers, wild turkeys, and numerous amphibians and reptiles. The state's protected natural areas preserve a remarkable combination of inland forests, freshwater wetlands, tidal marshes, and coastal ecosystems.
