About Maryland ▼ +

Maryland is a state in the Mid-Atlantic region of the United States. It is bordered by Pennsylvania to the north, Delaware and the Atlantic Ocean to the east, Virginia and West Virginia to the west and southwest, and the District of Columbia to the south. The capital of Maryland is Annapolis.

Maryland became the seventh state of the United States on April 28, 1788. The state is known for the Chesapeake Bay, coastal areas, forests, rivers, fertile agricultural lands, and varied landscapes extending from the Atlantic Coastal Plain to the Appalachian Mountains.

Geography of Maryland ▼ +

Maryland has an unusually varied landscape for a relatively small state, extending from the Atlantic Coastal Plain in the east to the Appalachian Mountains in the west. The Chesapeake Bay divides the state into the Eastern Shore and the western shore, with numerous tidal rivers and inlets branching into the surrounding lowlands. Central Maryland includes the rolling Piedmont, while the far west contains forested ridges, valleys, and higher elevations of the Appalachian Plateau.

The Eastern Shore forms part of the Delmarva Peninsula and is characterized by flat farmland, marshes, tidal creeks, and coastal wetlands. Around the Chesapeake Bay, peninsulas and river estuaries create a deeply indented shoreline. Western Maryland becomes increasingly rugged, with the Blue Ridge and Allegheny Mountains, narrow valleys, and major elevations such as Backbone Mountain. The Potomac River and its tributaries also form important geographic corridors across the state.

Maryland Borders ▼ +

Maryland shares land borders with Pennsylvania to the north, Delaware to the east, Virginia and West Virginia to the west and southwest, and the District of Columbia to the south.

Maryland also has extensive shorelines along the Chesapeake Bay, the Atlantic Ocean, and related coastal waters.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Maryland is Hoye-Crest on Backbone Mountain, which reaches approximately 3,360 feet (1,024 meters) above sea level.

Hoye-Crest is located in Garrett County in western Maryland, within the Appalachian Mountain region, and is the highest natural point in the state.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Maryland has an extensive network of rivers, streams, bays, and waterways. Major rivers include the Potomac River, Susquehanna River, Patuxent River, and Patapsco River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, transportation, water resources, agriculture, recreation, fisheries, and communities throughout the state.

Economy of Maryland ▼ +

Maryland's economy benefits strongly from its location between Baltimore and Washington, D.C., with major activity in biotechnology, cybersecurity, information technology, healthcare, defense, research, and professional services. The Baltimore region is an important center for healthcare, manufacturing, shipping, and logistics, while proximity to the nation's capital supports federal agencies, contractors, technology companies, and research institutions. Maryland is also home to major universities and scientific research facilities that contribute to innovation and skilled employment.

Chesapeake Bay gives Maryland an important maritime economy, including commercial fishing, blue crab and oyster industries, seafood processing, and recreational boating. The Port of Baltimore handles automobiles, machinery, containers, and other international cargo. Agriculture remains important on the Eastern Shore and other rural areas, with poultry, corn, soybeans, vegetables, and dairy among the major products. Tourism also benefits from the Chesapeake Bay, Atlantic beaches, historic sites, and outdoor recreation.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Maryland is divided into 23 counties and Baltimore City, which is an independent city and is not part of Baltimore County.

The state also contains cities, towns, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Maryland include Baltimore, Frederick, Gaithersburg, Rockville, Bowie, and Annapolis.

Annapolis is the state capital. Baltimore is the largest city by population and an important center for healthcare, education, culture, commerce, and transportation. The Washington, D.C. metropolitan area also includes several major population centers in Maryland, particularly in Montgomery and Prince George's counties.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Maryland's natural environment changes noticeably from the Atlantic Coastal Plain to the Appalachian Mountains. The Chesapeake Bay and its tidal rivers contain extensive marshes, shallow waters, mudflats, and estuaries that provide important nursery habitats for blue crabs, oysters, fish, and other aquatic life. The Eastern Shore also contains coastal wetlands, forests, and waterways that support large numbers of migratory and resident birds.

Western Maryland has a cooler, more mountainous environment with extensive deciduous forests, streams, rocky ridges, and freshwater habitats. Areas around the Appalachian Mountains support white-tailed deer, black bears, wild turkeys, and numerous forest species. Along the Atlantic Coastal Plain and Chesapeake Bay, wetlands and forests provide habitat for bald eagles, herons, ospreys, turtles, and other wildlife, making the connection between the state's inland forests and coastal ecosystems especially important.
