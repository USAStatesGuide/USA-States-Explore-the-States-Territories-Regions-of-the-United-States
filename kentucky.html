Kentucky, United States

About Kentucky ▼ +

Kentucky is a state in the eastern south-central United States. It is bordered by Illinois, Indiana, and Ohio to the north, West Virginia and Virginia to the east, Tennessee to the south, and Missouri to the west. The capital of Kentucky is Frankfort, while Louisville is the state's largest city by population.

Kentucky became the 15th state of the United States on June 1, 1792. The state is known for its rolling hills, forests, caves, limestone landscapes, rivers, agricultural areas, horse farms, and distinctive Appalachian and Cumberland Plateau landscapes in its eastern regions.

Geography of Kentucky ▼ +

Kentucky has a varied landscape shaped by the Appalachian Mountains, limestone plateaus, rolling hills, and major river valleys. Eastern Kentucky contains the rugged Appalachian Plateau, with steep forested ridges and narrow valleys, while central Kentucky includes the Bluegrass Region, a rolling limestone landscape known for fertile soils, springs, and extensive horse farms. The Pennyroyal Plateau surrounds much of central Kentucky and contains distinctive karst terrain formed in limestone.

Kentucky's limestone geology has produced an extensive underground landscape of caves and sinkholes, most famously at Mammoth Cave, part of the world's longest known cave system. Western Kentucky becomes flatter toward the Mississippi River, with lowlands and broad agricultural areas. The Ohio River forms most of the northern boundary, while the Kentucky, Cumberland, and Tennessee rivers cut through the interior and create important valleys and waterways.

Kentucky Borders ▼ +

Kentucky shares borders with Illinois, Indiana, and Ohio to the north; West Virginia and Virginia to the east; Tennessee to the south; and Missouri to the west.

Several of Kentucky's state boundaries follow major rivers. The Ohio River forms much of the northern boundary, while the Mississippi River forms a short section of the western boundary. The Tennessee River and other waterways also play important roles in the state's geography.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Kentucky is Black Mountain, which reaches approximately 4,145 feet (1,263 meters) above sea level.

Black Mountain is located in Harlan County in southeastern Kentucky near the Virginia border. It is part of the Appalachian Mountains and represents the highest natural elevation in the state.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Kentucky has an extensive network of rivers and streams. Major waterways include the Ohio River, Mississippi River, Kentucky River, Tennessee River, Cumberland River, and Green River.

These waterways are important for transportation, agriculture, ecosystems, recreation, fisheries, water resources, and industry. The Ohio River is particularly important because it forms much of Kentucky's northern boundary and connects the state to the Mississippi River system.

Economy of Kentucky ▼ +

Kentucky has a strong manufacturing base, particularly in automobile production, aerospace, machinery, and food and beverage processing. Automobile plants and parts suppliers are located in several parts of the state, while aerospace manufacturing and engineering are important around Louisville and northern Kentucky. The state's central location along major highways, railways, and the Ohio River also makes transportation and logistics important to its economy.

Agriculture has a distinctive role in Kentucky, especially through thoroughbred horse breeding and racing, cattle, poultry, soybeans, corn, and hay. Kentucky is also the leading center of bourbon production, with distilleries concentrated in areas where limestone-rich water and established aging traditions support the industry. Coal mining remains important in eastern Kentucky, while healthcare, tourism, and outdoor recreation contribute through attractions such as Mammoth Cave, the Kentucky Horse Park, and the state's scenic Appalachian landscapes.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Kentucky is divided into 120 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state and perform various administrative and public service functions.

The state also contains cities, towns, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Frankfort is the state capital, while Louisville is the largest city by population.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Kentucky include Louisville, Lexington, Bowling Green, Owensboro, Covington, and Frankfort.

Louisville is the largest city and an important center for transportation, logistics, manufacturing, healthcare, business, and culture. Lexington is a major educational and economic center and is internationally associated with horse breeding and racing. Frankfort is the state capital and center of state government.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Kentucky's natural environment is strongly influenced by its Appalachian forests, limestone geology, and extensive river systems. Eastern Kentucky contains some of the state's largest continuous forested landscapes, with steep wooded ridges, narrow valleys, and mountain streams. Central Kentucky's limestone terrain supports the Bluegrass landscape of fertile soils, springs, pastures, and scattered woodlands, while western Kentucky contains broader wetlands, forests, and river lowlands.

Kentucky's limestone bedrock has created an exceptional network of caves, sinkholes, and underground waterways, most notably at Mammoth Cave. Above ground, the Ohio, Kentucky, Cumberland, and Tennessee rivers provide important aquatic and wetland habitats. These environments support species such as white-tailed deer, black bears, wild turkeys, river otters, bats, and numerous fish and amphibians, while forested valleys and wetlands provide important habitats for migratory birds.
