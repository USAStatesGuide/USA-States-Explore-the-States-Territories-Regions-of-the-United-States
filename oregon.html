About Oregon ▼ +

Oregon is a state in the Pacific Northwest region of the United States. It is bordered by Washington to the north, Idaho to the east, Nevada and California to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. The capital of Oregon is Salem.

Oregon became the 33rd state of the United States on February 14, 1859. The state is known for its diverse landscapes, including the Pacific coastline, forests, mountains, valleys, high desert, rivers, lakes, and volcanic landscapes.

Geography of Oregon ▼ +

Oregon's geography is marked by a strong contrast between its wet western landscapes and the much drier interior. The Coast Range follows much of the Pacific coast, while the Cascade Range runs north to south through the state and contains volcanic peaks such as Mount Hood, Mount Jefferson, and the Three Sisters. Between the Coast Range and Cascades lies the Willamette Valley, a broad and fertile lowland that contains many of Oregon's largest communities and agricultural areas.

Eastern and central Oregon are characterized by high desert, plateaus, volcanic landscapes, and mountain ranges such as the Blue Mountains and Wallowa Mountains. The Columbia River forms much of the northern boundary with Washington and cuts through the Cascade Range to create the spectacular Columbia River Gorge. In the south, the Klamath Mountains and high desert add further geographic diversity, while the Pacific coast contains rocky headlands, sandy beaches, bays, dunes, and estuaries. This combination of volcanic mountains, fertile valleys, deep river gorges, and high desert gives Oregon a particularly varied landscape.

Oregon Borders ▼ +

Oregon shares land borders with four states: Washington to the north, Idaho to the east, Nevada to the southeast, and California to the south.

Oregon also has a coastline along the Pacific Ocean to the west. The Columbia River forms much of the northern boundary with Washington.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Oregon is Mount Hood, which reaches approximately 11,249 feet (3,429 meters) above sea level.

Mount Hood is a volcanic mountain in the Cascade Range and is located about 50 miles (80 kilometers) east of Portland. It is one of Oregon's most prominent natural landmarks and is part of Mount Hood National Forest.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Oregon has an extensive network of rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Columbia River, Willamette River, Deschutes River, Rogue River, Umpqua River, John Day River, and Snake River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, agriculture, transportation, hydroelectric power, drinking water, recreation, fisheries, and communities throughout the state. The Columbia River is particularly important for navigation and trade in the Pacific Northwest.

Economy of Oregon ▼ +

Oregon has a diverse economy with important strengths in technology, manufacturing, forestry, agriculture, food processing, healthcare, and tourism. The Portland metropolitan area is the state's main business and technology center, with major activities in software, electronics, semiconductor manufacturing, and advanced industries. The Portland region also benefits from its location along the Columbia River and its connections to Pacific trade.

Forestry and wood products remain important, particularly in western and southwestern Oregon, where extensive forests support logging, lumber, and related industries. The fertile Willamette Valley is a major agricultural region known for grass seed, nursery products, berries, vegetables, hazelnuts, and vineyards. The Columbia River supports shipping and commercial trade, while the Pacific coast supports fishing, seafood processing, and tourism. Outdoor recreation and tourism are also significant, with visitors drawn to the Cascade Mountains, Columbia River Gorge, high desert, forests, and Pacific coastline.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Oregon is divided into 36 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Oregon include Portland, Salem, Eugene, Gresham, Hillsboro, and Bend.

Salem is the state capital, while Portland is the largest city by population and a major economic, cultural, and transportation center. Eugene is an important educational and cultural center, while Hillsboro is known for technology and high-tech manufacturing. Bend is an important regional center for Central Oregon and outdoor recreation.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Oregon's natural environment is strongly influenced by its Pacific climate, volcanic mountains, and sharp contrast between the wet west and dry interior. Western Oregon contains extensive temperate rainforests with Douglas fir, western hemlock, and western red cedar, while the Cascade Range supports conifer forests, alpine meadows, volcanic peaks, and high-elevation lakes. Crater Lake, formed within the caldera of an ancient volcano, is one of the state's most distinctive natural features.

The Pacific coast contains rocky headlands, sandy beaches, dunes, estuaries, tidal wetlands, and marine habitats that support seals, sea lions, whales, seabirds, and other coastal wildlife. The Columbia River Gorge combines steep cliffs, forests, waterfalls, and river habitats, while central and eastern Oregon contain high desert, sagebrush, grasslands, and juniper woodlands. These varied ecosystems provide habitat for species such as Roosevelt elk, black bears, beavers, mule deer, mountain goats, salmon, and trout.
