Massachusetts, United States

About Massachusetts ▼ +

Massachusetts is a state in the New England region of the northeastern United States. It is bordered by New Hampshire and Vermont to the north, Rhode Island and Connecticut to the south, New York to the west, and the Atlantic Ocean to the east. The capital of Massachusetts is Boston.

Massachusetts became the sixth state of the United States on February 6, 1788. The state is known for its Atlantic coastline, forests, mountains, rivers, historic communities, islands, and diverse landscapes extending from the Berkshire Mountains to the coastal areas of the Atlantic Ocean.

Geography of Massachusetts ▼ +

Massachusetts has a varied landscape that includes mountains, hills, forests, valleys, rivers, wetlands, agricultural lands, coastal plains, beaches, and islands. Western Massachusetts contains portions of the Berkshire Mountains and Appalachian landscapes, while central and eastern parts of the state include hills, valleys, forests, and coastal lowlands.

The eastern part of Massachusetts reaches the Atlantic Ocean and includes Massachusetts Bay, Cape Cod, and numerous islands. Important geographic features include the Berkshire Mountains, Connecticut River Valley, Cape Cod, the Atlantic coastline, and the islands of Martha's Vineyard and Nantucket.

Massachusetts Borders ▼ +

Massachusetts shares land borders with New Hampshire and Vermont to the north, New York to the west, and Connecticut and Rhode Island to the south.

Massachusetts also has an extensive coastline along the Atlantic Ocean, including Massachusetts Bay, Cape Cod Bay, and Nantucket Sound.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Massachusetts is Mount Greylock, which reaches approximately 3,491 feet (1,064 meters) above sea level.

Mount Greylock is located in Berkshire County in western Massachusetts. It is part of the Berkshire Mountains and is the highest natural point in the state.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Massachusetts has an extensive network of rivers, streams, lakes, ponds, bays, and coastal waters. Major rivers include the Connecticut River, Charles River, Housatonic River, Blackstone River, and Merrimack River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, drinking water, transportation, agriculture, recreation, fisheries, hydroelectric power, and communities throughout the state.

Economy of Massachusetts ▼ +

Massachusetts has a diverse economy supported by technology, biotechnology, healthcare, education, finance, manufacturing, professional services, tourism, maritime industries, and other service sectors.

The Boston metropolitan area is a major economic and educational center, with important universities, hospitals, technology companies, financial institutions, and research organizations. Biotechnology, healthcare, higher education, information technology, manufacturing, and tourism are additional important sectors of the state's economy.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Massachusetts is divided into 14 counties. These counties are an important geographic and administrative part of the state, although many local governmental functions are carried out by cities and towns.

The state also contains cities, towns, villages, municipalities, and other local communities. Cities and towns provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Massachusetts include Boston, Worcester, Springfield, Cambridge, Lowell, and New Bedford.

Boston is the state capital and largest city. It is an important center for finance, healthcare, education, technology, culture, and transportation. Cambridge is internationally known for its universities and research institutions, while Worcester and Springfield are important regional centers in central and western Massachusetts.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Massachusetts contains forests, mountains, rivers, wetlands, lakes, ponds, beaches, coastal habitats, islands, and agricultural landscapes. Its varied geography and climate support a wide diversity of plant and animal life.

The Atlantic coastline, Cape Cod, Massachusetts Bay, and surrounding islands contain important marine and coastal ecosystems, while western Massachusetts contains extensive forests, mountains, rivers, and inland habitats.
