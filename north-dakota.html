About North Dakota ▼ +

North Dakota is a state in the north-central United States. It is part of the Great Plains and is known for its broad prairies, fertile agricultural land, oil and natural gas production, and extensive wildlife habitats. The state also contains the western Badlands and is home to Theodore Roosevelt National Park.

Geography of North Dakota ▼ +

North Dakota lies almost entirely within the Great Plains, but its landscape changes from the exceptionally flat Red River Valley in the east to the rugged Badlands in the west. The Red River Valley contains some of the state's most fertile agricultural land, while the central Drift Prairie consists of gently rolling plains shaped by glacial deposits. Western North Dakota rises into the Missouri Plateau, where open grasslands, rolling terrain, buttes, and dry valleys create a more rugged landscape.

The Missouri River crosses western and central North Dakota and has created broad valleys, reservoirs, and important wildlife corridors. The Red River of the North forms the eastern border with Minnesota, while the Little Missouri River cuts through the western Badlands. Theodore Roosevelt National Park protects some of the state's most distinctive Badlands scenery, with colorful rock formations, eroded valleys, grasslands, and winding river landscapes. Numerous prairie wetlands and glacial lakes are scattered across the state, especially in the north-central and eastern regions.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in North Dakota is White Butte, located in Slope County in the southwestern part of the state.

Elevation: 3,506 feet (1,069 meters)
Location: Slope County

White Butte rises above the surrounding prairie and Badlands landscape and is the highest natural elevation in North Dakota.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Rivers and reservoirs are important features of North Dakota's landscape. They provide water for communities, agriculture, wildlife, recreation, and industry.

Missouri River: A major river flowing through western and central North Dakota.
Red River of the North: Forms much of the eastern boundary with Minnesota.
James River: Flows through the southeastern part of the state.
Sheyenne River: A major tributary of the Red River of the North.

Knife River: A tributary of the Missouri River in western North Dakota.
Little Missouri River: Flows through the Badlands region.

Major lakes and reservoirs include Lake Sakakawea, Devils Lake, Lake Oahe, and Lake Ashtabula.

Economy of North Dakota ▼ +

North Dakota's economy is strongly shaped by agriculture and energy production. The state is a major producer of wheat, corn, soybeans, canola, barley, sugar beets, and livestock, with especially productive farmland in the Red River Valley. Western North Dakota is part of the Bakken oil-producing region, which has made crude oil and natural gas important sources of employment, investment, and state revenue.

Energy production also includes lignite coal and electricity generation, while wind power has expanded across the state's open plains. Agriculture supports grain handling, food processing, livestock operations, and related businesses, while manufacturing and machinery production serve farming and energy industries. Fargo is a major regional center for technology, healthcare, education, and business services, while Bismarck is an important center for government, healthcare, and energy-related activities. Transportation, construction, tourism, and outdoor recreation also contribute to the state's economy.

Natural Environment ▼ +

North Dakota's natural environment is dominated by Great Plains grasslands, but its western Badlands and extensive prairie wetlands create distinctive ecosystems. The Badlands along the Little Missouri River contain eroded cliffs, colorful rock formations, steep valleys, and mixed-grass prairie. In the central and eastern parts of the state, thousands of shallow wetlands and small lakes, often known as prairie potholes, provide important breeding habitat for birds and other wildlife.

The Missouri and Red rivers create important riparian ecosystems, while grasslands and wetlands across the northern and eastern plains support large numbers of migratory waterfowl and shorebirds. Theodore Roosevelt National Park protects Badlands, prairie, river valleys, and native wildlife habitats. North Dakota is home to bison, pronghorn, elk, white-tailed deer, coyotes, beavers, and numerous prairie birds, while its wetlands and rivers support fish, amphibians, and other aquatic species.
