About Navassa Island ▼ +

Navassa Island is a small, uninhabited island in the Caribbean Sea. It is an unincorporated territory of the United States and is located approximately 35 miles west of the southwestern coast of Haiti and about 90 miles south of Cuba.

The island is characterized by steep limestone cliffs, rocky terrain, forests, and coastal habitats. Navassa Island is managed as the Navassa Island National Wildlife Refuge and is recognized for its unique plants, reptiles, seabirds, and marine ecosystems.

Geography of Navassa Island ▼ +

Navassa Island is a small, rugged limestone island located in the Caribbean Sea. Much of its coastline consists of steep cliffs, while the interior contains rocky terrain, forest, shrubs, and limestone formations.

The island has no permanent streams or large freshwater rivers. Its geology and vegetation are strongly influenced by its limestone foundation, relatively dry climate, and exposure to tropical weather systems.

Navassa Island is situated between Haiti and Jamaica in the Caribbean region. It lies approximately 35 miles west of the southwestern peninsula of Haiti and about 90 miles south of Cuba.

The island is surrounded by the Caribbean Sea and is isolated from major population centers. Its position makes it an important habitat for species found in the Caribbean region.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point on Navassa Island is approximately 77 meters (253 feet) above sea level.

The island's elevated interior contrasts with its steep coastal cliffs. The terrain is generally rugged and rocky, with limestone formations covering much of the island.

Coastline and Waterways ▼ +

Navassa Island has a rugged coastline dominated by steep limestone cliffs and rocky shores. Several small coastal areas provide habitat for seabirds and marine organisms.

The island does not contain permanent rivers or major freshwater waterways. Rainfall and groundwater provide the limited freshwater resources available in the island's natural environment.

History of Navassa Island ▼ +

Navassa Island was claimed by the United States in 1857 under the Guano Islands Act. During the nineteenth century, the island was mined for guano, a valuable natural fertilizer.

Guano mining resulted in extensive human activity on the island and the construction of facilities for workers. Mining operations eventually declined and the island became uninhabited.

A lighthouse was established on Navassa Island in the late nineteenth century to help ships navigate the surrounding waters. The lighthouse was later automated and the island remained largely uninhabited.

Administrative Status ▼ +

Navassa Island is an unincorporated territory of the United States. It is not a U.S. state and does not have counties, cities, or a permanent civilian government structure.

The island is administered by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service as the Navassa Island National Wildlife Refuge. Access to the island is restricted in order to protect its wildlife and natural resources.

Major Settlements ▼ +

Navassa Island has no permanent cities, towns, or civilian settlements. The island has been uninhabited for many years.

Historical structures associated with guano mining and the lighthouse remain part of the island's human history, but there is no modern residential community or conventional urban infrastructure.

Marine Environment ▼ +

The waters surrounding Navassa Island contain coral reefs, rocky coastal habitats, and marine ecosystems typical of the Caribbean Sea.

The surrounding marine environment provides habitat for fish, corals, crustaceans, sea turtles, and other marine organisms. The island's isolation helps protect many of these habitats from intensive human development.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Navassa Island contains forests, shrubs, limestone habitats, coastal cliffs, and other natural environments. Despite its small size, the island supports a notable variety of plant and animal species.

The island provides important habitat for seabirds and contains several species of reptiles and plants that are adapted to its isolated Caribbean environment.

Navassa is particularly important for seabirds, including species such as the red-footed booby, brown booby, magnificent frigatebird, and brown noddy.

Conservation ▼ +

Navassa Island National Wildlife Refuge was established to protect the island's wildlife, plants, and natural habitats. The refuge is managed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

Conservation efforts focus on protecting seabird colonies, native plants, reptiles, coastal habitats, and the surrounding marine environment. The island's isolation is an important factor in maintaining its ecological value.

Navassa Island is not open for ordinary public visitation. Access is restricted and requires appropriate authorization for scientific research, conservation, management, or other permitted activities.
