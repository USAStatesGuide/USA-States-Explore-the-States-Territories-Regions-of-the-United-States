About Iowa ▼ +

Iowa is a state in the Midwestern United States. It is bordered by Minnesota to the north, Wisconsin and Illinois to the east, Missouri to the south, and Nebraska and South Dakota to the west. The capital and largest city of Iowa is Des Moines.

Iowa became the 29th state of the United States on December 28, 1846. The state is known for its fertile agricultural land, rolling prairies, rivers, forests, wetlands, and important farming and manufacturing industries.

Geography of Iowa ▼ +

Iowa is a mostly inland landscape of fertile plains and gently rolling hills shaped by glaciers and major rivers. The northern and central parts of the state contain broad agricultural plains formed from deep glacial soils, while the terrain becomes more varied toward the river valleys. The Mississippi River forms Iowa's entire eastern border, and the Missouri River forms much of its western border, giving the state a distinctive position between two major North American waterways.

Northeastern Iowa contains the Driftless Area, a region that escaped much of the last continental glaciation and is characterized by limestone bluffs, deeply cut valleys, springs, and rugged wooded hills. Western Iowa has another distinctive landscape in the Loess Hills, where wind-deposited silt forms long, narrow ridges along the Missouri River. Between these regions, extensive farmland and rolling prairie landscapes dominate much of the state.

Iowa Borders ▼ +

Iowa shares borders with Minnesota to the north, Wisconsin and Illinois to the east, Missouri to the south, and Nebraska and South Dakota to the west.

The Mississippi River forms most of Iowa's eastern boundary, while the Missouri River forms much of its western boundary. These major rivers are important geographic features of the state.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Iowa is Hawkeye Point, which reaches approximately 1,670 feet (509 meters) above sea level.

Hawkeye Point is located in Osceola County in northwestern Iowa. It is situated in a predominantly agricultural region near the Minnesota border.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Iowa has an extensive network of rivers and streams. Major waterways include the Mississippi River, Missouri River, Des Moines River, Iowa River, Cedar River, and Raccoon River.

These waterways are important for agriculture, ecosystems, recreation, water resources, wildlife habitats, and transportation. The Mississippi and Missouri rivers also form major parts of Iowa's eastern and western boundaries.

Economy of Iowa ▼ +

Iowa's economy is strongly shaped by its fertile farmland and its role as a major agricultural and food-producing state. Corn and soybeans are the leading crops, while large-scale production of hogs, cattle, eggs, and dairy products supports a substantial livestock industry. The state has a major network of grain elevators, feed mills, meat-processing plants, and other food-processing facilities that connect farms with national and international markets.

Manufacturing is closely linked to agriculture, with industries producing farm machinery, industrial equipment, food products, and other manufactured goods. Iowa has also developed important sectors in insurance and financial services, particularly around Des Moines, as well as biotechnology and renewable energy. Wind power is especially significant because Iowa's open plains provide favorable conditions for large wind farms, making the state one of the leading U.S. producers of wind-generated electricity.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Iowa is divided into 99 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state and perform various administrative and public service functions.

The state also contains cities, towns, townships, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Des Moines is the state capital and the largest city by population.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Iowa include Des Moines, Cedar Rapids, Davenport, Sioux City, Iowa City, and Waterloo.

Des Moines is the state capital and largest city and is an important center for government, finance, insurance, business, and services. Cedar Rapids is a major manufacturing and regional center, while Davenport is part of the Quad Cities metropolitan area along the Mississippi River.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Iowa's natural environment is closely tied to its former tallgrass prairie, fertile glacial soils, and major river systems. Most of the original prairie has been replaced by farmland, but restored prairie preserves still contain native grasses and wildflowers. The western part of the state includes the distinctive Loess Hills, where wind-deposited layers of fine sediment form narrow ridges and steep slopes along the Missouri River.

The Mississippi River forms Iowa's eastern border and supports floodplain forests, wetlands, islands, and aquatic habitats, while the Missouri River shapes the western edge. River valleys and remaining wetlands provide important habitats for waterfowl, fish, amphibians, and other wildlife. Woodlands are especially common along streams and in northeastern and southeastern Iowa, where species such as white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, beavers, and river otters can be found.
