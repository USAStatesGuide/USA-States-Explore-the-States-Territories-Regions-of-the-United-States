About Utah ▼ +

Utah is a state in the western region of the United States. It is bordered by Idaho and Wyoming to the north, Colorado to the east, Arizona to the south, and Nevada to the west. The capital and largest city of Utah is Salt Lake City.

Utah became the 45th state of the United States on January 4, 1896. The state is known for its dramatic mountain ranges, deserts, plateaus, canyons, forests, salt lakes, and other diverse landscapes.

Geography of Utah ▼ +

Utah's geography is shaped by a striking contrast between high mountain ranges, elevated plateaus, and dry desert basins. Northern and central Utah contain the Wasatch Range and other mountains, with deep valleys and high peaks rising above the Great Salt Lake region. Much of western Utah lies within the Great Basin and contains broad arid valleys, isolated mountain ranges, salt flats, and dry lakebeds. The Bonneville Salt Flats are one of the state's most distinctive landscapes in this western region.

Southern Utah forms part of the Colorado Plateau, where erosion has produced extensive sandstone cliffs, mesas, arches, slot canyons, and deep river gorges. The region contains five national parks: Zion, Bryce Canyon, Capitol Reef, Arches, and Canyonlands, each with distinctive geological formations. The Colorado River and its tributaries have carved many of southern Utah's major canyons, while the Great Salt Lake remains the state's largest inland body of water and one of the most important geographic features of northern Utah.

Utah Borders ▼ +

Utah shares land borders with six states: Idaho to the north, Wyoming to the northeast, Colorado to the east, Arizona to the south, Nevada to the west, and a small boundary with New Mexico at the Four Corners.

Utah is one of the Four Corners states, where Utah, Arizona, Colorado, and New Mexico meet at a single geographic point. Utah has no ocean coastline.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Utah is Kings Peak, which reaches approximately 13,528 feet (4,123 meters) above sea level.

Kings Peak is located in the Uinta Mountains in northeastern Utah. It is the highest natural point in the state and is part of the Uinta-Wasatch-Cache National Forest region.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Utah has an extensive network of rivers, streams, lakes, reservoirs, and wetlands. Major rivers include the Colorado River, Green River, Virgin River, San Juan River, Bear River, and Sevier River.

These waterways are important for water supply, agriculture, ecosystems, recreation, wildlife, hydroelectric power, and communities throughout the state. The Great Salt Lake is also one of Utah's most important geographic and environmental features.

Economy of Utah ▼ +

Utah has a diverse and rapidly growing economy with important strengths in technology, finance, healthcare, manufacturing, mining, construction, and outdoor recreation. The Salt Lake City metropolitan area is the state's main business center, while the Provo–Orem area is part of the technology corridor often known as Silicon Slopes, with companies involved in software, financial technology, cloud services, and other digital industries. Aerospace and defense activities are also important, particularly along the Wasatch Front.

Mining has a long economic history in Utah, with copper, gold, silver, and other minerals extracted from several regions. Tourism and outdoor recreation are major industries, supported by the state's five national parks, ski resorts, mountain landscapes, and desert scenery. Agriculture remains important in rural areas, producing cattle, dairy products, hay, fruits, and other crops, while transportation, logistics, education, and healthcare further contribute to the state's economy. The Wasatch Front's concentration of population and infrastructure also makes it a major regional transportation and commercial corridor.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Utah is divided into 29 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Utah include Salt Lake City, West Valley City, West Jordan, Provo, Orem, and Sandy.

Salt Lake City is the state capital and largest city. It is an important center for government, business, transportation, education, healthcare, and culture. Provo and Orem form an important population and educational center in Utah County, while West Valley City, West Jordan, and Sandy are major population centers in the Salt Lake Valley.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Utah's natural environment is shaped by strong contrasts in elevation and moisture. The Wasatch and other mountain ranges support forests of aspen, Douglas fir, spruce, and pine, along with alpine meadows, cold streams, and high-elevation habitats. In western Utah, the Great Salt Lake and its surrounding wetlands form a distinctive inland ecosystem, while the Great Basin contains sagebrush, salt flats, and extremely dry valleys.

Southern Utah is dominated by the arid landscapes of the Colorado Plateau, where desert vegetation grows around sandstone cliffs, mesas, arches, and deeply eroded canyons. The Colorado River and its tributaries create important riparian habitats in this otherwise dry region. Utah's varied ecosystems support species such as mule deer, elk, bighorn sheep, pronghorn, mountain lions, black bears, and desert reptiles, while the Great Salt Lake wetlands provide critical habitat for millions of migratory and breeding birds.
