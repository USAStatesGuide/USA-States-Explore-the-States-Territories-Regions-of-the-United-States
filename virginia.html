About Virginia ▼ +

Virginia is a state in the southeastern region of the United States. It is bordered by West Virginia and Maryland to the north, North Carolina and Tennessee to the south, Kentucky and West Virginia to the west, and the Atlantic Ocean to the east. The capital of Virginia is Richmond.

Virginia became the 10th state of the United States on June 25, 1788. The state is known for its mountains, forests, rivers, coastal plains, Chesapeake Bay, agricultural lands, historic communities, and diverse landscapes.

Geography of Virginia ▼ +

Virginia's landscape changes markedly from the Appalachian Mountains in the west to the low coastal plains in the east. The western part includes the Blue Ridge Mountains and the Shenandoah Valley, a broad agricultural valley bordered by mountain ranges. East of the Blue Ridge, the Piedmont consists of rolling hills and river valleys, gradually giving way to the flatter Coastal Plain, also known as the Tidewater region.

Chesapeake Bay is one of Virginia's defining geographic features, extending deep into the eastern part of the state and receiving numerous rivers and tidal tributaries. The Hampton Roads area around the lower James River forms a major coastal landscape of estuaries, tidal waterways, and lowlands. Virginia's coastline also includes barrier islands and marshes along the Atlantic, while major rivers such as the James, York, Rappahannock, and Potomac have shaped settlement, transportation, and agriculture across the state.

Virginia Borders ▼ +

Virginia shares land borders with Maryland and West Virginia to the north, North Carolina and Tennessee to the south, and Kentucky and West Virginia to the west.

Virginia also borders the Atlantic Ocean to the east. The state shares important waterways and boundary areas with Maryland and Washington, D.C., particularly around the Potomac River and Chesapeake Bay.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Virginia is Mount Rogers, which reaches approximately 5,729 feet (1,746 meters) above sea level.

Mount Rogers is located in southwestern Virginia near the North Carolina border. It is the highest natural point in the state and is part of the Blue Ridge Mountains.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Virginia has an extensive network of rivers, streams, estuaries, bays, and coastal waterways. Major rivers include the James River, Potomac River, Rappahannock River, York River, Shenandoah River, and Roanoke River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, transportation, agriculture, drinking water, recreation, wildlife, and communities throughout the state. The Chesapeake Bay and its tributaries also support important fisheries and coastal ecosystems.

Economy of Virginia ▼ +

Virginia has a highly diversified economy with major strengths in technology, government services, defense, finance, healthcare, transportation, and professional services. Northern Virginia is one of the country's major technology and data-center hubs and has a large concentration of cybersecurity companies, information technology firms, and federal contractors because of its proximity to Washington, D.C. Richmond is an important center for finance, insurance, government, and corporate services, while Virginia's universities and healthcare institutions contribute significantly to research and employment.

The Hampton Roads region has a major maritime economy centered on the Port of Virginia, naval facilities, shipbuilding, and transportation. Agriculture remains important in rural areas, producing poultry, cattle, soybeans, corn, tobacco, peanuts, vegetables, and specialty crops, while forestry supports timber and wood-product industries. Manufacturing, aerospace, biotechnology, and energy add further diversity, and tourism contributes through Colonial Williamsburg, Shenandoah National Park, Virginia Beach, historic sites, and the state's mountain and coastal landscapes.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Virginia is divided into 95 counties and also contains 38 independent cities. Independent cities are administratively separate from counties within the Commonwealth.

The state also contains towns, villages, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties, independent cities, and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Virginia include Virginia Beach, Chesapeake, Norfolk, Richmond, Arlington, and Newport News.

Richmond is the state capital and an important center for government, business, education, healthcare, and culture. Virginia Beach and the Hampton Roads region are major population and economic centers, while Northern Virginia contains major communities connected to the Washington, D.C. metropolitan area.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Virginia's natural environment changes from the forested Appalachian Mountains and Shenandoah Valley to the tidal wetlands of the Chesapeake Bay and Atlantic coast. The Blue Ridge and Appalachian regions contain extensive hardwood forests, mountain streams, waterfalls, and high-elevation habitats, while the Piedmont supports forests, rivers, and rolling landscapes. In southeastern Virginia, the Great Dismal Swamp forms a distinctive wetland ecosystem of forests, marshes, and shallow waterways.

Chesapeake Bay and its tidal tributaries provide important habitats for blue crabs, oysters, fish, waterfowl, and other aquatic species, while the Atlantic coast contains beaches, barrier islands, salt marshes, and coastal dunes. Virginia's forests and mountain habitats support white-tailed deer, black bears, wild turkeys, bobcats, and many woodland birds. Rivers such as the James, York, Rappahannock, and Potomac connect inland forests and wetlands with the bay and create important ecological corridors throughout the state.
