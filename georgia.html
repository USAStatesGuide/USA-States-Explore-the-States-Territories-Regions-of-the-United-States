Georgia, United States

About Georgia ▼ +

Georgia is a state in the southeastern United States. It borders Tennessee and North Carolina to the north, South Carolina to the northeast, Florida to the south, Alabama to the west, and the Atlantic Ocean to the southeast. Its capital and largest city is Atlanta.

Georgia became the 4th U.S. state on January 2, 1788. Known as the Peach State, it is also called the Empire State of the South. The state combines mountains in the north, rolling hills and forests in the central region, and coastal plains and wetlands in the south.

Geography of Georgia ▼ +

Georgia stretches from the southern Appalachian Mountains in the north to the Atlantic Coastal Plain in the south and east. The northernmost part includes the Blue Ridge Mountains, with steep forested slopes and valleys, while the Piedmont occupies much of central Georgia with rolling hills and red clay soils. The Fall Line marks the transition between the Piedmont and the lower Coastal Plain and has influenced the location of several major cities and historic settlements.

Southern Georgia is dominated by the broad Coastal Plain, characterized by pine forests, farmland, rivers, and wetlands. One of its most distinctive features is the Okefenokee Swamp, a vast freshwater wetland in the southeast. Along the Atlantic coast, Georgia has tidal marshes, estuaries, barrier islands, and sandy beaches, including the Golden Isles. The Altamaha River and other coastal waterways connect the interior landscape with the Atlantic Ocean.

State Borders ▼ +

Georgia shares land borders with Florida to the south, Alabama to the west, Tennessee and North Carolina to the north, and South Carolina to the northeast.

To the southeast, Georgia has a coastline along the Atlantic Ocean. The state's coastal boundary includes tidal marshes, barrier islands, and estuaries. The Savannah River forms much of the border with South Carolina.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Georgia is Brasstown Bald, located in the Blue Ridge Mountains of northeastern Georgia. It reaches an elevation of about 4,784 feet (1,458 meters) above sea level.

Brasstown Bald is located in Towns County and is surrounded by the Chattahoochee-Oconee National Forests. On clear days, its summit provides wide views across the mountainous region of northern Georgia.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Georgia has an extensive network of rivers and streams. Major waterways include the Savannah River, Chattahoochee River, Altamaha River, Ocmulgee River, and Oconee River.

The Savannah River forms much of Georgia's eastern border with South Carolina, while the Chattahoochee River is an important waterway in western Georgia and forms part of the border with Alabama. These rivers support ecosystems, agriculture, industry, recreation, and water supplies.

Economy of Georgia ▼ +

Georgia has a diversified economy with major strengths in transportation, logistics, manufacturing, finance, technology, agriculture, and entertainment. Atlanta is the state's dominant business center and a major transportation hub, supported by Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport and extensive highway and rail connections. The city is also an important center for technology, financial services, telecommunications, and corporate headquarters.

The Port of Savannah is one of the state's most important economic assets and handles large volumes of containerized international trade. Georgia also has major aerospace and automotive manufacturing operations, along with a growing film and television production industry. Agriculture remains distinctive to the state, with peanuts, poultry, cotton, pecans, blueberries, and peaches among its important products. Tourism, including Atlanta's attractions and the state's mountain and coastal destinations, adds another significant part of the economy.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Georgia is divided into 159 counties, the highest number of counties of any U.S. state east of the Mississippi River. Counties provide local government services and are important administrative divisions throughout the state.

The state also contains many incorporated cities and towns. Atlanta serves as the state capital and is the largest city, while other important urban centers are distributed across the metropolitan, Piedmont, and coastal regions.

Major Cities ▼ +

Atlanta is the largest city and capital of Georgia, and it is a major center for business, transportation, culture, and technology. Its metropolitan area is one of the largest urban regions in the southeastern United States.

Other major cities include Augusta, Columbus, Savannah, Athens, Macon, and Albany. Savannah is particularly known for its historic district and its important port on the Atlantic coast.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Georgia's natural environment changes markedly from the Appalachian mountains in the north to the coastal wetlands in the southeast. The Blue Ridge and northern uplands contain extensive hardwood forests, mountain streams, and cool, forested valleys. Farther south, longleaf pine ecosystems are characteristic of the Coastal Plain, while the state's rivers and wetlands provide important freshwater habitats.

The Okefenokee Swamp is one of Georgia's most distinctive natural areas, covering a vast expanse of freshwater marshes, cypress forests, peatlands, and shallow waterways. Along the Atlantic coast, tidal salt marshes and barrier islands form highly productive habitats for fish, shorebirds, and sea turtles. These coastal ecosystems, together with Georgia's forests and wetlands, support species such as alligators, white-tailed deer, black bears, wild turkeys, and numerous migratory birds.
