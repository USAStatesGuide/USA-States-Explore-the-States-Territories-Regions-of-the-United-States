About New Mexico ▼ +

New Mexico is a state in the southwestern region of the United States. It is bordered by Colorado and Oklahoma to the north and northeast, Texas to the east and southeast, Arizona to the west, and Utah to the northwest. It also shares a short international border with Mexico to the south. The capital of New Mexico is Santa Fe.

New Mexico became the 47th state of the United States on January 6, 1912. The state is known for its deserts, mountains, high plateaus, canyons, forests, and distinctive Southwestern landscapes. Its cultural heritage reflects strong Native American, Hispanic, and Anglo-American influences.

Geography of New Mexico ▼ +

New Mexico has a highly varied landscape shaped by high mountains, elevated plateaus, desert basins, and deep river valleys. Northern New Mexico contains the southern Rocky Mountains, including the Sangre de Cristo Mountains, while the central part of the state is crossed by the Rio Grande Rift, a long geological depression that contains the Rio Grande and several major valleys. Western New Mexico is part of the Colorado Plateau, where mesas, cliffs, and volcanic landscapes create distinctive terrain.

Southern New Mexico is dominated by the Chihuahuan Desert, with broad desert plains, mountain ranges, and isolated basins. The Sacramento and Guadalupe Mountains rise above the surrounding desert in the southeast, while the White Sands area contains vast gypsum dunes that form one of the state's most unusual landscapes. Wheeler Peak, in the Sangre de Cristo Mountains, is New Mexico's highest point at 13,161 feet (4,011 meters). The Rio Grande remains the state's principal river and forms a major geographic corridor from the northern mountains through central New Mexico toward the south.

New Mexico Borders ▼ +

New Mexico shares borders with four U.S. states: Colorado to the north, Oklahoma and Texas to the east, and Arizona to the west. It also borders Utah at the northwest corner.

New Mexico also shares an international border with Mexico along its southern boundary. The border is mainly with the Mexican states of Chihuahua and Sonora.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in New Mexico is Wheeler Peak, which reaches approximately 13,161 feet (4,011 meters) above sea level.

Wheeler Peak is located in the Sangre de Cristo Mountains near Taos and is the highest natural point in New Mexico.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

New Mexico's major rivers include the Rio Grande, Pecos River, Canadian River, San Juan River, and Gila River.

These waterways are important for drinking water, agriculture, ecosystems, recreation, and communities throughout the state. The Rio Grande is particularly important because it flows through central New Mexico and supports many communities and agricultural areas.

Economy of New Mexico ▼ +

New Mexico's economy is strongly shaped by energy production, federal research, defense, technology, agriculture, and tourism. The southeastern part of the state is a major oil and natural gas producing region within the Permian Basin, while solar and wind energy have expanded because of the state's abundant sunshine and open landscapes. Albuquerque and surrounding communities also have important activities in technology, aerospace, electronics, and advanced manufacturing.

Federal research and national-security activities are especially important to New Mexico, with Los Alamos National Laboratory and Sandia National Laboratories supporting scientific research, engineering, and high-skilled employment. Space-related activities include commercial and government operations around southern New Mexico. Agriculture and ranching remain important in rural areas, including cattle, chile peppers, pecans, dairy, and other crops grown with irrigation. Tourism is supported by Santa Fe, Taos, White Sands, historic pueblos, and the state's distinctive Native American, Hispanic, and Southwestern cultural heritage, while film and television production has become another major industry.

Administrative Division ▼ +

New Mexico is divided into 33 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, villages, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Local governments provide various public services to residents throughout New Mexico.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in New Mexico include Albuquerque, Las Cruces, Rio Rancho, Santa Fe, Roswell, and Farmington.

Santa Fe is the state capital and is known for its historic architecture and cultural institutions. Albuquerque is the largest city by population and an important center for business, education, technology, and transportation. Las Cruces is a major center in southern New Mexico, while Roswell and Farmington are important regional communities.

Natural Environment of New Mexico ▼ +

New Mexico's natural environment is defined by strong contrasts in elevation and climate. The Sangre de Cristo and other northern mountain ranges contain conifer forests, aspen groves, alpine meadows, and cold mountain streams, while lower elevations support piñon-juniper woodlands and open grasslands. In southern New Mexico, the Chihuahuan Desert contains dry basins, desert grasslands, shrubs, and mountain ranges that create isolated habitats within the arid landscape.

The Rio Grande forms an important ecological corridor through the state, with cottonwood forests, wetlands, and other riparian habitats along parts of its valley. These environments support species such as elk, mule deer, black bears, pronghorn, and numerous birds and reptiles. New Mexico also contains distinctive volcanic landscapes around the Jemez Mountains and the gypsum dunes of White Sands. Protected areas such as Bandelier National Monument and White Sands National Park preserve some of the state's most distinctive forests, canyons, desert habitats, and geological landscapes.
