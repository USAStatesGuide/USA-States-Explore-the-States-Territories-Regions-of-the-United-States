About Alabama ▼ +

Alabama is a state in the southeastern region of the United States. It is bordered by Tennessee to the north, Georgia to the east, Florida and the Gulf of Mexico to the south, and Mississippi to the west. The capital of Alabama is Montgomery.

Alabama became the 22nd state of the United States on December 14, 1819. The state is known for its forests, mountains, rivers, agricultural lands, coastal areas, and diverse landscapes extending from the Appalachian Highlands to the Gulf Coastal Plain.

Geography of Alabama ▼ +

Alabama stretches from the southern Appalachian foothills to the Gulf of Mexico, giving the state a noticeable change in landscape from north to south. The northeastern part is more elevated and includes Lookout Mountain and other ridges of the Appalachian system. Central Alabama is characterized by rolling hills and the Black Belt, a broad belt of dark, fertile soil that has played an important role in the state's agricultural history.

In the southwest, the Mobile-Tensaw Delta forms one of Alabama's most distinctive natural areas, where several rivers spread through wetlands before reaching Mobile Bay. Farther south, the landscape becomes flatter and includes coastal plains, sandy areas, barrier islands, and beaches along the Gulf of Mexico. This north-to-south transition is one of the defining geographic characteristics of Alabama.

Alabama Borders ▼ +

Alabama shares land borders with four states: Tennessee to the north, Georgia to the east, Florida to the south, and Mississippi to the west.

Alabama also has a coastline along the Gulf of Mexico in the southern part of the state.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Alabama is Cheaha Mountain, which reaches approximately 2,407 feet (734 meters) above sea level.

Cheaha Mountain is located in northeastern Alabama within the southern Appalachian region and is the highest natural point in the state.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Alabama has an extensive network of rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Alabama River, Coosa River, Tallapoosa River, Tennessee River, Tombigbee River, Black Warrior River, and Cahaba River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, transportation, water resources, agriculture, recreation, hydroelectric power, and communities throughout the state.

Economy of Alabama ▼ +

Alabama has a diverse economy with particularly strong ties to automotive manufacturing, aerospace, steel production, agriculture, forestry, and shipping. The state has developed major industrial centers around Birmingham, Huntsville, Mobile, and several cities in the central and northern parts of the state.

Huntsville is an important center for aerospace, space research, and defense, while Mobile's location on the Gulf Coast supports shipbuilding, port activities, and international trade. Alabama is also one of the major automotive manufacturing states in the United States, producing vehicles and automotive components. Agriculture remains important as well, particularly poultry, cattle, cotton, peanuts, soybeans, and timber.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Alabama is divided into 67 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Alabama include Huntsville, Birmingham, Montgomery, Mobile, Tuscaloosa, and Hoover.

Montgomery is the state capital. Huntsville is the largest city by population and an important center for aerospace, engineering, technology, and research. Birmingham is a major economic, medical, educational, and cultural center, while Mobile is an important Gulf Coast port city.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Alabama's natural environment changes considerably from the Appalachian foothills in the north to the Gulf Coast in the south. The state is especially known for its longleaf pine forests, limestone caves, river systems, and the extensive Mobile-Tensaw Delta, one of the largest wetland complexes in Alabama.

The southern wetlands and coastal areas provide important habitats for fish, birds, reptiles, and other wildlife, while the northern forests, mountains, and caves support a different range of species. Alabama's rivers and streams are also notable for their exceptional freshwater biodiversity, including numerous species of fish, mussels, turtles, and other aquatic organisms.
