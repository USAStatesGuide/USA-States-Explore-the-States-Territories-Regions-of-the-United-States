Florida, United States

About Florida ▼ +

Florida is a state in the southeastern region of the United States. It is bordered by Alabama and Georgia to the north, the Atlantic Ocean to the east, the Gulf of Mexico to the west, and the Straits of Florida to the south. The capital of Florida is Tallahassee.

Florida became the 27th state of the United States on March 3, 1845. The state is known for its long coastline, sandy beaches, wetlands, lakes, subtropical and tropical environments, and distinctive peninsula extending between the Atlantic Ocean and the Gulf of Mexico.

Geography of Florida ▼ +

Florida is a long, low-lying peninsula extending between the Gulf of Mexico and the Atlantic Ocean. Most of the state forms part of the Coastal Plain, with gently rolling terrain rather than high mountains. Much of Florida is built on layers of limestone, which have produced distinctive underground caves, sinkholes, springs, and underground drainage systems, particularly in the central and northern parts of the state.

Southern Florida is dominated by the Everglades, a vast subtropical wetland that stretches southward toward Florida Bay. The peninsula narrows toward the south and connects to the Florida Keys, a chain of low islands extending toward the Straits of Florida. Florida also has an extensive coastline shaped by barrier islands, beaches, bays, tidal waterways, and estuaries on both the Atlantic and Gulf coasts.

Florida Borders ▼ +

Florida shares land borders with two U.S. states: Georgia to the north and Alabama to the northwest.

Florida is surrounded by water on three sides. The Atlantic Ocean lies to the east, the Gulf of Mexico lies to the west, and the Straits of Florida lie to the south. The state is also relatively close to Cuba and the Bahamas.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Florida is Britton Hill, which reaches approximately 345 feet (105 meters) above sea level.

Britton Hill is located in Walton County in northwestern Florida, near the Alabama border. It is the lowest highest point of any U.S. state.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Florida has many rivers, springs, lakes, wetlands, and coastal waterways. Major rivers include the St. Johns River, Apalachicola River, Suwannee River, Peace River, and Withlacoochee River.

Florida's waterways are important for drinking water, ecosystems, agriculture, transportation, fishing, recreation, and tourism. The St. Johns River is one of the state's major rivers and flows northward before reaching the Atlantic Ocean near Jacksonville.

Economy of Florida ▼ +

Florida has one of the largest and most diverse state economies in the United States, with tourism, international trade, healthcare, construction, finance, and agriculture playing major roles. Central Florida is a global tourism center, particularly around Orlando, where theme parks, resorts, conventions, and related services attract millions of visitors. South Florida, especially the Miami area, is an important gateway for trade, finance, and business with Latin America and the Caribbean.

Florida's location also supports major port and maritime industries, with large ports handling containerized cargo, automobiles, and cruise passengers. Agriculture is especially associated with citrus, sugarcane, tomatoes, vegetables, and cattle, with large farming areas in central and southern Florida. Along the Space Coast, the Kennedy Space Center and nearby aerospace facilities support space launches, aerospace engineering, and related technology industries, giving the state an economic sector closely linked to the U.S. space program.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Florida is divided into 67 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Florida include Jacksonville, Miami, Tampa, Orlando, St. Petersburg, and Tallahassee.

Jacksonville is the largest city by population and covers a large area in northeastern Florida. Miami is a major international center for finance, trade, tourism, and culture. Orlando is internationally known for tourism and theme parks, while Tampa and St. Petersburg are important Gulf Coast population and economic centers.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Florida's natural environment is strongly shaped by its warm climate, low elevation, and position between the Atlantic Ocean and Gulf of Mexico. Southern Florida contains the Everglades, a vast wetland of sawgrass marshes, shallow waterways, mangrove forests, and freshwater habitats. The Florida Keys support distinctive tropical ecosystems, including seagrass meadows and coral reefs found in the surrounding shallow waters.

Florida's coastal habitats provide important shelter and feeding areas for species such as manatees, American crocodiles, sea turtles, dolphins, and numerous waterbirds. Mangrove forests protect shorelines and provide nursery habitat for fish and other marine life, while the state's springs and freshwater rivers support aquatic ecosystems farther inland. The combination of wetlands, subtropical forests, freshwater springs, and tropical coastal habitats gives Florida an unusually diverse natural environment.
