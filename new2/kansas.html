Kansas, United States

About Kansas ▼ +

Kansas is a state in the Midwestern United States. It is bordered by Nebraska to the north, Missouri to the east, Oklahoma to the south, and Colorado to the west. The capital of Kansas is Topeka, while Wichita is the state's largest city by population.

Kansas became the 34th state of the United States on January 29, 1861. The state is known for its broad prairies, fertile agricultural lands, rolling plains, grasslands, rivers, and important role in U.S. agriculture.

Geography of Kansas ▼ +

Kansas lies largely within the Great Plains and rises gradually from the lower, more humid eastern part of the state toward the higher and drier west. Eastern Kansas has rolling terrain, wooded river valleys, and more precipitation, while central Kansas contains broad agricultural plains and grasslands. Western Kansas forms part of the High Plains, with wide open landscapes, fewer trees, and a generally drier climate.

The Flint Hills are one of Kansas's most distinctive landforms, extending through east-central Kansas with rolling hills underlain by resistant limestone and some of the largest remaining areas of tallgrass prairie in North America. The Smoky Hills in north-central Kansas add another belt of rolling limestone and chalk terrain, including the striking formations of Monument Rocks farther west. Major rivers such as the Kansas, Arkansas, and Republican cross the plains and help shape the state's valleys and agricultural regions.

Kansas Borders ▼ +

Kansas shares land borders with Nebraska to the north, Missouri to the east, Oklahoma to the south, and Colorado to the west.

Kansas is a landlocked state and has no ocean coastline. Its major rivers, including the Kansas and Arkansas rivers, are important geographic features and form part of larger river systems across the Great Plains.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Kansas is Mount Sunflower, which reaches approximately 4,039 feet (1,231 meters) above sea level.

Mount Sunflower is located in Wallace County in western Kansas, near the Colorado border. Despite its name, it is a relatively gentle rise rather than a prominent mountain.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Kansas has an extensive network of rivers and streams. Major waterways include the Kansas River, Arkansas River, Republican River, Smoky Hill River, Saline River, and Neosho River.

These waterways are important for agriculture, drinking water, ecosystems, recreation, wildlife habitats, and flood management. Many rivers in Kansas are part of the larger Missouri River and Mississippi River drainage systems.

Economy of Kansas ▼ +

Kansas has an economy strongly connected to agriculture and the Great Plains. The state is one of the leading U.S. producers of wheat and cattle and also produces sorghum, corn, and soybeans. Livestock production supports meatpacking, animal feed, and food-processing industries, while grain elevators and agricultural businesses connect farms with markets across the country.

Kansas is also notable for its aviation and aerospace industry, particularly around Wichita, which has a long history of producing aircraft and aircraft components. The state has additional manufacturing in machinery, transportation equipment, and food products. Oil and natural gas production remain important, while strong winds across western Kansas have supported rapid growth in wind-energy generation. Major highways and rail routes also make Kansas an important transportation and distribution link across the central United States.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Kansas is divided into 105 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state and perform various administrative and public service functions.

The state also contains cities, townships, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Topeka is the state capital, while Wichita is the largest city by population.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Kansas include Wichita, Overland Park, Kansas City, Olathe, Topeka, and Lawrence.

Wichita is the largest city and an important center for aviation, manufacturing, business, and services. Topeka is the state capital and a center of state government. Overland Park, Olathe, and other communities in the Kansas City metropolitan area are major population and economic centers.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Kansas is strongly associated with its expansive prairie ecosystems, which range from tallgrass prairie in the east to mixed-grass and shortgrass prairie farther west. The Flint Hills contain one of the largest remaining continuous areas of tallgrass prairie in North America, with rolling grass-covered hills and limestone outcrops. Farther west, drier conditions create open grasslands adapted to lower rainfall.

The state's prairie habitats support species such as American bison, pronghorn, white-tailed deer, coyotes, prairie dogs, and numerous grassland birds. Rivers including the Kansas, Arkansas, and Republican, together with reservoirs and wetlands, provide important water and wildlife habitats within the largely open landscape. The eastern part of Kansas contains more wooded areas along rivers and streams, creating a transition between prairie and forest ecosystems.
