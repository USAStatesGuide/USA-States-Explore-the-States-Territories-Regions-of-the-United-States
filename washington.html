About Washington ▼ +

Washington is a state in the Pacific Northwest region of the United States. It is bordered by Canada to the north, Idaho to the east, Oregon to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. The capital of Washington is Olympia.

Washington became the 42nd state of the United States on November 11, 1889. The state is known for its mountains, forests, rivers, lakes, islands, coastal areas, fertile valleys, and diverse landscapes extending from the Pacific coast to the inland regions.

Geography of Washington ▼ +

Washington's geography is strongly divided by the Cascade Range, which separates the wetter landscapes of the west from the drier interior. Western Washington includes the Olympic Peninsula, the Puget Sound lowlands, and the Pacific coast, with rugged mountains, deep bays, islands, and forested valleys. The Olympic Mountains rise on the peninsula, while the Cascade Range contains major volcanic peaks such as Mount Rainier, Mount Baker, and Glacier Peak.

Eastern Washington is dominated by the Columbia Plateau, a broad landscape shaped by ancient volcanic activity and the Columbia River system. The region includes the dry shrub-steppe of the Columbia Basin, the rolling Palouse hills, and fertile irrigated valleys along the Columbia and Yakima rivers. The Columbia River forms an important geographic corridor through the state, while Puget Sound creates a complex network of waterways, islands, and coastal inlets in the west. This sharp contrast between Pacific forests, volcanic mountains, and the dry Columbia Plateau gives Washington a particularly distinctive geography.

Washington Borders ▼ +

Washington shares land borders with Canada to the north, Idaho to the east, and Oregon to the south.

Washington also borders the Pacific Ocean to the west. The state contains extensive coastal areas, bays, islands, and waterways, including Puget Sound.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Washington is Mount Rainier, which reaches approximately 14,411 feet (4,392 meters) above sea level.

Mount Rainier is an active stratovolcano located in the Cascade Range. It is one of the most prominent mountains in the United States and is protected within Mount Rainier National Park.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Washington has an extensive network of rivers, streams, lakes, reservoirs, bays, and coastal waterways. Major rivers include the Columbia River, Snake River, Yakima River, Walla Walla River, Spokane River, and Skagit River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, agriculture, transportation, hydroelectric power, water supply, recreation, wildlife, and communities throughout the state. The Columbia River is particularly important for navigation, irrigation, energy production, and regional commerce.

Economy of Washington ▼ +

Washington has a highly diversified economy with major strengths in technology, aerospace, international trade, agriculture, healthcare, forestry, and maritime industries. The Seattle metropolitan area is a major technology and business center, with important activities in software, cloud computing, online services, and biotechnology. The Puget Sound region also has a long-established aerospace industry, with major aircraft manufacturing and related engineering and supply chains concentrated around Seattle and Everett.

Washington's location on the Pacific gives its ports an important role in international trade, particularly through the Port of Seattle and the Port of Tacoma. Eastern Washington has a strong agricultural economy, producing apples, wheat, potatoes, cherries, hops, wine grapes, and other crops, supported in many areas by irrigation from the Columbia River system. Forestry and wood products remain important in western Washington, while commercial fishing, seafood, hydropower, manufacturing, and tourism further contribute to the state's economy. The Columbia River also supports major transportation and energy activities across the eastern part of the state.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Washington is divided into 39 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Washington include Seattle, Spokane, Tacoma, Vancouver, Bellevue, and Everett.

Olympia is the state capital, while Seattle is the largest city and an important center for technology, business, culture, education, and transportation. Spokane is a major economic center in eastern Washington, while Tacoma, Bellevue, Vancouver, and Everett are important population and economic centers in their respective regions.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Washington's natural environment is strongly shaped by the contrast between the wet Pacific coast and the dry interior east of the Cascades. The Olympic Peninsula contains temperate rainforests, rugged mountains, rivers, and coastal habitats, while the Cascade Range supports extensive conifer forests, alpine meadows, glaciers, and volcanic landscapes. Mount Rainier and other high peaks create important high-elevation ecosystems, while western forests are dominated by species such as Douglas fir, western hemlock, and western red cedar.

Puget Sound forms a complex marine ecosystem of islands, tidal flats, bays, estuaries, and deep waterways that supports salmon, orcas, seals, sea lions, and numerous seabirds. Eastern Washington is much drier and contains shrub-steppe, grasslands, pine forests, and river habitats shaped by the Columbia River and its tributaries. These contrasting environments support wildlife such as elk, black bears, mountain goats, mule deer, beavers, bald eagles, and many migratory birds, while protected areas such as Olympic and Mount Rainier national parks preserve some of the state's most distinctive ecosystems.
