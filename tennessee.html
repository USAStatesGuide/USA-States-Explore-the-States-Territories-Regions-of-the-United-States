About Tennessee ▼ +

Tennessee is a state in the southeastern United States, known for its musical heritage, Appalachian landscapes, historic sites, and major cities such as Nashville and Memphis. The state capital is Nashville, which is also its largest city. Tennessee became the 16th state of the United States on June 1, 1796.

Geography of Tennessee ▼ +

Tennessee stretches for more than 400 miles from the Appalachian Mountains in the east to the Mississippi River in the west, creating a strong geographic transition across the state. Eastern Tennessee contains the Great Smoky Mountains and the Ridge-and-Valley Appalachians, with high ridges, forested slopes, and narrow valleys. Farther west, the Cumberland Plateau rises above the surrounding terrain, while the Highland Rim and Nashville Basin form broad upland and lowland areas around central Tennessee.

Western Tennessee is considerably flatter and includes the fertile West Tennessee Plain, where the Mississippi River forms the state's western boundary. The Tennessee River crosses much of the state and forms several large reservoirs, while the Cumberland River flows through central Tennessee and Nashville. The eastern mountains, central plateaus and basins, and western river lowlands give Tennessee a distinctive sequence of landscapes from rugged Appalachian terrain to broad agricultural plains.

Tennessee Borders ▼ +

Tennessee borders Kentucky and Virginia to the north, North Carolina to the east, Georgia, Alabama, and Mississippi to the south, and Arkansas and Missouri to the west.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Tennessee is Clingmans Dome, located in the Great Smoky Mountains. It reaches approximately 6,643 feet (2,025 meters) above sea level. It is also the highest point in the Great Smoky Mountains National Park.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Tennessee has an extensive network of rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Mississippi River, Tennessee River, Cumberland River, Clinch River, Holston River, Duck River, and French Broad River. These waterways support transportation, agriculture, recreation, wildlife habitats, and hydroelectric power generation.

Economy of Tennessee ▼ +

Tennessee has a diversified economy with major strengths in automobile manufacturing, healthcare, logistics, food processing, chemicals, technology, and entertainment. The state has attracted major automotive plants and suppliers, particularly in central and eastern Tennessee, while Nashville has become a major center for healthcare companies and services. Memphis is one of the country's important freight and distribution hubs because of its location on the Mississippi River and its extensive air, rail, and highway connections.

Music and entertainment are especially important to Tennessee's economy, with Nashville internationally associated with country music and Memphis known for its blues, soul, and rock-and-roll heritage. Agriculture remains significant in rural areas, producing cattle, soybeans, corn, cotton, tobacco, and poultry. Manufacturing also includes machinery, electrical equipment, chemicals, and food products, while tourism generates income through the Great Smoky Mountains, historic music districts, outdoor recreation, and major attractions across the state.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Tennessee is divided into 95 counties. Nashville and Davidson County operate under a consolidated city-county government known as the Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County. Counties provide various local administrative and public services throughout the state.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities in Tennessee include Nashville, Memphis, Knoxville, Chattanooga, Clarksville, Murfreesboro, Franklin, and Jackson. Nashville is the state capital and largest city, while Memphis is a major cultural, transportation, and commercial center in western Tennessee.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Tennessee's natural environment changes significantly from the forested Appalachian Mountains in the east to the river lowlands in the west. The Great Smoky Mountains contain exceptionally diverse temperate forests, high-elevation habitats, mountain streams, and deep valleys, while the Cumberland Plateau is known for sandstone cliffs, waterfalls, caves, and forested uplands. Central Tennessee also contains limestone landscapes with caves, springs, and underground waterways.

The Tennessee and Cumberland river systems create important freshwater habitats, while the Mississippi River and its associated wetlands influence the western edge of the state. Tennessee's forests and waterways support black bears, white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, river otters, beavers, and numerous woodland and water birds. The Great Smoky Mountains are particularly notable for their exceptional plant diversity, while caves, rivers, and wetlands provide additional habitats for fish, amphibians, and other wildlife.
