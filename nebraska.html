About Nebraska ▼ +

Nebraska is a state in the Midwestern region of the United States. It is bordered by South Dakota to the north, Iowa and Missouri to the east, Kansas to the south, and Colorado and Wyoming to the west. The capital of Nebraska is Lincoln.

Nebraska became the 37th state of the United States on March 1, 1867. The state is known for its Great Plains landscapes, fertile agricultural lands, Sandhills, rivers, grasslands, and important agricultural and livestock industries.

Geography of Nebraska ▼ +

Nebraska lies almost entirely within the Great Plains, with a landscape that gradually rises from the wetter eastern lowlands toward the higher and drier High Plains in the west. The Sandhills dominate much of north-central Nebraska, forming one of the largest regions of grass-stabilized sand dunes in North America. Eastern Nebraska has more rolling farmland and loess-covered hills, while the western part contains broad grasslands, buttes, and more open High Plains terrain.

The Platte River crosses the state from west to east and forms an important geographic corridor through central Nebraska, joined by the North Platte and South Platte rivers. The Republican River drains much of southern Nebraska, while the Missouri River forms the state's eastern border. Along the Platte, broad river valleys, wetlands, and sandbars create distinctive landscapes, while the Pine Ridge in northwestern Nebraska rises above the surrounding plains with wooded hills, buttes, and rugged valleys.

Nebraska Borders ▼ +

Nebraska shares land borders with six states: South Dakota to the north, Iowa and Missouri to the east, Kansas to the south, and Colorado and Wyoming to the west.

Nebraska is a landlocked state and has no coastline. The Missouri River forms much of its eastern boundary with Iowa and Missouri.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Nebraska is Panorama Point, which reaches approximately 5,424 feet (1,653 meters) above sea level.

Panorama Point is located in the southwestern part of Nebraska, near the borders with Colorado and Wyoming. It is the highest natural point in the state.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Nebraska has an extensive network of rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Missouri River, Platte River, North Platte River, South Platte River, Niobrara River, Republican River, and Elkhorn River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, agriculture, irrigation, wildlife, recreation, water resources, and communities throughout the state.

Economy of Nebraska ▼ +

Nebraska's economy is closely tied to its productive farmland and large livestock industry. The state is a major producer of corn, soybeans, wheat, and sorghum, while cattle ranching is especially important across the central and western plains. Irrigation plays a major role in agriculture, particularly where groundwater from the Ogallala Aquifer supports crops in the western part of the state. Meatpacking, grain handling, and food processing connect Nebraska's farms and ranches to national markets.

Omaha is the state's largest business center, with important activities in finance, insurance, transportation, telecommunications, and corporate services. Lincoln also has a strong concentration of government, education, healthcare, and technology-related employment. Nebraska's central location and extensive rail and highway networks support freight transportation and distribution, while manufacturing includes agricultural machinery, food products, electrical equipment, and other industrial goods. Renewable energy, particularly wind power, has also expanded across the state's open plains.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Nebraska is divided into 93 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, villages, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Nebraska include Omaha, Lincoln, Bellevue, Grand Island, Kearney, and Fremont.

Lincoln is the state capital and an important center for government, education, and culture. Omaha is the largest city and a major economic, financial, transportation, educational, and cultural center.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Nebraska's natural environment is dominated by grasslands, with the Sandhills forming one of its most distinctive ecosystems. This vast region of grass-covered sand dunes contains wetlands, shallow lakes, and extensive native prairie that provide habitat for many grassland species. The Platte River and its surrounding wetlands form another important ecosystem, while the Pine Ridge in northwestern Nebraska contains wooded valleys, rugged hills, and mixed grassland habitats.

Nebraska's rivers and wetlands are especially important for migratory birds, with the Platte River serving as a major stopover during spring migration for species such as sandhill cranes and other waterfowl. Native grasslands support pronghorn, white-tailed deer, coyotes, prairie dogs, and numerous prairie birds, while river corridors provide habitat for beavers, river otters, fish, and amphibians. The Sandhills also play an important role in storing and slowly releasing groundwater into the underlying aquifer system.
