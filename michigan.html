Michigan, United States

About Michigan ▼ +

Michigan is a state in the Great Lakes region of the north-central United States. It is bordered by Wisconsin and Lake Michigan to the west, Indiana and Ohio to the south, and has international water boundaries with Minnesota, Wisconsin, Illinois, Indiana, and Ohio across the Great Lakes. The capital of Michigan is Lansing.

Michigan became the 26th state of the United States on January 26, 1837. The state is known for its two major peninsulas, extensive Great Lakes shorelines, forests, inland lakes, rivers, agricultural lands, and diverse landscapes.

Geography of Michigan ▼ +

Michigan is divided into two major peninsulas separated by the Straits of Mackinac. The Lower Peninsula has a distinctive mitten-like shape and contains much of the state's population, farmland, and major cities. The Upper Peninsula is more rugged and sparsely populated, with forested hills, rocky shorelines, waterfalls, and extensive wilderness. The Keweenaw Peninsula extends into Lake Superior and is one of the state's most distinctive geographic features.

Michigan has more than 3,000 miles of Great Lakes shoreline and borders Lake Superior, Lake Michigan, Lake Huron, and Lake Erie, giving it an unusually strong connection to freshwater geography. The Great Lakes have shaped its sandy beaches, coastal dunes, bays, and wetlands, while the Straits of Mackinac form a major water passage between Lake Michigan and Lake Huron. Inland, Michigan contains thousands of lakes and numerous rivers, including the Au Sable, Manistee, Muskegon, and Grand rivers.

Michigan Borders ▼ +

Michigan shares land borders with Ohio and Indiana to the south and Wisconsin to the west. Michigan also has water boundaries across the Great Lakes with several U.S. states and the Canadian province of Ontario.

Michigan has extensive shorelines along Lake Superior, Lake Michigan, Lake Huron, and Lake Erie. Its geographic position gives the state one of the longest freshwater coastlines in the United States.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Michigan is Mount Arvon, which reaches approximately 1,979 feet (603 meters) above sea level.

Mount Arvon is located in Baraga County in the Upper Peninsula. It is the highest natural point in the state.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Michigan has an extensive network of rivers, streams, inland lakes, and Great Lakes shorelines. Major rivers include the Grand River, Au Sable River, Manistee River, Muskegon River, St. Joseph River, and Menominee River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, drinking water, transportation, fisheries, recreation, tourism, agriculture, hydroelectric power, and communities throughout the state.

Economy of Michigan ▼ +

Michigan is closely associated with the American automotive industry, which developed around Detroit and remains an important part of the state's manufacturing base. Automobiles, engines, vehicle components, machinery, and other advanced manufactured products are produced across the state. Michigan also has major research and engineering activities related to transportation, including electric vehicles and automotive technology. Healthcare, higher education, and technology are additional important parts of the state's economy.

Agriculture is especially important in the Lower Peninsula, producing apples, cherries, blueberries, asparagus, corn, soybeans, dairy products, and other crops. Michigan's extensive forests support lumber and wood products, while the Upper Peninsula has a long history of iron ore and other mineral extraction. The Great Lakes also support shipping, commercial fishing, tourism, and recreation, with destinations such as the Sleeping Bear Dunes, Mackinac Island, and the state's many lakes and shoreline communities attracting visitors.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Michigan is divided into 83 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, villages, townships, municipalities, and other local communities. Counties, townships, and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Michigan include Detroit, Grand Rapids, Warren, Sterling Heights, Ann Arbor, and Lansing.

Lansing is the state capital. Detroit is the largest city by population and an important center for automotive manufacturing, business, culture, sports, and transportation. Grand Rapids is a major regional center for manufacturing, healthcare, education, and business, while Ann Arbor is widely known for higher education, research, and technology.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Michigan's natural environment is strongly shaped by the Great Lakes and its extensive forests. The Upper Peninsula and northern Lower Peninsula contain large areas of northern hardwood and coniferous forests, with wetlands, streams, inland lakes, and rugged shorelines. Along Lake Michigan and Lake Superior, glacial deposits have created prominent sand dunes and coastal habitats, including the large dune systems protected at Sleeping Bear Dunes and Pictured Rocks.

The Great Lakes and Michigan's many inland waterways support diverse freshwater ecosystems containing fish, waterfowl, amphibians, and aquatic plants. Coastal wetlands and river mouths provide important habitat for migratory birds and other wildlife, while forests support white-tailed deer, black bears, moose, beavers, and wild turkeys. Michigan's islands, forests, dunes, wetlands, and four Great Lakes create a distinctive combination of freshwater and terrestrial habitats.
