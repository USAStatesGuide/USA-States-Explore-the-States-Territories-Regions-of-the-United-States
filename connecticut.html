About Connecticut ▼ +

Connecticut is a state in the New England region of the northeastern United States. It is bordered by Massachusetts to the north, Rhode Island to the east, New York to the west, and Long Island Sound to the south. The capital of Connecticut is Hartford.

Connecticut became the 5th state of the United States on January 9, 1788. The state is known for its historic towns, coastal areas, forests, rivers, rolling hills, and long maritime tradition. It is one of the six New England states.

Geography of Connecticut ▼ +

Connecticut lies between the uplands of western New England and the coastal lowlands of Long Island Sound. Its western landscape includes the Taconic Mountains and the hills of the Litchfield region, while the central Connecticut River Valley forms a broad corridor of lower, more fertile land. The state also contains forests, small lakes, wetlands, and rolling countryside shaped by its glacial past.

The Connecticut River is the state's principal waterway and runs southward through the center of the state before entering Long Island Sound. Along the southern coast, the landscape changes to salt marshes, tidal rivers, beaches, rocky shores, bays, and offshore islands. Long Island Sound gives Connecticut a distinctive coastal geography and has historically supported ports and communities such as New Haven, Bridgeport, and Mystic along its shoreline.

Connecticut Borders ▼ +

Connecticut shares land borders with three states: Massachusetts to the north, Rhode Island to the east, and New York to the west.

The southern boundary of Connecticut is formed largely by Long Island Sound, which connects the state to the Atlantic Ocean. The coastline has numerous bays, harbors, beaches, and coastal communities.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Connecticut is Mount Frissell, which reaches approximately 2,380 feet (725 meters) above sea level.

The highest elevation is located on the southwestern border of Connecticut with Massachusetts. The summit of Mount Frissell itself is in Massachusetts, while Connecticut's highest point is on the southern slope of the mountain.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Connecticut has an extensive network of rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Connecticut River, Housatonic River, Thames River, Farmington River, and Quinnipiac River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, drinking water, agriculture, recreation, transportation, and local communities. The Connecticut River is especially significant because it flows through the center of the state and empties into Long Island Sound.

Economy of Connecticut ▼ +

Connecticut has a highly developed economy with particular strengths in insurance, aerospace, precision manufacturing, finance, healthcare, and professional services. Hartford is widely associated with the insurance industry, while the Hartford–East Hartford area has a major concentration of aerospace manufacturing and engineering, including the production of aircraft engines and components.

Advanced manufacturing remains an important part of the state's industrial identity, with companies producing aircraft systems, precision machinery, medical equipment, and other specialized products. Coastal cities such as New Haven, Bridgeport, and New London support port activity, marine industries, and trade. Yale University and other higher education and research institutions also contribute to Connecticut's workforce, innovation, healthcare, and services economy.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Connecticut is divided into 8 counties. The state also uses planning regions for regional governmental and administrative purposes.

Connecticut contains cities, towns, boroughs, villages, and other local communities. Unlike many states, Connecticut abolished county governments in 1960, while its counties continue to serve mainly as geographic and statistical divisions.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Connecticut include Bridgeport, New Haven, Stamford, Hartford, Waterbury, and Norwalk.

Hartford is the state capital and an important center for insurance, government, and business. Bridgeport is the largest city by population, while New Haven is a major educational and cultural center. Stamford is an important business and financial center in southwestern Connecticut.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Connecticut's natural environment is dominated by temperate forests, especially forests of oak, maple, birch, and other deciduous trees. The Connecticut River Valley contains floodplain forests, wetlands, and freshwater habitats, while the western uplands support extensive wooded hills and protected natural areas. Many of these landscapes were shaped by glaciers that left behind ponds, lakes, and distinctive rocky terrain.

Along Long Island Sound, salt marshes, tidal creeks, estuaries, beaches, and rocky shores provide important habitat for fish, shellfish, shorebirds, and migratory birds. The state's rivers and wetlands also support turtles, frogs, waterfowl, and other freshwater species. Connecticut's coastal and inland ecosystems are closely connected, with tidal waters, river corridors, and forests forming a network of habitats across the state.
