About West Virginia ▼ +

West Virginia is a state in the Appalachian region of the eastern United States. It is bordered by Pennsylvania and Maryland to the northeast and east, Virginia to the southeast, Kentucky to the southwest, and Ohio to the northwest. The capital of West Virginia is Charleston.

West Virginia became the 35th state of the United States on June 20, 1863. The state is known for its Appalachian mountains, forests, valleys, rivers, rugged landscapes, coalfields, and historic communities.

Geography of West Virginia ▼ +

West Virginia is almost entirely mountainous and is characterized by deeply dissected Appalachian terrain rather than broad flat plains. The Allegheny Plateau covers much of the western and central part of the state, where rivers have carved steep valleys, wooded ridges, and narrow gorges. The eastern mountains include the Allegheny Mountains and Ridge-and-Valley Appalachians, while the Eastern Panhandle contains lower valleys and ridges along the Potomac River.

The New River has carved one of the state's most distinctive landscapes, creating a deep gorge surrounded by steep forested cliffs and rugged hills. The Monongahela and Kanawha rivers form important valleys through the mountainous interior, while the Ohio River borders West Virginia along much of the west and northwest. In the northeast, the state's highest elevations are found around Spruce Knob and the surrounding Allegheny highlands. This combination of plateaus, narrow valleys, deep river gorges, and forested Appalachian ridges gives West Virginia its distinctive mountainous geography.

West Virginia Borders ▼ +

West Virginia shares borders with Pennsylvania to the north, Maryland to the northeast and east, Virginia to the southeast, Kentucky to the southwest, and Ohio to the northwest.

The Ohio River forms a substantial portion of West Virginia's western and northwestern boundary. The state has no ocean coastline.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in West Virginia is Spruce Knob, which reaches approximately 4,863 feet (1,482 meters) above sea level.

Spruce Knob is located in Pendleton County in the Allegheny Mountains. It is the highest natural point in West Virginia and is part of the Monongahela National Forest.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

West Virginia has an extensive network of rivers and streams. Major waterways include the Ohio River, Kanawha River, Monongahela River, Potomac River, Greenbrier River, and New River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, transportation, industry, water resources, recreation, wildlife, and communities throughout the state. The Ohio and Kanawha rivers have historically played important roles in commerce and transportation.

Economy of West Virginia ▼ +

West Virginia's economy has long been shaped by its Appalachian natural resources, particularly coal, natural gas, timber, and minerals. Coal mining remains an important industry in the southern and central Appalachian coalfields, although its role has declined from historical levels. Natural gas production has expanded in the northwestern part of the state as part of the Marcellus and Utica shale regions. Chemical manufacturing is also important, especially along the Kanawha River Valley, where the Charleston area developed a major industrial and chemical corridor.

Manufacturing, energy-related industries, healthcare, and education provide major sources of employment, particularly around Charleston, Huntington, Morgantown, and other regional centers. The Ohio, Kanawha, and Monongahela rivers support transportation and industrial activity, while agriculture and forestry remain important in rural areas. Tourism and outdoor recreation have grown around destinations such as New River Gorge National Park and Preserve, the Monongahela National Forest, and the state's rivers, mountains, and recreational areas. Whitewater rafting, hiking, skiing, and other outdoor activities contribute to the economies of many Appalachian communities.

Administrative Division ▼ +

West Virginia is divided into 55 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, villages, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in West Virginia include Charleston, Huntington, Morgantown, Parkersburg, Wheeling, and Martinsburg.

Charleston is the state capital and an important center for government, business, healthcare, education, and culture. Huntington is a major population and economic center in the western part of the state, while Morgantown is an important educational, healthcare, and research center.

Natural Environment ▼ +

West Virginia is dominated by Appalachian forests, steep ridges, narrow valleys, and mountain streams. The Monongahela National Forest contains extensive hardwood forests, high-elevation spruce habitats, wetlands, and cold mountain waterways, while the New River Gorge contains steep forested cliffs, rocky outcrops, and river ecosystems. In areas of limestone and other soluble rock, caves, sinkholes, and underground drainage create distinctive karst environments.

The state's rivers and streams form important freshwater habitats, particularly along the New, Gauley, Cheat, Monongahela, and Kanawha rivers. These waterways support fish, amphibians, and other aquatic species, while the surrounding forests provide habitat for black bears, white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, bobcats, and numerous woodland birds. West Virginia's protected forests, mountain wetlands, and river valleys also support recreation and biodiversity, with hiking, fishing, whitewater rafting, and other outdoor activities closely connected to the state's natural environment.
