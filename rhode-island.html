Rhode Island, United States

About Rhode Island ▼ +

Rhode Island is a state in the New England region of the northeastern United States. It is bordered by Connecticut to the west, Massachusetts to the north and east, and Block Island Sound and the Atlantic Ocean to the south. The capital of Rhode Island is Providence.

Rhode Island became the 13th state of the United States on May 29, 1790. The state is known for its coastal landscapes, Narragansett Bay, islands, beaches, historic towns, forests, and maritime heritage.

Geography of Rhode Island ▼ +

Rhode Island is the smallest U.S. state by area and is dominated by a highly indented coastline and the waters of Narragansett Bay. The western mainland contains gently rolling terrain, forests, ponds, and small river valleys, while the eastern part is shaped by the bay and its surrounding coastal lowlands. Narragansett Bay extends far inland and separates several important coastal areas, giving the state a landscape closely connected to the sea.

Rhode Island includes several significant islands, most notably Aquidneck Island, Conanicut Island, and Block Island. Aquidneck Island contains Newport and has a varied coastline of rocky shores, beaches, coves, and harbors, while Block Island lies offshore in the Atlantic and contains sandy beaches, dunes, ponds, and coastal wetlands. The state's many bays, inlets, tidal rivers, and sheltered harbors create an unusually extensive coastline for such a small state and have strongly influenced its settlement and maritime history.

Rhode Island Borders ▼ +

Rhode Island shares land borders with two states: Connecticut to the west and Massachusetts to the north and east.

Rhode Island also has extensive coastal boundaries along Narragansett Bay, Block Island Sound, and the Atlantic Ocean.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Rhode Island is Jerimoth Hill, which reaches approximately 812 feet (247 meters) above sea level.

Jerimoth Hill is located in Foster in Providence County, in the northwestern part of the state. It is the highest natural point in Rhode Island.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Rhode Island has numerous rivers, streams, bays, and coastal waterways. Major rivers include the Blackstone River, Woonasquatucket River, Seekonk River, Pawtuxet River, and Pawcatuck River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, drinking water, recreation, fishing, transportation, industry, and coastal communities. Narragansett Bay is particularly important to the state's environment and maritime economy.

Economy of Rhode Island ▼ +

Rhode Island's economy is closely connected to healthcare, education, marine industries, tourism, professional services, manufacturing, and technology. Providence is the state's main economic center, with major activities in healthcare, higher education, finance, business services, and technology. The state's long maritime tradition also supports boatbuilding, marine engineering, fishing, seafood processing, and other businesses linked to Narragansett Bay and the Atlantic Ocean.

Tourism is especially important along the coast, with Newport known for its historic waterfront, sailing culture, and Gilded Age mansions, while Block Island attracts visitors to its beaches, dunes, and coastal landscapes. Commercial fishing and aquaculture contribute to the state's seafood industry, including products such as lobster, clams, and oysters. Manufacturing remains present in areas such as precision products, jewelry, and specialized equipment, while universities, hospitals, and research institutions provide additional high-skilled employment across the state.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Rhode Island is divided into 5 counties. Counties are an important geographic level within the state, although Rhode Island's counties do not function as local governments.

The state also contains cities, towns, villages, municipalities, and unincorporated communities. Cities and towns provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Rhode Island include Providence, Warwick, Cranston, Pawtucket, East Providence, and Woonsocket.

Providence is the state capital and largest city by population. It is an important center for education, healthcare, business, culture, and government. Warwick is a major suburban and transportation center, while Cranston, Pawtucket, East Providence, and Woonsocket are important population and economic centers.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Rhode Island's natural environment is strongly influenced by Narragansett Bay and its extensive Atlantic coastline. The bay contains tidal flats, salt marshes, rocky shores, coves, and shallow estuaries that provide important nursery habitats for fish and shellfish. Along the outer coast, sandy beaches, dunes, coastal ponds, and maritime vegetation are especially characteristic of Block Island and other exposed coastal areas.

Rhode Island's inland areas contain forests, freshwater ponds, streams, and wetlands that contrast with the state's heavily marine coastal environment. The coastal waters support species such as striped bass, bluefish, shellfish, seals, and numerous seabirds, while salt marshes provide feeding and nesting areas for shorebirds and migratory species. Inland forests and wetlands support white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, beavers, turtles, frogs, and many woodland birds, creating diverse habitats within the state's small area.
