About Nevada ▼ +

Nevada is a state in the western region of the United States. It is bordered by Oregon and Idaho to the north, Utah to the east, Arizona to the southeast, and California to the west and southwest. The capital of Nevada is Carson City.

Nevada became the 36th state of the United States on October 31, 1864. The state is known for its desert landscapes, mountain ranges, valleys, dry lakes, and distinctive Great Basin geography, as well as the Las Vegas metropolitan area.

Geography of Nevada ▼ +

Nevada is dominated by the Basin and Range landscape of the Great Basin, where numerous mountain ranges run roughly north to south and are separated by broad, dry valleys. The central and northern parts of the state contain some of the most extensive examples of this rugged terrain, with isolated ranges rising above desert basins. Western Nevada reaches the eastern slopes of the Sierra Nevada, including the high mountain country around Lake Tahoe, while the southern part of the state extends into the Mojave Desert.

Nevada has no ocean coastline and is one of the driest states in the United States. Much of its water drains into closed Great Basin basins rather than reaching the sea, creating distinctive dry lakes and salt flats. The Colorado River forms part of the southeastern boundary and supplies Lake Mead, while the Humboldt River crosses northern Nevada before ending in the Great Basin. Other notable landscapes include the Black Rock Desert, Great Basin National Park, and the high Snake Range in the east.

Nevada Borders ▼ +

Nevada shares land borders with five states: Oregon and Idaho to the north, Utah to the east, Arizona to the southeast, and California to the west and southwest.

Nevada has no ocean coastline because it is a landlocked state.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Nevada is Boundary Peak, which reaches approximately 13,140 feet (4,006 meters) above sea level.

Boundary Peak is located in the White Mountains near Nevada's western border with California and is the highest natural point in the state.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Nevada has relatively few permanent rivers because of its arid climate. Important waterways include the Humboldt River, Truckee River, Carson River, and Virgin River. Major lakes include Lake Tahoe, Pyramid Lake, and Lake Mead.

These waterways and lakes are important for water resources, ecosystems, agriculture, recreation, tourism, and communities throughout the state.

Economy of Nevada ▼ +

Nevada's economy is strongly shaped by tourism, gaming, entertainment, mining, and services. Las Vegas is the state's dominant tourism center, with casinos, hotels, conventions, restaurants, live entertainment, and large-scale events generating extensive economic activity. Reno is another important tourism and business center, while northern Nevada has attracted manufacturing, distribution, and technology-related investment. Gold mining is also a major industry, making Nevada one of the leading gold-producing regions in the United States.

Nevada's location in the western United States supports logistics, warehousing, and distribution along major transportation routes connecting California with the interior West. The state's abundant sunshine and open desert landscapes have also encouraged large-scale solar energy development. Construction, healthcare, manufacturing, and technology contribute to the growing urban economies of Las Vegas and Reno, while agriculture is concentrated mainly in irrigated valleys where cattle, hay, and other crops can be produced.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Nevada is divided into 16 counties and 1 independent city. Counties and the independent city of Carson City form an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Nevada include Las Vegas, Henderson, Reno, North Las Vegas, Sparks, and Carson City.

Carson City is the state capital. Las Vegas is the largest city by population and a major center for tourism and entertainment. Reno is an important regional center for technology, tourism, and commerce, while Henderson and North Las Vegas are major population centers in the Las Vegas Valley.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Nevada's natural environment is dominated by the dry landscapes of the Great Basin and Mojave Desert, but its mountain ranges create cooler and wetter habitats at higher elevations. The Great Basin contains extensive sagebrush ecosystems, salt flats, dry valleys, and isolated mountain ranges, while higher slopes support pinyon-juniper woodlands and, in some ranges, forests of ponderosa pine, fir, and bristlecone pine. The Spring Mountains near Las Vegas are particularly distinctive because their elevation creates habitats very different from the surrounding Mojave Desert.

Nevada's limited lakes and wetlands are especially important for wildlife. Pyramid Lake supports native Lahontan cutthroat trout and Cui-ui fish, while Lake Tahoe contains a deep, clear freshwater ecosystem surrounded by the Sierra Nevada. Desert habitats support species such as desert bighorn sheep, pronghorn, coyotes, and reptiles adapted to arid conditions, while mountain areas provide habitat for mule deer, black bears, and numerous birds. Great Basin National Park protects distinctive high-elevation forests, caves, and mountain ecosystems.
