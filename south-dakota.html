About South Dakota ▼ +

South Dakota is a state in the north-central United States, located in the Great Plains region. It is known for Mount Rushmore, the Black Hills, Badlands National Park, and its wide open landscapes. The capital is Pierre, while Sioux Falls is the largest city by population. South Dakota became the 40th state of the United States on November 2, 1889.

Geography of South Dakota ▼ +

South Dakota lies within the Great Plains, but its landscape varies considerably from east to west. Eastern South Dakota contains fertile plains, rolling prairies, and the broad Coteau des Prairies, while the central part of the state is shaped by the Missouri River and its wide valleys and reservoirs. Western South Dakota rises toward the Black Hills, an isolated mountain region of forested ridges, granite formations, and narrow canyons that stands prominently above the surrounding plains.

The southwestern part of the state contains the Badlands, where the White River has carved deeply eroded cliffs, buttes, pinnacles, and colorful rock formations into the prairie. The Missouri River divides the state geographically and has created important valleys and reservoirs, including Lake Oahe and Lake Sharpe. Other major rivers include the Cheyenne, James, and Big Sioux, while the Black Hills contain higher elevations, forests, and streams that contrast sharply with the surrounding grasslands.

South Dakota Borders ▼ +

South Dakota borders North Dakota to the north, Minnesota and Iowa to the east, Nebraska to the south, and Wyoming and Montana to the west.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in South Dakota is Black Elk Peak, located in the Black Hills. It reaches approximately 7,244 feet (2,208 meters) above sea level. It is the highest natural point in South Dakota and one of the highest points in the Great Plains region.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

South Dakota has several important rivers and waterways. The Missouri River is the state's major river and forms a significant part of its central and western geography. Other important waterways include the Cheyenne River, James River, Big Sioux River, Moreau River, White River, and Vermillion River.

Economy of South Dakota ▼ +

South Dakota's economy is strongly connected to agriculture, tourism, financial services, manufacturing, healthcare, and natural resources. Cattle ranching is particularly important across the western plains, while eastern South Dakota produces large quantities of corn, soybeans, wheat, and other crops. Agriculture also supports extensive livestock operations, grain handling, meat processing, and food-related industries.

Tourism is a major part of the state's economy, especially in the Black Hills, where Mount Rushmore, Custer State Park, and Badlands National Park attract visitors. Mining has a long history in the Black Hills, particularly gold production, although its role is smaller today than in the past. Sioux Falls is the state's main commercial center, with important activities in finance, healthcare, retail, and business services, while manufacturing, transportation, and renewable energy add further diversity to the economy.

Administrative Division ▼ +

South Dakota is divided into 66 counties. Pierre is the state capital and the seat of Hughes County. Counties provide local government services and administrative functions throughout the state.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities in South Dakota include Sioux Falls, Rapid City, Aberdeen, Brookings, Watertown, Mitchell, Yankton, and Pierre. Sioux Falls is the largest city and a major economic and commercial center in the eastern part of the state.

Natural Environment ▼ +

South Dakota's natural environment changes from the open prairies of the Great Plains to the forested mountains of the Black Hills. The Black Hills contain ponderosa pine forests, rocky ridges, streams, and mountain habitats, while the surrounding plains are dominated by mixed-grass and shortgrass prairie. In the southwest, Badlands National Park contains a distinctive dry landscape of eroded cliffs, buttes, pinnacles, and grasslands formed by wind and water erosion.

The Missouri River and its reservoirs provide important freshwater habitats, while prairie wetlands and shallow lakes across the eastern part of the state support waterfowl and migratory birds. South Dakota's grasslands provide habitat for bison, pronghorn, prairie dogs, coyotes, and many grassland birds, while the Black Hills support elk, mule deer, white-tailed deer, mountain goats, and other wildlife. Custer State Park is particularly known for its large bison herd and diverse combination of forests, grasslands, and rocky terrain.
