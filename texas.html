Texas, United States

About Texas ▼ +

Texas is a state in the south-central region of the United States. It is bordered by Oklahoma to the north, Arkansas and Louisiana to the east, New Mexico to the west, and Mexico to the south. Texas also has a coastline along the Gulf of Mexico. The capital of Texas is Austin.

Texas became the 28th state of the United States on December 29, 1845. The state is known for its large size, diverse landscapes, major cities, ranching traditions, energy resources, agriculture, technology, manufacturing, and extensive Gulf Coast.

Geography of Texas ▼ +

Texas has one of the most varied landscapes in the United States, with a broad geographic transition from the arid mountains and deserts of the west to the forests and coastal plains of the east and southeast. The Trans-Pecos region contains the Chihuahuan Desert and the state's highest mountains, including the Guadalupe Mountains. Farther east, the High Plains and Llano Estacado form broad, elevated grasslands, while the Edwards Plateau and Texas Hill Country are characterized by limestone hills, springs, canyons, and winding rivers.

Eastern Texas contains the Piney Woods, an extensive forested region of pine and hardwoods, while the Gulf Coastal Plain stretches along the southern and southeastern coast. The Gulf shoreline includes barrier islands, bays, estuaries, coastal marshes, and the distinctive Laguna Madre. Major rivers such as the Rio Grande, Red, Brazos, Colorado, and Trinity cross different regions of the state, creating important valleys and transportation corridors. This combination of desert mountains, high plains, limestone landscapes, forests, and Gulf Coast environments gives Texas exceptional geographic variety.

Texas Borders ▼ +

Texas shares land borders with four U.S. states: Oklahoma to the north, Arkansas to the northeast, Louisiana to the east, and New Mexico to the west.

Texas also shares an international border with Mexico to the south and southwest. The state has a coastline along the Gulf of Mexico to the southeast.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Texas is Guadalupe Peak, which reaches approximately 8,751 feet (2,667 meters) above sea level.

Guadalupe Peak is located in western Texas within Guadalupe Mountains National Park. It is the highest natural point in the state and forms part of the Guadalupe Mountains.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Texas has an extensive network of rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Rio Grande, Red River, Brazos River, Colorado River, Trinity River, Sabine River, and Neches River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, water resources, agriculture, recreation, transportation, reservoirs, and communities throughout the state. The Rio Grande also forms much of the international boundary between Texas and Mexico.

Economy of Texas ▼ +

Texas has one of the largest and most diversified state economies in the United States, with energy remaining one of its defining industries. The Permian Basin in West Texas is one of the country's major oil and natural gas producing regions, while Houston is a leading center for energy companies, refining, petrochemicals, and related engineering services. Texas is also a major producer of wind and solar power, and its large industrial base includes electronics, machinery, chemicals, transportation equipment, and semiconductor manufacturing.

The state's major metropolitan areas have developed distinctive economic roles. Austin is an important technology and semiconductor center, while Dallas–Fort Worth has major activities in technology, finance, telecommunications, aviation, and business services. Houston also has important aerospace and medical industries. Agriculture and ranching remain significant, particularly cattle, cotton, sorghum, corn, wheat, and other crops. Gulf Coast ports, including the Port of Houston, support extensive international trade, while aerospace, construction, healthcare, tourism, and logistics further strengthen the state's economy.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Texas is divided into 254 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Texas include Houston, San Antonio, Dallas, Austin, Fort Worth, and El Paso.

Austin is the state capital and an important center for government, technology, education, and culture. Houston is the largest city by population and a major center for energy, healthcare, aerospace, and international trade. Dallas and Fort Worth form a major economic and transportation region, while San Antonio is an important cultural, military, tourism, and economic center.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Texas contains several distinctive ecosystems because of its enormous size and strong climatic contrasts. The Trans-Pecos region includes the Chihuahuan Desert, rugged mountains, deep canyons, and the Rio Grande, while the Edwards Plateau supports limestone hills, springs, caves, and open grasslands. Central Texas also contains the oak and juniper landscapes of the Hill Country, whereas the Piney Woods in the east are dominated by pine and hardwood forests.

Along the Gulf Coast, barrier islands, coastal prairies, salt marshes, bays, and estuaries provide important habitats for fish, shrimp, sea turtles, shorebirds, and waterfowl. Texas's rivers, wetlands, and reservoirs support diverse freshwater ecosystems, while its plains and grasslands provide habitat for white-tailed deer, pronghorn, coyotes, armadillos, and many grassland birds. The Big Bend region is particularly distinctive for its combination of desert, mountain, and river environments, supporting species such as desert bighorn sheep, black bears, mountain lions, and numerous reptiles and birds.
