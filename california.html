California, United States

About California ▼ +

California is a state on the western coast of the United States. It is bordered by Oregon to the north, Nevada and Arizona to the east, and Mexico to the south. The Pacific Ocean forms its western boundary. The capital of California is Sacramento.

California became the 31st state of the United States on September 9, 1850. It is known for its long Pacific coastline, mountains, valleys, deserts, forests, agricultural regions, and large metropolitan areas. Its varied geography includes the Sierra Nevada, Central Valley, and Mojave Desert.

Geography of California ▼ +

California has an exceptionally diverse geography shaped by several major landforms and active geological processes. The Sierra Nevada rises along the eastern part of the state and includes Mount Whitney, the highest point in the contiguous United States. Between the Sierra Nevada and the Coast Ranges lies the broad Central Valley, one of the country's most important agricultural regions. The state's landscape is also influenced by the San Andreas Fault, a major fault system associated with the boundary between the Pacific and North American tectonic plates.

Along the west, California has a long Pacific coastline with beaches, cliffs, bays, wetlands, and coastal mountains. Farther inland, the landscape changes dramatically into the Mojave and Colorado deserts, while Death Valley in eastern California contains the lowest point in North America. The combination of high mountain ranges, a broad agricultural valley, active fault zones, and extensive desert and coastal landscapes makes California one of the most geographically diverse states in the United States.

California Borders ▼ +

California shares land borders with three U.S. states: Oregon to the north, Nevada to the east, and Arizona to the southeast.

California also shares an international border with Mexico to the south. The Pacific Ocean forms the state's western boundary and is an important part of its geography, economy, climate, and ecosystems.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in California is Mount Whitney, which reaches approximately 14,505 feet (4,421 meters) above sea level.

Mount Whitney is located in the Sierra Nevada in eastern California. It is the highest point in the contiguous United States and is part of the Sequoia National Park region.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

California has many important rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Sacramento River, San Joaquin River, American River, Russian River, and Los Angeles River.

The Sacramento and San Joaquin river systems are especially important because they drain much of the Central Valley. California's rivers and reservoirs provide water for cities, agriculture, ecosystems, hydroelectric power, and recreation.

Economy of California ▼ +

California has one of the most diversified economies in the United States, with major strengths in technology, entertainment, agriculture, international trade, aerospace, manufacturing, finance, healthcare, and professional services. The San Francisco Bay Area, particularly Silicon Valley, is a major global center for technology, software, semiconductors, and innovation, while the Los Angeles region is internationally known for film and television production.

Agriculture is another distinctive part of California's economy. The Central Valley produces large quantities of fruits, vegetables, nuts, dairy products, and other crops, supported by extensive irrigation systems. The state's Pacific ports, including the Port of Los Angeles and the Port of Long Beach, handle large volumes of international trade. Aerospace and defense, biotechnology, tourism, and renewable energy also contribute significantly to California's broad economic base.

Administrative Division ▼ +

California is divided into 58 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in California include Los Angeles, San Diego, San Jose, San Francisco, Fresno, and Sacramento.

Los Angeles is the largest city by population and a major center for entertainment, culture, business, and international trade. San Francisco and San Jose are important technology and economic centers, while Sacramento is the state capital. San Diego is a major coastal city and economic center in Southern California.

Natural Environment ▼ +

California is known for several distinctive ecosystems, from the giant sequoia forests of the Sierra Nevada and the coastal redwood forests in the north to the dry landscapes of the Mojave and Colorado Deserts. The Sierra Nevada also contains alpine environments, while the Central Valley supports extensive wetlands, grasslands, and river habitats shaped by the Sacramento and San Joaquin rivers.

The state's coastline contains beaches, rocky shores, estuaries, and kelp forests that support sea otters, seals, sea lions, whales, and many seabirds. California's interior habitats support species such as black bears, mule deer, mountain lions, and desert bighorn sheep. Protected areas such as Yosemite, Sequoia, Kings Canyon, and Redwood National and State Parks preserve some of the state's most distinctive forests, mountains, valleys, and wildlife habitats.
