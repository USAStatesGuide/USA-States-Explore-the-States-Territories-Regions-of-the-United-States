Wisconsin, United States

About Wisconsin ▼ +

Wisconsin is a state in the north-central region of the United States, in the Upper Midwest. It is bordered by Lake Superior and Michigan to the north, Michigan and Lake Michigan to the east, Illinois to the south, and Iowa and Minnesota to the west. The capital of Wisconsin is Madison.

Wisconsin became the 30th state of the United States on May 29, 1848. The state is known for its Great Lakes shoreline, forests, farmland, rivers, lakes, rolling hills, dairy farms, and diverse landscapes.

Geography of Wisconsin ▼ +

Wisconsin's landscape is strongly influenced by glaciers, the Great Lakes, and its extensive inland waterways. Northern Wisconsin is known as the Northwoods, with large forests, rocky terrain, and thousands of lakes, while central and southern areas contain fertile farmland, rolling hills, river valleys, and prairie remnants. In southwestern Wisconsin, the Driftless Area escaped the most recent glaciation and is characterized by steep wooded ridges, deeply carved valleys, limestone bluffs, and winding rivers.

Wisconsin has shorelines on both Lake Superior and Lake Michigan, creating distinctive coastal landscapes of cliffs, beaches, bays, wetlands, and islands. The Apostle Islands lie in Lake Superior off the northern coast, while the Door Peninsula extends into Lake Michigan and contains bays, limestone shorelines, and smaller islands. Major rivers such as the Wisconsin and Mississippi help shape the state's interior, with the Mississippi forming much of Wisconsin's western boundary. This combination of glacial terrain, forests, inland lakes, unusual unglaciated valleys, and Great Lakes shorelines gives Wisconsin a particularly varied geography.

Wisconsin Borders ▼ +

Wisconsin shares land borders with Michigan to the northeast, Illinois to the south, Iowa to the southwest, and Minnesota to the west.

Wisconsin also borders Lake Superior to the north and Lake Michigan to the east. These Great Lakes provide extensive shoreline and important transportation, recreational, and ecological resources.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Wisconsin is Timms Hill, which reaches approximately 1,951 feet (595 meters) above sea level.

Timms Hill is located in Price County in northern Wisconsin. It is the highest natural point in the state and is situated within a forested region characterized by rolling terrain, lakes, and woodlands.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Wisconsin has an extensive network of rivers, streams, lakes, wetlands, and Great Lakes shorelines. Major rivers include the Wisconsin River, Fox River, Chippewa River, Menominee River, St. Croix River, and Black River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, agriculture, transportation, hydroelectric power, recreation, fisheries, water resources, and communities throughout the state. The Great Lakes also provide major freshwater resources and support commercial and recreational activities.

Economy of Wisconsin ▼ +

Wisconsin has a strong manufacturing and agricultural economy, with particular strengths in food production, machinery, transportation equipment, paper products, and dairy farming. The state is one of the country's leading producers of milk and cheese, supported by extensive dairy farms and a large network of food-processing facilities. Manufacturing is especially important in southeastern and central Wisconsin, where companies produce industrial machinery, engines, electrical equipment, fabricated metals, and other specialized products.

Wisconsin's paper and forest-products industries have a long history, particularly in communities along rivers in the northern and central parts of the state. Agriculture also includes corn, soybeans, cranberries, potatoes, vegetables, and livestock, while breweries and food manufacturers contribute to the state's distinctive food industry. The Great Lakes and the Port of Milwaukee support cargo transportation and connections with regional and international markets. Healthcare, higher education, finance, technology, tourism, and outdoor recreation further diversify the economy, with visitors drawn to the Northwoods, Door Peninsula, Apostle Islands, and Wisconsin's many lakes and state parks.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Wisconsin is divided into 72 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, villages, towns, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Wisconsin include Milwaukee, Madison, Green Bay, Kenosha, Racine, and Appleton.

Madison is the state capital and an important center for government, education, research, healthcare, and technology. Milwaukee is the largest city and a major center for manufacturing, business, culture, healthcare, and commerce. Green Bay is an important regional center for manufacturing, transportation, and commerce in northeastern Wisconsin.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Wisconsin's natural environment is strongly shaped by its forests, inland lakes, wetlands, and Great Lakes shorelines. The Northwoods contain extensive conifer and hardwood forests surrounding thousands of lakes and ponds, while northern wetlands provide important habitats for aquatic plants and wildlife. Along Lake Superior, the Apostle Islands combine rocky shores, sandy beaches, forests, and freshwater habitats, while the Door Peninsula contains limestone shorelines, bays, wetlands, and coastal forests.

Southwestern Wisconsin's Driftless Area has a distinctive ecosystem of wooded ridges, limestone bluffs, spring-fed streams, and deeply carved river valleys that escaped the most recent glaciation. The Mississippi and Wisconsin river systems support wetlands, floodplain forests, and diverse freshwater habitats, while prairie remnants and oak savannas occur in parts of the southern state. Wisconsin's forests and waterways provide habitat for white-tailed deer, black bears, wolves, beavers, river otters, bald eagles, waterfowl, and many freshwater fish species.
