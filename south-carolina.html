About South Carolina ▼ +

South Carolina is a state in the southeastern region of the United States. It is bordered by North Carolina to the north, North Carolina and Georgia to the west and southwest, and the Atlantic Ocean to the southeast. The capital of South Carolina is Columbia.

South Carolina became the 8th state of the United States on May 23, 1788. The state is known for its Atlantic coastline, beaches, coastal plains, forests, rivers, lakes, historic cities, and the Blue Ridge Mountains in the northwest.

Geography of South Carolina ▼ +

South Carolina's landscape changes from the Blue Ridge Mountains in the northwest to the flat Lowcountry along the Atlantic coast. The Blue Ridge occupies only a small part of the state but contains forested slopes, steep valleys, and the state's highest elevations. Farther southeast, the Piedmont consists of rolling hills and red-clay soils, while the Sandhills form a distinct belt of sandy, gently rolling terrain near the Fall Line, where the uplands meet the Coastal Plain.

Eastern and southern South Carolina are dominated by the Lowcountry, a flat coastal region crossed by rivers, tidal creeks, marshes, and estuaries. The Atlantic coast includes broad beaches, barrier islands, and the Sea Islands, with Charleston Harbor forming one of the state's most important coastal features. Major rivers such as the Savannah, Santee, Congaree, and Pee Dee drain the state toward the Atlantic, while the Congaree floodplain creates an extensive lowland landscape in central South Carolina.

South Carolina Borders ▼ +

South Carolina shares land borders with two states: North Carolina to the north and Georgia to the west and southwest.

South Carolina also has a coastline along the Atlantic Ocean to the southeast, including numerous beaches, tidal waterways, and barrier islands.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in South Carolina is Sassafras Mountain, which reaches approximately 3,554 feet (1,083 meters) above sea level.

Sassafras Mountain is located in Pickens County in the northwestern part of the state, near the border with North Carolina. It is part of the Blue Ridge Mountains and is the highest natural point in South Carolina.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

South Carolina has an extensive network of rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Savannah River, Congaree River, Broad River, Saluda River, Edisto River, Wateree River, and Pee Dee River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, drinking water, agriculture, recreation, transportation, industry, hydroelectric power, and communities throughout the state. The coastal rivers and estuaries also provide important habitats for wildlife and marine species.

Economy of South Carolina ▼ +

South Carolina has a diverse economy with major strengths in automotive manufacturing, aerospace, advanced manufacturing, logistics, tourism, agriculture, and healthcare. The Upstate region has become an important center for automobile production and related suppliers, while aerospace manufacturing and research are especially important around Charleston and other industrial areas. The Port of Charleston is a major container port and connects South Carolina with international trade routes.

Agriculture remains important across the state, with poultry, cattle, soybeans, cotton, corn, and peaches among its major products. Forestry and wood products are also significant because of South Carolina's extensive pine forests. The coastal Lowcountry supports commercial fishing, seafood-related activities, and tourism, while Charleston, Myrtle Beach, and the state's barrier islands attract visitors through beaches, historic districts, golf, and coastal recreation. Manufacturing, technology, finance, healthcare, and professional services further diversify the state's economy.

Administrative Division ▼ +

South Carolina is divided into 46 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in South Carolina include Charleston, Columbia, North Charleston, Mount Pleasant, Rock Hill, and Greenville.

Columbia is the state capital and an important center for government, education, healthcare, and business. Charleston is a major historic, cultural, tourism, and port city. Greenville is an important economic and manufacturing center in the Upstate, while North Charleston and Mount Pleasant are major population centers in the Charleston metropolitan area.

Natural Environment ▼ +

South Carolina's natural environment is strongly shaped by its warm climate and Atlantic coastal plain. The Lowcountry contains extensive salt marshes, tidal creeks, estuaries, and maritime forests, while the Sea Islands support beaches, dunes, maritime vegetation, and coastal wetlands. Farther inland, longleaf pine and mixed hardwood forests cover large areas, and the Congaree floodplain contains one of the state's most distinctive bottomland forest ecosystems.

South Carolina's coastal wetlands provide nursery habitats for fish, shrimp, crabs, oysters, and other marine species, while beaches and barrier islands support sea turtles and many shorebirds. Inland rivers and wetlands provide habitat for alligators, white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, river otters, and numerous amphibians and birds. The Blue Ridge and Upstate add forested mountain habitats, clear streams, and waterfalls, creating a natural transition from Appalachian ecosystems to the state's coastal marshes and estuaries.
