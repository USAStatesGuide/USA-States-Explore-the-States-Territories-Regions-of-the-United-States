About Jarvis Island ▼ +

Jarvis Island is an uninhabited coral island in the central Pacific Ocean. It is an unincorporated, unorganized territory of the United States and forms part of the United States Minor Outlying Islands.

The island has no permanent human population and has no towns, cities, or local government. Jarvis Island is protected as part of the Jarvis Island National Wildlife Refuge and is surrounded by important marine habitats. Its remote location contributes to the preservation of its natural environment.

Geography of Jarvis Island ▼ +

Jarvis Island is a small, low-lying coral island in the central Pacific Ocean. It is located south of the equator and is part of a group of remote U.S. Pacific islands. The island is surrounded by a coral reef and shallow coastal waters.

The island is mostly flat and consists primarily of coral sand, reef limestone, and other materials associated with its marine environment. Jarvis Island has no mountains, permanent rivers, or large freshwater lakes. Its low elevation makes its coastal environment vulnerable to storms and changes in sea level.

Location and Surrounding Area ▼ +

Jarvis Island is located in the central Pacific Ocean, approximately halfway between the Hawaiian Islands and the Cook Islands. It lies south of the equator and is one of the most remote islands administered by the United States.

The island has no land borders because it is completely surrounded by the Pacific Ocean. Its nearest neighboring U.S. Pacific islands include Baker Island and Howland Island, although these islands are separated from Jarvis Island by large distances of open ocean.

Highest Point ▼ +

Jarvis Island is a very low-lying coral island. Its highest natural elevation is approximately 23 feet (7 meters) above sea level.

The island has no significant hills or mountains. Its generally flat landscape is characteristic of small coral islands in the tropical Pacific Ocean.

Waterways and Coastal Areas ▼ +

Jarvis Island has no permanent rivers, streams, or freshwater lakes. Rainfall generally infiltrates the porous coral ground, and the island does not contain major permanent surface-water systems.

The island is surrounded by coral reefs, shallow coastal waters, and the open Pacific Ocean. These marine environments provide habitat for fish, corals, seabirds, sea turtles, and other marine organisms.

Human Activity and Economy ▼ +

Jarvis Island has no permanent residents and does not have a conventional local economy. There are no towns, commercial centers, hotels, or permanent businesses on the island.

Human activity is limited and is primarily associated with wildlife management, scientific research, conservation, and occasional authorized visits. The island's protected status limits development and helps preserve its natural ecosystems.

Administrative Status ▼ +

Jarvis Island is an unincorporated, unorganized territory of the United States. It is administered as part of the United States Minor Outlying Islands rather than as a state, commonwealth, or organized U.S. territory.

The island is federally managed and is protected as the Jarvis Island National Wildlife Refuge. Because there is no permanent population, Jarvis Island does not have municipalities, counties, cities, or local elected government.

Settlements and Population ▼ +

Jarvis Island has no permanent population and has no officially established cities or towns. There are no permanent residential communities on the island.

The island was temporarily inhabited during historical settlement efforts connected with the extraction of guano. A small settlement was established during the twentieth century, but it was later abandoned. Today, access is restricted and human presence is generally limited to authorized scientific, conservation, and management activities.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Jarvis Island supports a distinctive tropical coral-island ecosystem. Its vegetation is adapted to exposed and relatively dry conditions, while the island provides important nesting, breeding, and resting habitat for numerous seabirds.

The surrounding marine environment contains coral reefs, fish, sea turtles, and many other forms of ocean wildlife. Jarvis Island is particularly valuable for seabirds and other marine-dependent species. Its protection as a national wildlife refuge helps conserve both terrestrial habitats and surrounding marine ecosystems.

Conservation ▼ +

Jarvis Island is protected as part of the Jarvis Island National Wildlife Refuge. The island and its surrounding waters provide important habitat for seabirds, shorebirds, and marine wildlife.

Conservation efforts focus on protecting native habitats, seabird nesting areas, coral reef ecosystems, and surrounding marine resources. The island's remote and uninhabited character helps limit direct human disturbance.

Access to Jarvis Island is restricted and requires appropriate authorization. Activities are managed to protect the island's wildlife, natural resources, and fragile ecosystems.
