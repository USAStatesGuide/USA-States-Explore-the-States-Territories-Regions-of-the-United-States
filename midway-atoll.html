About Midway Atoll ▼ +

Midway Atoll is a remote coral atoll in the North Pacific Ocean and an unincorporated territory of the United States. It is located approximately 1,300 miles northwest of Honolulu, Hawaii, near the northwestern end of the Hawaiian Archipelago.

The atoll consists of a shallow lagoon surrounded by coral reef and three main islands: Sand Island, Eastern Island, and Spit Island. Midway is internationally known for its importance in marine conservation, seabird habitat, and the Battle of Midway during World War II.

Geography of Midway Atoll ▼ +

Midway Atoll is a low-lying coral atoll formed around a submerged volcanic structure. It consists of a coral reef surrounding a large shallow lagoon, with small islands and sand and coral areas around its perimeter.

The two principal islands are Sand Island and Eastern Island. Sand Island is the largest and contains most of the remaining infrastructure. Eastern Island is smaller and is largely covered by natural vegetation and historic military remains.

Location and Surroundings ▼ +

Midway Atoll is located in the North Pacific Ocean, approximately 1,300 miles northwest of Honolulu. It lies near the northwestern end of the Hawaiian Archipelago and is geographically isolated from the main Hawaiian Islands.

The atoll is surrounded by open ocean and extensive coral reef habitats. Its position makes it an important stopover and breeding area for many species of seabirds and marine wildlife.

Highest Point ▼ +

Midway Atoll is extremely low-lying and has no mountains or significant natural high points. Its islands generally rise only a few meters above sea level.

The low elevation is characteristic of coral atolls and makes Midway particularly sensitive to storms, coastal erosion, and long-term changes in sea level.

Lagoon and Waterways ▼ +

The central lagoon is the dominant water feature of Midway Atoll. It is enclosed by coral reef and provides sheltered marine habitat for fish, seabirds, sea turtles, and other aquatic organisms.

The surrounding coral reef and ocean waters are part of a larger marine ecosystem that supports a wide variety of species. Unlike continental regions, Midway Atoll has no natural rivers or freshwater river systems.

History of Midway Atoll ▼ +

Midway Atoll was formally annexed by the United States in 1867 and later became an important stop for trans-Pacific aviation and communications. The U.S. Navy also developed facilities on the islands during the twentieth century.

Midway became especially important during World War II. In June 1942, the Battle of Midway was fought in the waters around the atoll. The battle was a major naval engagement between the United States and Japan and became an important turning point in the Pacific War.

After World War II, Midway continued to serve military and aviation purposes for several decades. Military activities were eventually reduced, and the area became increasingly focused on wildlife conservation and historical preservation.

Administrative Status ▼ +

Midway Atoll is an unincorporated territory of the United States. It is not a U.S. state and does not have counties, cities, or a permanent civilian government structure like the states of the mainland.

Midway Atoll National Wildlife Refuge is managed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. The atoll is also part of the Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument, one of the world's important protected marine areas.

Major Settlements ▼ +

Midway Atoll has no permanent civilian cities or towns. Sand Island historically contained residential, military, aviation, and administrative facilities.

Today, the atoll has a very small temporary population consisting primarily of personnel involved in wildlife management, conservation, maintenance, research, and other authorized activities.

Marine Environment ▼ +

The waters surrounding Midway Atoll contain extensive coral reef habitats and support a diverse marine ecosystem. The lagoon, reef flats, reef slopes, and surrounding ocean provide habitat for numerous marine species.

Marine life around Midway includes coral communities, reef fish, sharks, rays, sea turtles, and other organisms. The protected waters also provide important feeding and breeding areas for wildlife throughout the Hawaiian Archipelago.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Midway Atoll is one of the most important seabird colonies in the Pacific Ocean. The islands provide nesting habitat for large populations of albatrosses, including Laysan albatrosses and black-footed albatrosses.

Other seabirds found at Midway include boobies, frigatebirds, terns, noddies, and shearwaters. The islands and surrounding waters also provide habitat for the endangered Hawaiian monk seal and the threatened green sea turtle.

The combination of coral reefs, lagoon waters, sandy beaches, coastal vegetation, and seabird colonies makes Midway Atoll an important ecological area in the central Pacific.

Conservation ▼ +

Midway Atoll National Wildlife Refuge was established to protect the area's wildlife and habitats. The refuge forms part of the Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument, which protects a vast area of land and ocean in the northwestern Hawaiian Islands.

Conservation programs at Midway focus on protecting seabird colonies, restoring native habitats, controlling invasive species, protecting marine wildlife, and preserving important historical resources.

Access to Midway Atoll is restricted. Visitors generally require authorization and must comply with conservation, historical preservation, and wildlife protection rules.
