About Kingman Reef ▼ +

Kingman Reef is a largely submerged coral reef in the central Pacific Ocean. It is an unincorporated territory of the United States and is located approximately halfway between Hawaii and American Samoa.

The reef consists of a narrow, partly submerged coral structure surrounding a deep lagoon. Most of Kingman Reef remains below sea level, with only small areas of sand and coral rubble occasionally exposed above the water.

Geography of Kingman Reef ▼ +

Kingman Reef is a low-lying coral reef in the central Pacific Ocean. It forms a roughly triangular structure around a deep lagoon and covers a much larger marine area than its exposed land surface.

The reef is almost completely submerged. Its shallow reef platform, coral formations, sandbars, and surrounding ocean waters create a highly distinctive tropical marine environment. Because of its extremely low elevation, Kingman Reef has no mountains, rivers, or conventional terrestrial landscape.

Location and Surroundings ▼ +

Kingman Reef is located in the central Pacific Ocean, approximately halfway between Hawaii and American Samoa. It lies north of Palmyra Atoll and is part of the Pacific Remote Islands region.

The reef is surrounded by open Pacific Ocean waters and is isolated from major population centers. Its remote location contributes to the relatively undisturbed condition of its marine ecosystem.

Highest Point ▼ +

Kingman Reef has no significant natural high point because almost the entire reef is submerged. Small areas of coral rubble and sand can rise only slightly above sea level.

The extremely low elevation makes Kingman Reef particularly vulnerable to changes in sea level, storms, waves, and other oceanic processes.

Lagoon and Reef ▼ +

The central feature of Kingman Reef is its deep lagoon, surrounded by a shallow coral reef platform. The lagoon is connected to the surrounding Pacific Ocean through openings in the reef.

The reef provides a variety of marine habitats, including shallow coral areas, deeper waters, reef edges, and lagoon environments. These habitats support numerous species of fish, corals, seabirds, and other marine organisms.

History of Kingman Reef ▼ +

Kingman Reef was first recorded by Western navigators in the nineteenth century. The reef was named after U.S. sea captain W. E. Kingman, who described the reef during a voyage in 1853.

The United States formally took possession of Kingman Reef in the nineteenth century. Because the reef is almost entirely submerged, it has never developed a permanent settlement or conventional urban infrastructure.

During the twentieth century, Kingman Reef was occasionally visited by vessels and became associated with the wider history of U.S. activities in the central Pacific.

Administrative Status ▼ +

Kingman Reef is an unincorporated territory of the United States. Unlike a U.S. state, it does not have a state government, counties, cities, or a permanent civilian population.

The reef and its surrounding waters are managed primarily for conservation. Kingman Reef National Wildlife Refuge is part of the Pacific Remote Islands National Wildlife Refuge Complex.

Major Settlements ▼ +

Kingman Reef has no permanent cities, towns, or civilian settlements. There is no resident population living on the reef.

Due to its remote location, extremely small exposed land area, and protected status, Kingman Reef does not have conventional roads, public services, commercial centers, or residential communities.

Marine Environment ▼ +

Kingman Reef contains a highly productive marine ecosystem. Its coral reefs, lagoon, reef slopes, and surrounding ocean provide habitat for a wide variety of marine species.

The waters around the reef support numerous reef fish, sharks, rays, corals, sea turtles, seabirds, and other organisms. The reef is considered an important example of a relatively remote Pacific coral ecosystem.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Kingman Reef has very little terrestrial vegetation because most of its surface is submerged. Its ecological importance is primarily associated with its marine environment and surrounding coral reef habitats.

The reef provides feeding and resting areas for seabirds and supports a diverse community of marine organisms. Its isolated location has helped preserve important ecological processes within the central Pacific.

Conservation ▼ +

Kingman Reef is protected as a National Wildlife Refuge. The surrounding marine environment is also included within the broader protected area of the Pacific Remote Islands Marine National Monument.

Conservation measures help protect coral reefs, fish, seabirds, sea turtles, and other wildlife from human disturbance and other environmental pressures.

Because of its protected status and remote location, access to Kingman Reef is highly restricted and is generally limited to authorized activities, scientific research, conservation, and other permitted purposes.
