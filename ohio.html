Ohio, United States

About Ohio ▼ +

Ohio is a state in the Midwestern region of the United States. It is bordered by Michigan to the north, Pennsylvania and West Virginia to the east, Kentucky to the south, Indiana to the west, and Lake Erie to the north. The capital of Ohio is Columbus.

Ohio became the 17th state of the United States on March 1, 1803. The state is known for its Great Lakes coastline, fertile plains, forests, rivers, rolling hills, agricultural lands, and major urban centers.

Geography of Ohio ▼ +

Ohio's landscape changes from the glacial plains of the north and west to the rugged Appalachian Plateau in the southeast. Northern Ohio contains broad, relatively flat plains shaped by glaciers, including fertile farmland and low ridges, while central Ohio has gently rolling terrain and river valleys. Southeastern Ohio is more heavily dissected by streams, with wooded hills, narrow valleys, sandstone cliffs, and distinctive landscapes such as Hocking Hills.

Lake Erie forms Ohio's entire northern boundary and has strongly influenced the state's geography, settlement, and transportation. Its shoreline includes beaches, wetlands, bays, coastal marshes, and important ports, while the western Lake Erie region contains shallow waters and islands. The Ohio River forms much of the state's southeastern and southern boundary, creating another major transportation and geographic corridor. The Scioto, Miami, Maumee, and Cuyahoga rivers are also important features, connecting Ohio's interior landscapes with the Great Lakes and Ohio River systems.

Ohio Borders ▼ +

Ohio shares land borders with five states: Michigan to the north, Pennsylvania to the east, West Virginia to the southeast, Kentucky to the south, and Indiana to the west.

Ohio also has a shoreline along Lake Erie in the northern part of the state.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Ohio is Campbell Hill, which reaches approximately 1,549 feet (472 meters) above sea level.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Ohio has an extensive network of rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Ohio River, Maumee River, Scioto River, Miami River, Muskingum River, Great Miami River, and Hocking River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, transportation, water resources, agriculture, recreation, industry, and communities throughout the state.

Economy of Ohio ▼ +

Ohio has a highly diversified economy with a strong industrial base. Automotive manufacturing and vehicle components remain important, alongside aerospace, machinery, steel, chemicals, plastics, and advanced manufacturing. Cleveland and Toledo have major connections to Great Lakes shipping and industry, while Cincinnati and Columbus are important centers for business, logistics, healthcare, finance, and professional services. Ohio's central position also makes it a major transportation and distribution hub linking the Midwest, Great Lakes, and eastern United States.

Agriculture is especially important across western and central Ohio, where fertile soils support corn, soybeans, wheat, livestock, dairy farming, and specialty crops. The Lake Erie region also supports vineyards, fruit production, and tourism. Major highways, railways, the Ohio River, and Lake Erie provide important routes for moving industrial and agricultural products. Healthcare, higher education, technology, energy, and tourism further diversify the state's economy, with attractions such as Cedar Point, Hocking Hills, and Lake Erie contributing to regional tourism.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Ohio is divided into 88 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, villages, townships, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Ohio include Columbus, Cleveland, Cincinnati, Toledo, Akron, and Dayton.

Columbus is the state capital and the largest city by population. Cleveland is a major city on Lake Erie and an important center for healthcare, industry, culture, and transportation. Cincinnati is located along the Ohio River and is an important economic and cultural center, while Toledo is a major Great Lakes port city.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Ohio's natural environment reflects the transition from the glacial plains of northern and western Ohio to the forested Appalachian landscapes of the southeast. The Lake Erie shoreline contains coastal wetlands, marshes, beaches, islands, and shallow freshwater habitats, while the western Lake Erie region is especially important for migratory birds and aquatic ecosystems. Southern and southeastern Ohio contain more extensive forests, wooded hills, sandstone cliffs, caves, and stream-cut valleys, including the distinctive landscapes of Hocking Hills.

Ohio's rivers and wetlands provide important habitats for fish, amphibians, waterfowl, and other wildlife. The Ohio, Scioto, Maumee, Cuyahoga, and other river systems connect forests, wetlands, and agricultural landscapes across the state. Forest habitats support white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, black bears, beavers, and many woodland birds, while Lake Erie supports walleye, yellow perch, waterfowl, and other freshwater species. Protected areas and state parks preserve representative examples of Ohio's forests, wetlands, river valleys, and Lake Erie ecosystems.
