About the United States (USA)

United States: Geography and Territory ▼ +

The United States of America (USA) is a sovereign federal republic consisting, within the federal union, of 50 states and the District of Columbia. Its territory extends across North America and the Pacific Ocean, while it also includes American territories and islands under U.S. sovereignty in the Caribbean and Pacific regions, including Puerto Rico, Guam, American Samoa, the U.S. Virgin Islands, and the Northern Mariana Islands, as well as the U.S. Minor Outlying Islands.

The United States has a total area of approximately 9.8 million km², making it the fourth-largest country in the world by total area under the commonly used ranking. The contiguous United States stretches from the Atlantic Ocean in the east to the Pacific Ocean in the west, with Canada to the north and Mexico to the south. Alaska lies in the northwestern part of North America, bordered by Canada to the east and separated from Russia by the Bering Strait, while Hawaii is located in the Pacific Ocean.

The country has an exceptionally diverse physical geography, including plains, plateaus, mountains, deserts, forests, and regions with polar and subpolar climates. Its major mountain ranges include the Rocky Mountains and the Appalachian Mountains, while notable desert regions include the Mojave Desert, Sonoran Desert, and Great Basin.

The United States also has extensive coastlines along the Atlantic Ocean, Pacific Ocean, and Gulf of Mexico, as well as Alaska's coastlines along the Bering Sea, Arctic Ocean, and Gulf of Alaska. It contains a vast network of rivers and lakes, including the Mississippi River, Missouri River, and Great Lakes. This vast geographical extent and exceptional natural diversity give rise to a wide range of climatic and ecological regions across the United States and its areas under U.S. sovereignty.

Structure of the United States ▼ +

The United States of America (USA), in the official sense of the country, consists of 50 states and the District of Columbia (Washington, D.C.). The 50 states are divided into 48 contiguous states located on the mainland of North America, plus Alaska and Hawaii. The District of Columbia is a federal district that contains the capital, Washington, D.C., and is not a state.

In addition to the states and the District of Columbia, the United States exercises sovereignty over five inhabited U.S. territories: Puerto Rico, Guam, American Samoa, the Northern Mariana Islands, and the U.S. Virgin Islands, as well as a number of U.S. Minor Outlying Islands. These territories and areas differ from the states in their political and legal status and therefore are not included in the number of 50 states.

Thus, a distinction should be made between the United States as a country: 50 states + the District of Columbia, and the broader scope of areas under U.S. sovereignty, which also includes the territories and other outlying areas.

In brief:

🇺🇸 50 States + 🏛️ District of Columbia = United States of America (USA), as the federal union

🇺🇸 50 states + 🏛️ District of Columbia + 🌴 5 inhabited U.S. territories + 🏝️ U.S. Minor Outlying Islands = the United States and its territories and outlying islands

Administrative Division of the United States ▼ +

The administrative structure of the United States varies from one state to another according to the constitution, laws, and governmental systems of each state. The 50 states are the primary constituent units of the federal system. Most states are divided into counties, which generally serve as important units of local government. However, some states use different terms or organizational arrangements for county-level jurisdictions, such as parishes in Louisiana and boroughs in Alaska.

Within the states, local government may consist of cities, towns, villages, townships, municipalities, and other types of local governmental units, depending on the laws and administrative systems of each state. These local governments can differ considerably in their names, powers, boundaries, responsibilities, and organizational structures. Some states also contain independent cities, which function as county-equivalent jurisdictions and are not administratively part of any county.

In addition, there are unincorporated communities and areas that do not have their own incorporated municipal government. The District of Columbia (Washington, D.C.) has a distinct governmental structure and is not part of any state. The U.S. territories also have their own governmental and administrative arrangements, which differ from those of the 50 states and from one territory to another.

Consequently, there is no single administrative hierarchy, such as “state → county → city,” that applies uniformly throughout the United States. The exact structure depends on the particular state, territory, or jurisdiction.
