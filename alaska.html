About Alaska ▼ +

Alaska is a state in the northwestern part of North America and the northernmost and westernmost state of the United States. It is separated from the contiguous United States by Canada. Alaska borders Canada to the east and faces the Pacific Ocean, the Bering Sea, the Bering Strait, and the Arctic Ocean. The capital of Alaska is Juneau.

Alaska became the 49th state of the United States on January 3, 1959. It is the largest U.S. state by land area and is known for its mountains, glaciers, forests, tundra, rivers, lakes, islands, and extensive coastal environments.

Geography of Alaska ▼ +

Alaska is the largest U.S. state and has some of the most dramatic landscapes in North America. Its terrain includes the Alaska Range, Brooks Range, vast tundra, boreal forests, glaciers, deep valleys, rivers, and thousands of lakes. The Alaska Range contains Denali, the highest mountain in North America, while the northern part of the state extends into the Arctic region.

Alaska is surrounded by the Arctic Ocean, Bering Sea, and Pacific Ocean and has an exceptionally long and irregular coastline. Its southern and southeastern regions are characterized by rugged mountains, glaciers, fjords, islands, and temperate rainforests. The Aleutian Islands extend westward from the Alaska Peninsula, forming a long chain of volcanic islands between the Pacific Ocean and the Bering Sea.

Alaska Borders ▼ +

Alaska has one international land border: Canada to the east. It shares borders with the Canadian provinces of British Columbia and Yukon.

Alaska also borders the Pacific Ocean, Bering Sea, Bering Strait, and Arctic Ocean.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Alaska is Denali, which reaches approximately 20,310 feet (6,190 meters) above sea level.

Denali is located in the Alaska Range and is the highest mountain in North America.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Alaska has an extensive network of rivers and streams. Major waterways include the Yukon River, Kuskokwim River, Susitna River, Copper River, Kobuk River, and Colville River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, fisheries, transportation, water resources, recreation, and communities across the state.

Economy of Alaska ▼ +

Alaska's economy is strongly connected to its natural resources and remote geographic location. Oil and natural gas have long been among the state's most important economic activities, particularly in the North Slope region. Mining is also significant, with Alaska producing resources such as gold, zinc, lead, and other minerals. The state government and public services also play an important role in employment and economic activity.

Commercial fishing is another major part of Alaska's economy, especially for salmon, pollock, crab, and other seafood. Tourism is closely linked to the state's distinctive landscapes and wildlife, attracting visitors to glaciers, national parks, mountains, coastal areas, and wilderness destinations. Transportation and shipping are also important because Alaska's vast distances and position between North America and the Pacific make air, sea, and road connections essential to many communities.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Alaska does not use counties as its primary local-government structure. Its county-equivalent areas include organized boroughs and census areas, along with other forms of local government.

The state also contains municipalities, cities, unified municipalities, and many unincorporated communities.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Alaska include Anchorage, Fairbanks, Juneau, Sitka, Ketchikan, and Wasilla.

Juneau is the state capital. Anchorage is the largest city by population and a major transportation and economic center. Fairbanks is an important interior population and transportation center.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Alaska contains some of the largest and least-developed natural landscapes in the United States. Its environment includes Arctic tundra, vast boreal forests, mountain ranges, glaciers, wetlands, rivers, and extensive coastal ecosystems. The state also contains large areas of permanent ice, including glaciers that cover parts of its major mountain regions.

Alaska's wildlife is closely connected to these varied environments. The Arctic and interior support species such as caribou, moose, grizzly bears, and wolves, while coastal waters provide habitat for whales, sea otters, seals, sea lions, salmon, and many seabirds. Southeastern Alaska is also known for its temperate rainforests, while the Aleutian Islands contain distinctive volcanic landscapes and important marine habitats.
