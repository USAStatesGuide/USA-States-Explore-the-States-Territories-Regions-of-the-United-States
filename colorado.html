Colorado, United States

About Colorado ▼ +

Colorado is a state in the western United States, centered on the Rocky Mountains. It borders Wyoming and Nebraska to the north, Kansas and Oklahoma to the east, New Mexico and Arizona to the south, and Utah to the west. Its capital and largest city by population is Denver.

Colorado became the 38th state of the United States on August 1, 1876. It is widely known for its high elevations, extensive Rocky Mountain landscapes, plateaus, forests, plains, and arid regions.

Geography of Colorado ▼ +

Colorado is dominated by the Rocky Mountains, which extend across the central part of the state and include the rugged Front Range and the Continental Divide. The state's highest peaks are found here, including Mount Elbert, the highest point in Colorado. Farther west, the landscape changes into the Colorado Plateau, with broad mesas, deep canyons, and distinctive rock formations.

East of the mountains, Colorado opens into the Great Plains, where the land is generally flatter and more suitable for agriculture. Major rivers such as the Colorado, Arkansas, and South Platte begin in or near the mountain region and flow toward different parts of the state. This sharp transition from high alpine mountains to foothills, plateaus, and open plains gives Colorado one of the most varied landscapes in the interior western United States.

Colorado Borders ▼ +

Colorado borders seven U.S. states: Wyoming and Nebraska to the north, Kansas and Oklahoma to the east, New Mexico and Arizona to the south, and Utah to the west.

Colorado's southwest corner is part of the Four Corners region, where Colorado, Arizona, New Mexico, and Utah meet.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Colorado is Mount Elbert, which rises to approximately 14,440 feet (4,401 meters) above sea level.

Mount Elbert is located in the Sawatch Range of the Rocky Mountains and is the highest summit in Colorado.

Mountain Ranges ▼ +

Major mountain ranges in Colorado include the Sawatch Range, Front Range, San Juan Mountains, and Sangre de Cristo Mountains.

Colorado is famous for its numerous peaks above 14,000 feet, commonly known as fourteeners.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Major rivers associated with Colorado include the Colorado River, South Platte River, North Platte River, Arkansas River, Rio Grande, and Yampa River.

Colorado's mountain ranges are an important source of water for rivers flowing toward the Great Plains, the Southwest, and the Colorado River Basin.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Colorado is divided into 64 counties. Counties are important units of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, municipalities, special districts, unincorporated communities, and other local governmental areas.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities in Colorado include Denver, Colorado Springs, Aurora, Fort Collins, Lakewood, and Thornton.

Denver is the state capital and largest city. Colorado Springs, Aurora, and Fort Collins are other major population and economic centers.

Climate and Natural Environment ▼ +

Colorado's natural environment is strongly shaped by elevation, with conditions ranging from relatively dry grasslands on the eastern plains to cold alpine environments above the tree line. The Rocky Mountains contain extensive forests of lodgepole pine, ponderosa pine, and spruce-fir, along with alpine meadows, rocky peaks, and permanent snowfields in the highest areas.

The mountains also influence Colorado's water supply, storing winter snow that feeds rivers and reservoirs as it melts in spring and summer. These habitats support species such as elk, mule deer, bighorn sheep, black bears, and mountain goats. Colorado's western plateaus and canyons add drier ecosystems, while the eastern plains contain native grasslands and important habitat for pronghorn and prairie wildlife.

Economy ▼ +

Colorado has a diversified economy with strong activity in aerospace, technology, energy, agriculture, manufacturing, and outdoor recreation. The Denver and Boulder areas are important centers for technology, telecommunications, financial services, and professional businesses, while Colorado Springs has a major aerospace and defense sector.

The Rocky Mountains give Colorado a major tourism economy based on skiing, hiking, mountain resorts, and other outdoor activities. The eastern plains support cattle ranching and crops such as wheat, corn, and hay, while the state also produces oil, natural gas, and other energy resources. Craft brewing and food production have also become notable parts of Colorado's modern economy.
