About Illinois ▼ +

Illinois is a state in the Midwestern United States. It is bordered by Wisconsin to the north, Indiana to the east, Kentucky to the southeast, Missouri and Iowa to the west, and Michigan across Lake Michigan. The capital of Illinois is Springfield, while Chicago is the state's largest city.

Illinois became the 21st state of the United States on December 3, 1818. The state is known for its extensive agricultural areas, major cities, rivers, prairies, forests, wetlands, and its location along Lake Michigan and the Mississippi River system.

Geography of Illinois ▼ +

Illinois is largely a flat, glacially shaped state with some of the most fertile plains in the Midwest. The central and northern regions form part of the Corn Belt and are dominated by broad agricultural landscapes, while the western edge is marked by bluffs overlooking the Mississippi River. The Illinois River crosses the state from the northeast toward the Mississippi, creating an important natural and transportation corridor.

Northeastern Illinois is defined by Lake Michigan and the Chicago metropolitan area, where the Great Lakes connect the state to a major inland waterway system. In southern Illinois, the landscape changes noticeably around the Shawnee Hills, with wooded ridges, valleys, cliffs, and sandstone formations. The southern tip of the state lies between the Mississippi and Ohio rivers, giving Illinois a distinctive geographic position at the meeting point of major Midwestern river systems.

Illinois Borders ▼ +

Illinois shares land borders with Wisconsin to the north, Indiana to the east, Kentucky to the southeast, Missouri to the southwest, and Iowa to the west.

Illinois also borders Lake Michigan to the northeast. Across the lake lies the state of Michigan. The Mississippi River forms much of Illinois' western boundary, while the Ohio River forms part of its southern boundary.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest natural point in Illinois is Charles Mound, which reaches approximately 1,235 feet (376 meters) above sea level.

Charles Mound is located in Jo Daviess County in the northwestern part of the state, near the Wisconsin border. It is part of the Driftless Area, a region with distinctive hills and valleys.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Illinois has an extensive network of rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Mississippi River, Illinois River, Ohio River, Wabash River, Kaskaskia River, and Rock River.

These waterways are important for transportation, agriculture, ecosystems, recreation, fisheries, flood management, and water resources. The Illinois River also forms an important connection between the Mississippi River and the Chicago region's waterway system.

Economy of Illinois ▼ +

Illinois has one of the most diversified economies in the United States, with Chicago serving as a major center for finance, business, technology, manufacturing, and commerce. The Chicago metropolitan area is also an important hub for food processing, professional services, healthcare, and corporate activity. The state's central location and connections to the Great Lakes, Mississippi River, railways, and major highways have made transportation and distribution particularly important to its economy.

Agriculture remains a major part of Illinois, especially across the fertile central and northern plains of the Corn Belt, where corn and soybeans are the leading crops. The state also has important industries in machinery, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, food processing, and advanced manufacturing. Major waterways such as the Illinois and Mississippi rivers, together with extensive rail and highway networks and O'Hare International Airport, connect Illinois farms and industries with markets across the United States and abroad.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Illinois is divided into 102 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state and perform various administrative and public service functions.

The state also contains cities, villages, towns, townships, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Chicago is the largest city, while Springfield serves as the state capital.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Illinois include Chicago, Aurora, Naperville, Joliet, Rockford, and Springfield.

Chicago is the largest city in Illinois and a major transportation, commercial, cultural, and economic center. Springfield is the state capital and an important center of state government. Other major cities contribute to manufacturing, education, healthcare, commerce, and regional services.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Illinois is largely characterized by the fertile prairie landscapes of the central and northern parts of the state, which were shaped by glaciers and once covered much of the region with tallgrass prairie. Today, only small areas of the original prairie remain, while fertile soils support extensive farmland. The Illinois River and its wetlands form an important ecological corridor between the Mississippi River and Lake Michigan.

The state's western border along the Mississippi River contains floodplains, wetlands, and forested river habitats, while Lake Michigan supports beaches, dunes, wetlands, and aquatic ecosystems along northeastern Illinois. Southern Illinois has a distinctly more forested landscape, especially in the Shawnee National Forest, with wooded hills, sandstone formations, and streams. These varied environments support white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, waterfowl, river fish, amphibians, and many migratory birds.
