Puerto Rico, United States

About Puerto Rico ▼ +

Puerto Rico is an island territory of the United States located in the northeastern Caribbean. It is the easternmost and smallest of the Greater Antilles and lies between the Caribbean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean. San Juan is the capital and largest city.

Puerto Rico is an unincorporated U.S. territory rather than one of the 50 states. The island is known for its tropical climate, mountainous interior, coastal plains, beaches, forests, rivers, historic cities, and diverse cultural heritage.

Puerto Rico's Status ▼ +

Puerto Rico is an unincorporated territory of the United States. It is not a U.S. state, but people born in Puerto Rico are generally U.S. citizens under U.S. law.

Puerto Rico has its own local government and constitution, while the United States federal government has authority over matters defined by federal law. The territory has its own elected governor, legislature, municipalities, and local government institutions.

Puerto Rico uses the U.S. dollar as its currency and is part of the United States political system while retaining a distinct territorial status.

Geography of Puerto Rico ▼ +

Puerto Rico is a roughly rectangular island measuring about 110 miles from west to east and around 40 miles from north to south. It is characterized by a mountainous interior surrounded by coastal plains and lowlands.

More than half of the island consists of hills and mountainous terrain. The Cordillera Central crosses the interior, while the northern region contains extensive limestone formations and karst landscapes. Coastal plains are more prominent along parts of the northern and southern shores.

Puerto Rico's geographic position gives it an important location between the Caribbean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean, near the Mona Passage, a major maritime route between the Caribbean and the Atlantic.

Location and Surrounding Waters ▼ +

Puerto Rico is located in the northeastern Caribbean Sea. The Atlantic Ocean lies to the north, while the Caribbean Sea lies to the south. The island is east of Hispaniola and west of the Virgin Islands.

The Mona Passage separates Puerto Rico from the Dominican Republic and Haiti on the island of Hispaniola. The Virgin Islands are located to the east of Puerto Rico.

Puerto Rico has an extensive coastline containing beaches, rocky shores, bays, mangroves, coral reefs, and coastal wetlands.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Puerto Rico is Cerro de Punta, located in the central mountainous region near the municipalities of Jayuya and Ponce. It rises to approximately 4,390 feet (1,338 meters) above sea level.

Cerro de Punta is part of the Cordillera Central, which forms the main mountainous backbone of Puerto Rico. The surrounding highlands contain steep slopes, forests, streams, and numerous valleys.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Puerto Rico has a dense network of rivers and streams. Important rivers include the Río de la Plata, Río Grande de Loíza, Río Grande de Arecibo, Río Grande de Añasco, and Río Culebrinas.

Rivers generally begin in the mountainous interior and flow toward the surrounding coastal areas. The northern part of the island contains many relatively well-watered river systems, while parts of the southern coast are comparatively drier.

Puerto Rico has hundreds of surface-water streams and waterways that support freshwater ecosystems, agriculture, communities, reservoirs, and wetlands.

Climate of Puerto Rico ▼ +

Puerto Rico has a predominantly tropical marine climate with warm temperatures throughout the year and relatively small seasonal temperature changes compared with temperate regions.

Climate conditions vary according to elevation and location. The northern and mountainous areas generally receive more rainfall, while parts of the southern coast are comparatively dry because of the island's topography and prevailing weather patterns.

Puerto Rico can experience tropical storms and hurricanes, particularly during the Atlantic hurricane season. Heavy rainfall can produce flooding and landslides in mountainous areas.

Economy of Puerto Rico ▼ +

Puerto Rico has a diversified economy that includes manufacturing, pharmaceuticals, medical products, tourism, services, trade, finance, construction, agriculture, and government activity. Manufacturing has historically played an important role in the territory's economic structure.

Tourism is also significant because of Puerto Rico's beaches, historic cities, tropical landscapes, forests, cultural attractions, and cruise ports. San Juan serves as an important center for commerce, tourism, transportation, and services.

Agriculture includes products such as coffee, plantains, bananas, tropical fruits, livestock products, and other crops. The island's varied climate and terrain allow different agricultural activities across its regions.

Municipalities of Puerto Rico ▼ +

Puerto Rico is divided into 78 municipalities. These municipalities provide local government services and administration across the island and its smaller associated islands.

Unlike U.S. states, Puerto Rico does not use counties as its principal local-government divisions. Municipalities serve as the main local administrative units.

Major municipalities include San Juan, Bayamón, Carolina, Ponce, Caguas, Guaynabo, and Mayagüez.

Major Cities and Urban Areas ▼ +

San Juan is the capital and largest city of Puerto Rico and is the principal center of government, commerce, tourism, culture, and transportation.

Other important urban areas include Bayamón, Carolina, Ponce, Caguas, Guaynabo, and Mayagüez.

Population is concentrated particularly around the metropolitan area of San Juan and other coastal and valley communities, while the mountainous interior generally has lower population density.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Puerto Rico contains a wide variety of natural environments, including tropical forests, mountains, rivers, wetlands, mangroves, beaches, coral reefs, caves, coastal ecosystems, and limestone landscapes.

The El Yunque National Forest is one of the island's best-known natural areas and contains tropical forest ecosystems, mountain streams, waterfalls, and diverse plant and animal species.

Puerto Rico's coastal and marine ecosystems include coral reefs, seagrass beds, mangrove forests, beaches, and estuaries. These environments provide habitat for numerous species and help protect coastal areas from waves, storms, and erosion.

Islands and Offshore Areas ▼ +

In addition to the main island, the territory includes several smaller islands and cays. The best-known inhabited offshore islands include Vieques and Culebra, located east of the main island.

Other smaller islands include Mona, Desecheo, and numerous coastal islets and cays. These islands contain beaches, forests, cliffs, reefs, and other important coastal and marine habitats.
