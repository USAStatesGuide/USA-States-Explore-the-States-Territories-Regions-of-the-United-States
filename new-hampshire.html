About New Hampshire ▼ +

New Hampshire is a state in the New England region of the northeastern United States. It is bordered by Maine to the east, Massachusetts to the south, Vermont to the west, and the Canadian province of Quebec to the north and northwest. The state also has a short coastline along the Atlantic Ocean. The capital of New Hampshire is Concord.

New Hampshire became the 9th state of the United States on June 21, 1788. The state is known for its White Mountains, forests, lakes, rivers, scenic landscapes, and Atlantic coastline. Mount Washington is one of its most famous natural landmarks.

Geography of New Hampshire ▼ +

New Hampshire is dominated in the north by the White Mountains, part of the northern Appalachian system. This region contains rugged peaks, narrow valleys, forests, and alpine environments, including Mount Washington, the highest point in the northeastern United States. Central New Hampshire is known for its Lakes Region, where Lake Winnipesaukee and numerous smaller lakes occupy a landscape shaped by glaciers. Farther south, the terrain becomes lower and more gently rolling, with the Merrimack River Valley forming an important geographic corridor.

Southeastern New Hampshire reaches the Atlantic Ocean along a short but distinctive coastline known as the Seacoast. The coastal area includes sandy beaches, rocky shores, tidal rivers, and small estuaries, while the Piscataqua River forms part of the state's boundary with Maine. The combination of the White Mountains, central lakes, Merrimack Valley, and Atlantic Seacoast gives New Hampshire a strong north-to-south geographic contrast within a relatively small area.

New Hampshire Borders ▼ +

New Hampshire shares land borders with three U.S. states: Maine to the east, Massachusetts to the south, and Vermont to the west. It also borders the Canadian province of Quebec to the north and northwest.

New Hampshire also has a short coastline along the Atlantic Ocean in the southeastern part of the state.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in New Hampshire is Mount Washington, which reaches approximately 6,288 feet (1,917 meters) above sea level.

Mount Washington is located in the Presidential Range of the White Mountains and is the highest natural point in New Hampshire.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

New Hampshire has an extensive network of rivers and lakes. Major rivers include the Merrimack River, Connecticut River, Androscoggin River, Piscataqua River, and Contoocook River.

Important lakes include Lake Winnipesaukee, Squam Lake, and Lake Sunapee. These waterways are important for ecosystems, recreation, tourism, water resources, and communities throughout the state.

Economy of New Hampshire ▼ +

New Hampshire has a diverse economy with strong concentrations in advanced manufacturing, technology, healthcare, professional services, and tourism. Southern New Hampshire, particularly the communities along the Merrimack Valley and near the Massachusetts border, has developed important industries in electronics, precision manufacturing, computer technology, and business services. Healthcare and higher education are also major employers, while the state's proximity to Boston provides access to a large regional market.

Tourism is especially important in the White Mountains and Lakes Region, where skiing, hiking, camping, boating, and scenic travel support hotels, restaurants, outdoor recreation businesses, and seasonal employment. Forestry and wood products remain significant in the northern part of the state, while the Seacoast supports marine businesses, fishing, tourism, and port-related activities. Manufacturing, construction, retail, and professional services contribute to the economy across the state.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in New Hampshire include Manchester, Nashua, Concord, Derry, Dover, and Rochester.

Concord is the state capital. Manchester is the largest city by population and an important commercial and cultural center. Nashua is a major population and business center in southern New Hampshire, while Dover and Rochester are important communities in the eastern part of the state.

Natural Environment ▼ +

New Hampshire is heavily forested, with northern hardwood and coniferous forests covering much of the state. The White Mountains contain spruce-fir forests, alpine meadows, rocky summits, and cold mountain streams, with Mount Washington supporting some of the state's most distinctive high-elevation habitats. Central New Hampshire is characterized by lakes, wetlands, and forested shorelines, including the extensive freshwater environment around Lake Winnipesaukee.

New Hampshire's forests and mountains provide habitat for moose, black bears, white-tailed deer, beavers, bobcats, and many woodland birds. Rivers and lakes support trout, salmon, waterfowl, amphibians, and other freshwater species. Along the Seacoast, salt marshes, tidal rivers, estuaries, rocky shores, and beaches create coastal habitats for shorebirds, seals, shellfish, and marine life. Protected areas such as the White Mountain National Forest preserve important mountain and forest ecosystems.
