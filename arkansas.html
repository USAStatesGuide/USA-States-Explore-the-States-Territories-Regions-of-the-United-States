About Arkansas ▼ +

Arkansas is a state in the south-central region of the United States. It is bordered by Missouri to the north, Tennessee and Mississippi to the east, Louisiana and Texas to the south, and Oklahoma to the west. The capital and largest city of Arkansas is Little Rock.

Arkansas became the 25th state of the United States on June 15, 1836. The state is known for its mountains, forests, rivers, lakes, hot springs, agricultural lands, and diverse landscapes. The Ozark and Ouachita Mountains are important geographic features of the state.

Geography of Arkansas ▼ +

Arkansas has a strong contrast between its mountainous western and northwestern regions and the broad, low-lying plains of the east. The Ozark Mountains cover much of the northwestern part of the state, while the Ouachita Mountains extend across west-central Arkansas. Between these mountain regions lies the Arkansas River Valley, a broad landscape that separates the Ozarks from the Ouachitas and contains fertile valleys, forests, and river lowlands.

Eastern Arkansas is largely part of the Mississippi Alluvial Plain, a flat and fertile region shaped by the Mississippi River and its floodplain. One of the state's most distinctive landforms is Crowley's Ridge, a narrow elevated ridge rising above the surrounding plain in eastern Arkansas. The combination of mountains, river valleys, floodplains, and isolated uplands gives Arkansas a distinctive transition between the interior highlands and the Mississippi River lowlands.

Arkansas Borders ▼ +

Arkansas shares land borders with six states: Missouri to the north, Tennessee and Mississippi to the east, Louisiana and Texas to the south, and Oklahoma to the west.

The Mississippi River forms much of Arkansas's eastern boundary. Several other major rivers also flow through the state and help shape its landscape and ecosystems.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Arkansas is Mount Magazine, which reaches approximately 2,753 feet (839 meters) above sea level.

Mount Magazine is located in western Arkansas within the Ouachita Mountains. It is the highest natural point in the state and is part of Mount Magazine State Park.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Arkansas has an extensive network of rivers and waterways. Major rivers include the Mississippi River, Arkansas River, White River, St. Francis River, Ouachita River, and Red River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, agriculture, transportation, recreation, water resources, and communities. The Mississippi River is especially important because it forms much of the state's eastern boundary.

Economy of Arkansas ▼ +

Arkansas has an economy closely connected to agriculture, food production, transportation, manufacturing, forestry, and retail businesses. The fertile plains of eastern Arkansas make the state one of the leading producers of rice in the United States, while poultry, soybeans, cotton, cattle, and timber are also important agricultural products.

Food processing and forest products are major industries, supported by the state's farms and extensive woodlands. Arkansas is also known as the headquarters location of several large retail and transportation companies, particularly in the northwestern part of the state. Manufacturing, healthcare, tourism, and outdoor recreation contribute further to the economy, especially in regions known for lakes, rivers, mountains, and natural attractions.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Arkansas is divided into 75 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Arkansas include Little Rock, Fort Smith, Fayetteville, Springdale, Jonesboro, and Rogers.

Little Rock is the state capital and largest city by population. Fayetteville, Springdale, and Rogers are important population centers in northwestern Arkansas, while Fort Smith is a major city in the western part of the state and Jonesboro is an important center in northeastern Arkansas.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Arkansas is known for its extensive forests, clear streams, natural springs, caves, and mountain landscapes. The Ozark and Ouachita regions contain large areas of forest and rugged terrain, with limestone formations, underground caves, and spring-fed waterways. These environments provide habitats for species such as white-tailed deer, black bears, wild turkeys, and many species of fish and birds.

Eastern Arkansas has a very different environment, shaped by the Mississippi River and its floodplain. The region contains bottomland hardwood forests, wetlands, swamps, and seasonal floodplain habitats. Areas such as the Cache River National Wildlife Refuge protect important wetlands and forests for migratory birds and other wildlife. This contrast between the forested highlands and the riverine lowlands is a defining feature of Arkansas's natural environment.
