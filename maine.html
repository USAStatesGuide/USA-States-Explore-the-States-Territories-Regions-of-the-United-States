About Maine ▼ +

Maine is a state in the northeastern region of the United States and is the northernmost state in New England. It is bordered by New Hampshire to the west, the Canadian provinces of Quebec and New Brunswick to the north and northeast, and the Atlantic Ocean to the southeast. The capital of Maine is Augusta.

Maine became the 23rd state of the United States on March 15, 1820. The state is known for its extensive forests, mountains, lakes, rivers, rocky coastline, islands, and coastal communities. Much of northern and western Maine is characterized by forests and mountainous landscapes.

Geography of Maine ▼ +

Maine occupies the northeastern corner of the United States and is characterized by rugged mountains, vast forests, glacial lakes, and a deeply indented Atlantic coastline. The western and central parts include sections of the Appalachian Mountains, with Mount Katahdin rising to 5,269 feet (1,606 meters), the highest point in the state. North of the mountains lies the Maine North Woods, a vast forested region crossed by rivers, lakes, and remote wilderness.

Maine's coastline along the Gulf of Maine is one of its most distinctive geographic features, with numerous rocky peninsulas, bays, coves, tidal inlets, and offshore islands shaped by glaciers and the sea. The coast includes places such as Penobscot Bay and Casco Bay, while the Appalachian Trail reaches its northern terminus at Mount Katahdin. Northern and western Maine are generally more elevated and sparsely populated, contrasting with the more developed coastal and southern regions.

Maine Borders ▼ +

Maine shares a land border with New Hampshire to the west. It also shares international borders with the Canadian provinces of Quebec to the northwest and New Brunswick to the north and northeast.

Maine also has an extensive coastline along the Atlantic Ocean and the Gulf of Maine. Its coastal region contains numerous islands, bays, coves, harbors, and rocky shorelines.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Maine is Mount Katahdin, which reaches approximately 5,269 feet (1,606 meters) above sea level.

Mount Katahdin is located in northern Maine within Baxter State Park. It is the highest mountain in the state and forms the northern endpoint of the Appalachian Trail.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Maine has an extensive network of rivers, streams, lakes, and ponds. Major rivers include the Penobscot River, Kennebec River, Androscoggin River, St. John River, Saco River, Aroostook River, and Allagash River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, recreation, fisheries, forests, agriculture, transportation, hydroelectric power, and water resources. Maine also contains thousands of lakes and ponds, including Moosehead Lake, the largest lake in the state.

Economy of Maine ▼ +

Maine's economy has strong ties to its forests and Atlantic coastline. The state is especially known for its lobster industry, which supports commercial fishing, seafood processing, distribution, and coastal communities. Forestry remains important in northern and western Maine, where extensive forests supply lumber, paper, and other wood products. Agriculture also has a distinctive role, particularly through potatoes, wild blueberries, dairy farming, and other crops.

Maine also has a long tradition of boatbuilding and marine manufacturing, while aquaculture produces species such as farmed Atlantic salmon and shellfish. Tourism is a major part of the coastal economy, supported by rocky shores, islands, lighthouses, and destinations such as Acadia National Park. Outdoor recreation in Maine's mountains, forests, lakes, and rivers further contributes to tourism, alongside growing sectors such as healthcare, education, advanced manufacturing, and professional services.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Maine is divided into 16 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, towns, plantations, unorganized territories, and other local areas. Municipalities and counties provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Maine include Portland, Lewiston, Bangor, South Portland, Auburn, and Biddeford.

Augusta is the state capital. Portland is the largest city by population and an important economic, cultural, and transportation center. Bangor is an important regional center for northern and central Maine, while Lewiston and Auburn form a major urban area in the western part of the state.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Maine is one of the most heavily forested states in the United States, with vast stands of spruce, fir, pine, and hardwood trees covering much of the North Woods. The western and northern interior contains mountains, clear lakes, rivers, wetlands, and remote wilderness, while Mount Katahdin and the surrounding forests form one of the state's most distinctive natural landscapes. These habitats support moose, black bears, white-tailed deer, beavers, loons, and many other species.

Along the Gulf of Maine, the landscape changes to rocky headlands, tidal marshes, estuaries, islands, and cold marine waters. Acadia National Park protects a distinctive combination of coastal forests, granite mountains, rocky shores, and offshore islands. Maine's coastal waters support lobster, Atlantic puffins, seals, whales, and many seabirds, while its rivers and lakes provide important habitats for Atlantic salmon, trout, and other freshwater species.
