About New York ▼ +

New York is a state in the northeastern region of the United States. It is bordered by Canada to the north, Vermont, Massachusetts, Connecticut, and the Atlantic Ocean to the east, New Jersey and Pennsylvania to the south, and Pennsylvania, Lake Erie, and Canada to the west. The capital of New York is Albany.

New York became the 11th state of the United States on July 26, 1788. The state is known for New York City, the Adirondack and Catskill Mountains, the Great Lakes, the Finger Lakes, the Hudson River, and its important role in finance, commerce, education, culture, transportation, and industry.

Geography of New York ▼ +

New York has a distinctive geographic structure that stretches from the Atlantic coast to the Great Lakes and the Canadian border. The Adirondack Mountains occupy much of the northeastern part of the state and contain rugged peaks, forests, lakes, and remote wilderness. The Hudson Valley forms a major north-south corridor between the Adirondacks and the Catskill Mountains, while western New York includes broad plains and the Great Lakes shoreline.

The Finger Lakes are one of New York's most distinctive landscapes, consisting of long, narrow lakes formed by glacial activity. Lake Erie and Lake Ontario shape the western and northern borders and are connected by the Niagara River, which includes the famous Niagara Falls. In the southeast, the Hudson River reaches New York Harbor, while Long Island extends eastward into the Atlantic Ocean with beaches, bays, coastal wetlands, and barrier beaches. This combination of mountains, glacial lakes, major rivers, Great Lakes, and Atlantic coastline gives New York an unusually varied geography.

New York Borders ▼ +

New York shares land borders with several U.S. states and an international border with Canada. It borders Vermont, Massachusetts, and Connecticut to the east, New Jersey and Pennsylvania to the south, and Pennsylvania to the west.

New York also borders Canada along parts of Lake Ontario, the St. Lawrence River, and Lake Erie. The state has access to the Atlantic Ocean through Long Island and New York Harbor.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in New York is Mount Marcy, which reaches approximately 5,344 feet (1,629 meters) above sea level.

Mount Marcy is located in the Adirondack Mountains in northern New York. It is the highest mountain in the state and is surrounded by forests, lakes, valleys, and other peaks of the Adirondack High Peaks region.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

New York has an extensive network of rivers, lakes, canals, and other waterways. Major rivers include the Hudson River, Mohawk River, Genesee River, St. Lawrence River, Delaware River, and Niagara River.

The Hudson River flows south toward New York Harbor, while the Mohawk River is an important tributary of the Hudson. The Niagara River connects Lake Erie and Lake Ontario. Major lakes include Lake Ontario, Lake Erie, Lake George, Oneida Lake, and the Finger Lakes.

Economy of New York ▼ +

New York has one of the largest and most diversified state economies in the United States. New York City is a global center for finance, banking, investment, media, advertising, fashion, technology, and international business, with Wall Street forming one of the world's most important financial districts. The wider metropolitan area also has major concentrations of healthcare, biotechnology, professional services, and technology, while Albany and the Hudson Valley support government, research, education, and advanced industries.

Upstate New York has a more varied economic base that includes manufacturing, healthcare, higher education, agriculture, and tourism. The state's farms produce dairy products, apples, grapes, wine, corn, and other crops, with the Finger Lakes and Hudson Valley particularly known for vineyards and specialty agriculture. Tourism is important around Niagara Falls, the Adirondack Mountains, the Finger Lakes, and Lake George. Major transportation routes, including the Erie Canal system, railways, highways, and the Port of New York and New Jersey, also connect New York to national and international markets.

Administrative Division ▼ +

New York is divided into 62 counties. Counties are an important level of local government and administration within the state.

New York City consists of five boroughs that correspond to five counties: Manhattan is New York County, Brooklyn is Kings County, Queens is Queens County, The Bronx is Bronx County, and Staten Island is Richmond County.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in New York include New York City, Buffalo, Rochester, Yonkers, Syracuse, and Albany.

New York City is the largest city and a major global center for finance, culture, media, and commerce. Buffalo is an important city near Lake Erie, while Rochester and Syracuse are major educational, technological, and commercial centers. Albany is the state capital and an important governmental and educational center.

Natural Environment ▼ +

New York contains a wide range of ecosystems, from the extensive forests and mountain habitats of the Adirondacks and Catskills to the freshwater environments of the Great Lakes and Finger Lakes. The Adirondack region contains large areas of protected forest, rocky mountains, wetlands, and thousands of lakes and ponds, while the Hudson River Valley provides important river, wetland, and forest habitats. Northern and western New York also contain major freshwater ecosystems along Lake Ontario and Lake Erie.

New York's coastal environment is especially distinctive on Long Island and around New York Harbor, where beaches, barrier islands, tidal marshes, estuaries, and coastal bays support fish, shellfish, shorebirds, and other wildlife. Inland forests provide habitat for white-tailed deer, black bears, beavers, bobcats, and wild turkeys, while rivers and lakes support trout, bass, waterfowl, and other freshwater species. Protected areas such as Adirondack Park, Catskill Park, and numerous state parks preserve important examples of New York's forests, mountains, wetlands, and coastal habitats.
