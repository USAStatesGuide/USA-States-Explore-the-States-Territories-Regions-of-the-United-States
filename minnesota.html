About Minnesota ▼ +

Minnesota is a state in the Upper Midwest region of the United States. It is bordered by Canada to the north, Wisconsin and Lake Superior to the east, Iowa to the south, and South Dakota and North Dakota to the west. The capital of Minnesota is Saint Paul.

Minnesota became the 32nd state of the United States on May 11, 1858. The state is known for its numerous lakes, forests, rivers, wetlands, prairies, agricultural lands, and diverse landscapes extending from the Great Lakes region to the northern forests and western prairies.

Geography of Minnesota ▼ +

Minnesota is known for its exceptional abundance of lakes and waterways, earning it the nickname "Land of 10,000 Lakes." Northern Minnesota is dominated by boreal forests, rocky terrain, and thousands of lakes, with the Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness forming one of the state's most distinctive landscapes. Along the northeastern edge, the North Shore of Lake Superior features rugged cliffs, forests, waterfalls, and rocky beaches.

Central Minnesota contains forests, lakes, and rolling glacial terrain, while the south and west open into broad agricultural plains. The Red River Valley in the northwest is an exceptionally flat and fertile region, while the Minnesota River Valley cuts through the southern part of the state. The Mesabi Iron Range in northeastern Minnesota is another distinctive geographic feature, characterized by long ridges containing major iron ore deposits.

Minnesota Borders ▼ +

Minnesota shares land borders with North Dakota and South Dakota to the west, Iowa to the south, and Wisconsin to the east. It also borders Michigan across Lake Superior.

Minnesota shares an international border with Canada to the north, including the provinces of Manitoba and Ontario. The state also has a shoreline along Lake Superior.

Highest Point ▼ +

The highest point in Minnesota is Eagle Mountain, which reaches approximately 2,301 feet (701 meters) above sea level.

Eagle Mountain is located in Cook County in northeastern Minnesota, within the Superior National Forest. It is the highest natural point in the state.

Rivers and Waterways ▼ +

Minnesota has an extensive network of rivers, streams, lakes, and wetlands. Major rivers include the Mississippi River, Minnesota River, Red River of the North, St. Croix River, and Rainy River.

These waterways are important for ecosystems, drinking water, transportation, agriculture, recreation, fisheries, tourism, hydroelectric power, and communities throughout the state.

Economy of Minnesota ▼ +

Minnesota has a highly diversified economy, with major strengths in healthcare, medical technology, manufacturing, agriculture, food processing, finance, and retail. The Minneapolis-Saint Paul metropolitan area is the state's main business center and is home to major healthcare organizations, medical-device companies, financial institutions, technology firms, and corporate headquarters. Manufacturing includes machinery, medical equipment, electronics, food products, and other industrial goods.

Agriculture is especially important across southern and western Minnesota, where corn, soybeans, sugar beets, wheat, and other crops are grown, while livestock and dairy farming support related food industries. The Red River Valley is one of the state's most productive agricultural regions. Northern Minnesota has a long history of iron ore mining in the Mesabi Range, as well as forestry and wood products. The state's lakes, forests, and Lake Superior shoreline also support tourism, fishing, outdoor recreation, and related businesses.

Administrative Division ▼ +

Minnesota is divided into 87 counties. Counties are an important level of local government within the state.

The state also contains cities, townships, municipalities, unincorporated communities, and other local areas. Counties and municipalities provide various local governmental services to residents.

Major Cities ▼ +

Major cities and population centers in Minnesota include Minneapolis, Saint Paul, Rochester, Duluth, Bloomington, and Brooklyn Park.

Saint Paul is the state capital, while Minneapolis is the largest city by population. Together, Minneapolis and Saint Paul form the core of the state's largest metropolitan area. Rochester is an important healthcare and medical research center, while Duluth is a major port and transportation center on Lake Superior.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Minnesota's natural environment is strongly defined by its forests, wetlands, and extraordinary number of lakes. Northern Minnesota contains boreal and mixed forests surrounding thousands of lakes and waterways, with the Boundary Waters region providing extensive wilderness habitat. The North Shore of Lake Superior combines dense forests with rocky cliffs, waterfalls, and cold freshwater ecosystems. In the south and west, much of the original prairie has been converted to farmland, although native prairie remains in protected areas.

Minnesota's lakes and rivers provide important habitat for walleye, northern pike, trout, waterfowl, and other aquatic species, while wetlands serve as breeding and feeding areas for many birds and amphibians. Northern forests support moose, black bears, wolves, beavers, and white-tailed deer. The state also contains important river ecosystems along the Mississippi and Minnesota rivers, while Lake Superior supports cold-water fish and other species adapted to its deep, clear waters.
