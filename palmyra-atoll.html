About Palmyra Atoll ▼ +

Palmyra Atoll is a remote coral atoll in the central Pacific Ocean and an incorporated, unorganized territory of the United States. It is located approximately 1,000 miles southwest of Honolulu, Hawaii, near the southern end of the Hawaiian Archipelago.

The atoll consists of numerous small islands surrounding a shallow lagoon and extensive coral reefs. Palmyra is known for its lush tropical vegetation, abundant seabirds, rich marine biodiversity, and protected natural environment.

Geography of Palmyra Atoll ▼ +

Palmyra Atoll is a low-lying coral atoll consisting of numerous small islands, coral reefs, channels, and a large central lagoon. It is one of the southernmost atolls of the Hawaiian Archipelago.

Unlike many other Pacific atolls, Palmyra receives relatively high rainfall and supports dense tropical vegetation. Coconut palms, coastal forests, wetlands, mangroves, and other plants cover many of the islands.

Location and Surroundings ▼ +

Palmyra Atoll is located in the central Pacific Ocean, approximately 1,000 miles southwest of Honolulu. It lies between the Hawaiian Islands and the equatorial Pacific and is one of the most isolated protected areas under United States jurisdiction.

The atoll is surrounded by deep Pacific Ocean waters and extensive coral reef ecosystems. Its isolation and protected status have helped preserve a highly productive tropical marine environment.

Highest Point ▼ +

Palmyra Atoll is extremely low-lying. Its highest natural areas reach approximately 10 meters (33 feet) above sea level.

Most of the atoll consists of low coral islands, beaches, reef flats, lagoons, and wetlands. There are no mountains or significant elevated landforms.

Lagoon and Waterways ▼ +

Palmyra Atoll contains a large central lagoon connected to the surrounding Pacific Ocean by channels. The lagoon and surrounding reef provide sheltered habitats for fish, corals, sea turtles, seabirds, and many other species.

The atoll has numerous tidal channels and shallow marine waterways between its islands. These waterways are an important part of the atoll's tropical ecosystem.

History of Palmyra Atoll ▼ +

Palmyra Atoll was first sighted by Western explorers in the late eighteenth century. It was formally claimed by the United States in the nineteenth century and later became associated with the Kingdom and then the Territory of Hawaii.

During World War II, Palmyra was used by the United States military. Military construction significantly altered parts of the atoll, including the creation of an airfield and changes to some channels and islands.

After the military period ended, Palmyra gradually returned to a primarily conservation-oriented role. The atoll is now recognized as an important site for scientific research and protection of Pacific marine and terrestrial ecosystems.

Administrative Status ▼ +

Palmyra Atoll is an incorporated, unorganized territory of the United States. It is the only incorporated territory of the United States that is not organized under a territorial government.

The terrestrial portion of Palmyra Atoll is managed as the Palmyra Atoll National Wildlife Refuge by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. The surrounding marine environment is protected as part of the Pacific Islands Heritage Marine National Monument.

Major Settlements ▼ +

Palmyra Atoll has no permanent civilian cities or towns. There is no conventional urban population or permanent residential community on the atoll.

A small number of researchers, conservation workers, and authorized personnel may stay temporarily on the atoll for scientific, environmental, maintenance, and management activities.

Marine Environment ▼ +

Palmyra Atoll contains extensive coral reefs, shallow reef flats, channels, and lagoon habitats. Its marine environment supports a rich diversity of tropical Pacific species.

The surrounding waters provide habitat for numerous species of reef fish, sharks, rays, corals, sea turtles, and other marine organisms. The atoll is also an important nursery and feeding area for marine life.

Natural Environment ▼ +

Palmyra Atoll has an unusually lush natural environment for a Pacific atoll. Dense tropical forests, coconut palms, wetlands, mangrove areas, beaches, and coral reefs create a variety of habitats.

The atoll supports large populations of seabirds, including frigatebirds, boobies, terns, noddies, and other species. Green sea turtles and numerous marine species also use the surrounding waters and coastal habitats.

Palmyra is especially important because of its high marine biodiversity and its relatively remote location. Its coral reefs and tropical forests provide valuable habitat for wildlife throughout the central Pacific.

Conservation ▼ +

Palmyra Atoll National Wildlife Refuge protects the islands, coastal habitats, and wildlife of the atoll. The refuge is managed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

The surrounding marine environment is also protected as part of the Pacific Islands Heritage Marine National Monument. Conservation programs focus on protecting coral reefs, seabirds, sea turtles, native habitats, and other marine and terrestrial species.

Access to Palmyra Atoll is restricted. Visitors generally require authorization and must follow rules designed to protect the atoll's sensitive natural and historical resources.
